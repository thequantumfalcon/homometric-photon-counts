Can calibration rescue the optical bench?

Promising at the eight tested tighter-calibration settings. The correction had zero errors in 200 trials per structure at each setting. The simultaneous 95% upper limits for these 16 simulated error probabilities are 2.84%. The broader calibration allowance failed, reaching 55.0% observed error for A in one stress setting.

This is a calibration requirement to test, not physical validation. The tighter target is 0.25 µm residual position error per axis and 2 nm residual in the signed quadratic optical-path coefficient. Calibration measurements and an estimator were not simulated. Finite tested settings do not certify an entire continuous tolerance box.

What changed?

The same fixed amplitude mask, photon scale, score and threshold are retained. A phase offset derived from an estimated per-cell optical transfer is applied before phase quantization. The calibration estimate is label-independent and deliberately imperfect in the joint stress tests. Gain, background and phase variance vary simultaneously.

The initial 18-setting plan and the tighter eight-setting plan were frozen before their own independently seeded trials. The tighter budget was selected from development moments; no validation outcomes were used. All cases and failures are retained.

Held-out results, including failures

The uncorrected x-offset tests at −2.25 and +2.25 µm gave 47/200 and 42/200 A errors; at −2.5 and +2.5 µm they gave 165/200 and 166/200. The old nominal 2 µm test was less demanding. The mean crossing in the screen is not a 5% risk boundary.

The next physical gate

Measure the complex optical transfer of every cell and the actual phase/amplitude response. Test whether the three-parameter transfer model fits independent measurements and whether its calibration residuals meet the target. The assumed static amplitude mask, independent snapshots and detector model also need measurement.

A full physical error-rate test is substantial: even one setting and method would need 72 error-free trials per label for simultaneous one-sided 95% upper bounds below 5%. At 5,000 snapshots per trial and an assumed 10 Hz, that is 20 hours of acquisition, excluding calibration. Start with the transfer comparison; simulation speed is not laboratory feasibility.

Scope and remaining gaps

No real optical transfer, amplitude-mask implementation, phase response, afterpulsing, polarization, crosstalk or temporal drift has been validated. The experiment emulates programmed phase masks; it does not reconstruct unknown materials. No new Lean theorem, rigorous interval risk certificate, physical experiment or novelty claim is supplied. Development used AI assistance; no external peer review is claimed.
