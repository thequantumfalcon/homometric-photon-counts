Next laboratory gate: compare transfer before attempting performance claims
==========================================================================

This is an unexecuted protocol. No instrument, laboratory partner or measured
data are available in this checkpoint. Do not buy equipment based on a claimed
physical validation. The first decision is whether the assumed transfer and
calibration precision can be demonstrated with an available apparatus.

1. Record the actual optical layout, wavelength, polarization, pixel geometry,
   illumination, relay magnification, focusing lens and collection mode. Check
   that independent static amplitude compensation can be implemented; the
   simulator does not assume a phase-only SLM can freely control amplitude.

2. With a separate coherent reference, measure each of the 63 cells' complex
   collected amplitude using phase stepping. Repeat at centered collection and
   displaced/aberrated settings, and repeat across time. Retain unnormalized
   power, reference phase, dark frames, exposures and stage readings. Determine
   calibration precision from repeated data, not from nominal stage resolution.
   63 cells x four phase steps x ten repetitions is 2,520 frames; this is only
   a protocol-size illustration, NOT proof of sufficient precision or a required
   budget. Shot noise and reference stability determine the actual budget.

3. Fit the prescribed three-parameter optical response using calibration data
   only. Compare predicted and measured complex coefficients on separate check
   settings. Record residual spatial structure, not only fitted x/y/d errors.
   The tighter simulated target is a residual of at most 0.25 um per position
   axis and 2 nm in the signed quadratic OPD coefficient, for the tested finite
   settings. These are targets to assess, not demonstrated hardware tolerances.
   A general per-cell residual is not covered just because its RMS is small.
   Reject this model if unmodeled amplitude/phase residuals are material.

4. Measure phase versus command, amplitude coupling and settling. Confirm the
   full injected Gaussian phase law and temporal independence. Measure gain,
   background, dead time, afterpulsing and cross-gate memory. The tested corner
   values were gain 0.98/1.02, phase variance 0.049/0.051 rad^2 and background
   0.10/0.15 counts per gate. They are not a certificate for the entire box.
   Test whether 5 ms gates and an illustrative 10 Hz settled-snapshot rate
   actually give independent measurements. A frame-rate specification is not
   evidence of independence or an achieved acquisition rate.

5. Only after the transfer gate passes, freeze the measured calibration,
   correction rule, score, threshold, exclusions and sample budget. Randomize
   blinded mask order. Compare corrected and uncorrected data at centered and
   adverse settings, plus identical-mask controls. Keep all failed runs.
   A one-trial-per-label/method pilot at two settings uses 8 x 5,000 = 40,000
   snapshots, or 66 min 40 s at an ASSUMED 10 Hz, excluding setup/calibration.
   Two additional control trials add 16 min 40 s. This is only a model-comparison
   pilot; it cannot establish a low misclassification probability.

6. A confirmatory error-rate experiment is much larger. For one predeclared
   setting and method, zero errors in 72 independent trials per label gives
   Bonferroni one-sided 95% simultaneous upper bounds below 5% for the two
   error probabilities. That is 720,000 snapshots: 20 hours at 10 Hz before
   calibration and exclusions. If errors occur, the required evidence changes.
   Multiple settings require another multiplicity-aware design. The simulator's
   200 trials per label cost 55 h 33 min 20 s per setting/method at 10 Hz if
   repeated physically. Software execution speed is not laboratory feasibility.

Minimal retained record for every acquisition:
  run ID, UTC time, blinded mask ID, phase command array, correction array,
  calibration/check split, wavelength and polarization, stage settings,
  estimated x/y/OPD with uncertainty, measured per-cell complex transfer,
  actual amplitude transmissions, raw detector timestamps, gate boundaries,
  dark/reference readings, settling delays, exclusions with reasons.

Stop/revise if the optical-transfer model is rejected, calibration uncertainty
exceeds the tested targets, or snapshots are dependent. Add measured effects
to a new version of the simulator and preserve this checkpoint. A successful
programmed-mask emulator would still not prove unknown-material reconstruction,
the original fourth-order-only physical effect or historical novelty.
