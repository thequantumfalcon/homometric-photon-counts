CONTINUOUS PARAMETER-BOX ARGUMENT

Use the geometry, phase model and fixed score from ../design-search/METHOD.txt.
Let eta denote actual gain, b actual background, s the pre-quantization Gaussian
command variance, and t=exp(-s/2). The score is always

  Y(N)=(N)_2/63^2-(17/10)*N/63,

and threshold is always h=-3200551/5000000. The normalization is NOT eta-dependent.

1. Intensity polynomial family

In the raw mixed-moment recurrence, a site's characteristic coefficient has
factor t^((a-b)^2). Thus the kth intensity moment is a polynomial of degree at
most 2*k^2 in t. For a monomial term, sum_j a_j=sum_j b_j=k, so
sum_j(a_j-b_j)^2 <= sum_j a_j^2+sum_j b_j^2 <= 2*k^2.
The mixed recurrence retains every site and every required term.

For uniform L=256 phase quantization, multiply the principal coefficient by
sinc((a-b)*pi/L). Add a complex coefficient enclosure for the omitted Fourier
aliases, bounded as in the prior METHOD using the uniform lower variance
s_min=1/25. All assessed boxes have s>=s_min. The tail bound decreases with s,
so the bound at s_min applies throughout the domain.

At any s in the domain, every exact characteristic moment is represented by a
choice inside these coefficient enclosures. After the finite recurrence the
diagonal's real parts therefore give a ball-coefficient polynomial family
containing the actual intensity moment at that s. Alias coefficients may depend
on s: a single polynomial is NOT claimed to represent the full quantized moment
function for all s. Uniform enclosure at each point is all interval evaluation
requires. This distinction also applies when combining the moment families.

Write M_k(t)=E[(I/63)^k] for the enclosed family, k=0,...,5. For conditional
Poisson primaries with rate lambda=eta*I+b,

  R_k=E[lambda^k]
     =sum_{j=0}^k binom(k,j)*eta^j*b^(k-j)*63^j*M_j(t).

These are polynomial families in eta,b,t. From the count factorial identities,

  mu=R_2/63^2-beta*R_1/63,
  Q=R_4/63^4+(4/63^4-2*beta/63^3)*R_3
       +(2/63^4-4*beta/63^3+beta^2/63^2)*R_2+beta^2*R_1/63^2,
  V=Q-mu^2,  beta=17/10.

The polynomial operations enclose these expressions before parameter intervals
are substituted. In particular, gain effects do not renormalize the score and
the variance is not bounded by separately subtracting unrelated wide mean and
second-moment intervals.

2. Translation and enclosure

Expand each polynomial at eta0=1, b0=1/8, t0=exp(-1/40), using binomial expansion.
For a univariate polynomial sum_j a_j*t^j, the coefficient of (t-t0)^k is
sum_{j>=k} a_j*binom(j,k)*t0^(j-k). Apply the same finite translation in eta and b.
All coefficients, including t0, are evaluated with outward Arb enclosures.

For each box, enclose t=exp(-s/2), then evaluate the translated polynomials on
the intervals for eta-eta0, b-b0 and t-t0. Ordinary interval multiplication and
addition enclose every simultaneous parameter choice. Correlations among these
enclosures may make the result conservative, but cannot invalidate coverage.
Integer powers of centered intervals are calculated by repeated multiplication;
generic real powers are not used for intervals crossing zero.

3. Uniform dead-time correction

The earlier pathwise and Poisson inserted-pair argument supplies nonnegative
bounds epsilon1 on |E[Y']-E[Y]| and epsilon2 on |E[(Y')^2]-E[Y^2]| for accepted
counts under initially-live nonparalyzable dead time. Its pair factor is
d-d^2/2, monotone for 0<=d<=1. Substituting d_max=22/5000000 therefore covers
every dead time between zero and 22 ns for a 5 ms gate.

The required pair-weighted raw count moments, omitting this geometric factor,
are R_2, R_3+2R_2, R_4+5R_3+4R_2, and
R_5+9R_4+19R_3+8R_2. Insert these into the earlier score-change bounds, then
translate/evaluate these polynomials over the same parameter box.

If the unthinned score mean is mu and the thinned mean is mu+delta, then

  Var(Y') <= Var(Y)+epsilon2-2*mu*delta-delta^2
           <= Var(Y)+epsilon2+2*|mu|*epsilon1.

Use the box-wide upper variance bound, upper epsilon1/epsilon2 and upper |mu|
to obtain a uniform upper bound Vmax. Widen the mean interval by epsilon1.
This avoids counting parameter uncertainty as random extra observations and
does not assume that the unknown parameter values are re-estimated per sample.

4. Fixed-threshold risk and sample budget

For A let d_A=h-upper(mean_A); for B let d_B=lower(mean_B)-h. When d_G>0,
Cantelli's inequality for m iid snapshots gives

  P_G(error) <= Vmax_G/(Vmax_G+m*d_G^2).

All endpoints are exported as outward rational bounds; final risk fractions
are exact. Both errors are strictly below 1/20 whenever

  m > max_G 19*Vmax_G/d_G^2.

The recorded sufficient integer is floor(max_G 19*Vmax_G/d_G^2)+1.
For the 2% gain/variance box and b in [0.10,0.15] this gives 4,502. Keeping
5,000 snapshots yields the reported worst error below 4.53%. For the 4% box
and b in [0.075,0.175], it gives 10,769; 11,000 is a convenient rounded budget.
If a distance is nonpositive, the method supplies no such sufficient count.
This is failure to certify that box with these enclosures, not a lower bound
on the optimal sample count or proof that its distributions are indistinguishable.

5. Interpretation and trust

The guarantee is simultaneous over the entire box for any fixed unknown
parameters within it. It assumes the specified Gaussian command law, site and
snapshot independence, equal field amplitudes, exact encoded spatial frequency,
ideal uniform phase quantizer and the stated detector model. It does not prove
those assumptions from calibration data. Time-varying/dependent parameters,
afterpulsing and optical transfer errors require further arguments.

Arb arithmetic, source execution and input identity remain trusted computational
components. Point and corner tests support implementation review but do not
replace the continuum enclosure proof. This new argument is not Lean-formalized,
externally peer reviewed or physically validated.
