FINITE GUARANTEES WITH SPECIFIED TEMPORAL DEPENDENCE
21 October 2026 UTC

Summary and exact scope
=======================
This continuation closes a specified temporal-dependence problem. It does
not assume that correlated observations become independent after a finite
waiting period. It bounds their complete joint-law difference, or accounts
for their covariance directly.

The phase process is a stationary Gaussian AR(1) process, with independent
innovations across sites and times, and the count detector has independent
Poisson emissions conditional on the entire phase path. The marginal
single-snapshot model is exactly the already proved 63-site model.

Two complementary results follow.

1. Full-distribution testing: sampling every 63rd base time step when
   |a|<=1/2 preserves the previous rigorous interval

     112606153000000001 <= m_*(a,63) <= 112608396000000000

   for the optimal equal-prior average 95% decision at s=2 log 2,
   eta=1/512, b=0. The sufficient decision rule is the same fixed rational
   score already saved, with no knowledge of a required. The lower bound
   even allows the decision rule to know a. Both statements refer to the
   experiment that keeps only the regularly sampled counts. They are not
   lower bounds for an experiment that also uses all intervening counts.
   A second, simpler score rule uses all consecutive counts and guarantees
   at least 95% under EACH geometry after 2372403589637318817 counts when
   |a|<=1/2. Its bound is sufficient only, not claimed optimal.

2. Moment inference: all consecutive observations can be used. A valid
   variance bound for a single-count statistic is inflated by at most
   (1+a_max)/(1-a_max). At a_max=1/2 this is 3. Thus a fixed-sample
   Chebyshev calibration procedure, including the three-group fixed-guard
   procedure developed in the adjacent calibration folder, keeps the same
   confidence radii and error guarantee after each sample-group size is
   multiplied by 3. Independence between groups is not required.

These are mathematical guarantees under a specified dependence model.
No observed or simulated data are used. The AR coefficient upper bound is
a model input; an autocorrelation point estimate is not such a guarantee.
The sample budgets remain far too large to be described as practical.

1. Model and the 62-dimensional observable phase
===============================================
Let n=63 and let z_Gj be the preserved unit phasors for G=A,B. They satisfy
sum_j z_Gj=0. At integer base times t>=1 suppose

  theta_1 ~ N(0,s Id_n),
  theta_(t+1)=a theta_t+sqrt(s(1-a^2)) epsilon_(t+1),          (T1)

where s>0, |a|<1, and the epsilon vectors are independent standard
n-dimensional Gaussians, independent of theta_1. Thus

  Cov(theta_t,theta_u)=s a^|t-u| Id_n.

Set I_G(t)=|sum_j z_Gj exp(i theta_tj)|^2. Conditional on the entire
phase path, the counts are independent, with

  N_t | (theta_u)_u ~ Poisson(b+eta I_G(t)),
  eta>0, b>=0.                                              (T2)

The detector is time-local: its conditional rate uses theta_t only. No
additional detector memory or afterpulsing is included in this model.

Let P=Id_n-11^T/n. Intensity is unchanged when a scalar common phase is
added. Choose a matrix U with 62 orthonormal columns spanning 1-perp, so
UU^T=P. The standardized observable vector

  X_t=U^T theta_t/sqrt(s)

is a 62-dimensional stationary standard Gaussian AR(1) process with
coefficient a. Intensity depends only on X_t; the discarded common
coordinate cancels pathwise. This is why the dimension in all path-law
bounds below is 62, not 63. An arbitrary added common-phase process would
also cancel, even if it were dependent on the contrast process.

Sampling at times 1,1+ell,...,1+(m-1)ell gives another stationary AR(1)
sequence of contrast vectors with coefficient r=a^ell. Its single-time
law is unchanged. The symbol m always counts retained observations.

2. Exact path relative entropy and a full-count TV bound
=======================================================
Write P_(m,r) for the joint law of the m standardized contrast vectors,
and Q_m for m independent standard 62-dimensional Gaussians. With d=62,

  KL(P_(m,r) || Q_m) = -d(m-1) log(1-r^2)/2.                (T3)

All logarithms are natural. Here is a direct derivation. Under P, a new
vector given the previous vector x has law N(rx,(1-r^2)Id_d), whereas
under Q its conditional law is N(0,Id_d). Integrating the logarithm of
these two normal densities gives the conditional relative entropy

  [r^2 |x|^2 - d r^2 - d log(1-r^2)]/2.

The stationary expectation of |x|^2 is d, cancelling the first two
terms. The first vector has the same law under P and Q, and the log
likelihood of the remaining Markov path is the sum of m-1 conditional
log likelihoods. Taking expectations proves (T3). Equivalently the
m-by-m AR correlation matrix R has determinant (1-r^2)^(m-1).

Our total variation convention is TV(P,Q)=sup_E |P(E)-Q(E)|. Pinsker's
inequality in this convention is TV(P,Q)<=sqrt(KL(P||Q)/2). A short
proof is included to fix the constant. Aggregate both laws to a set E
attaining the positive variation and its complement. Jensen's inequality
for the convex function x log x shows that the original KL is at least
the Bernoulli divergence D(p||q), where p-q=TV(P,Q). As a function of p,
D(p||q) has value and first derivative zero at p=q, and second derivative
1/[p(1-p)]>=4. Therefore D(p||q)>=2(p-q)^2. The endpoint cases follow
by limits. This proves the stated form of Pinsker.

Applying the geometry-specific intensity and conditional Poisson Markov
kernel cannot increase TV. Indeed the difference of probabilities of an
output event is the integral of a function taking values in [0,1] against
P-Q, whose absolute value is at most TV(P,Q). Therefore for either G,

  TV(law_G(N_1,N_(1+ell),...,N_(1+(m-1)ell)), P_G^m)
    <= delta(m,a,ell)
    := min(1, (1/2)sqrt(d(m-1)[-log(1-a^(2ell))])).          (T4)

Here P_G is the FULL one-count marginal. No count truncation is introduced
in this step. The discarded-tail error in the previous iid certificate
has already been paid there.

For a specified bound |a|<=a_max<1, no logarithm evaluation is needed:

  delta^2 <= d(m-1) a_max^(2ell)
              /[4(1-a_max^(2ell))],                        (T5)

because -log(1-u)=integral_0^u (1-v)^(-1) dv<=u/(1-u).
Every numerical statement in the executable uses the rational right
side of (T5) and rigorously enclosed square roots.

A useful extension of the same proof is immediate. If the contrast path
is jointly Gaussian with covariance R_m tensor Id_d, where R_m is positive
definite and has diagonal entries 1, its relative entropy to independent
contrasts is -(d/2)log det R_m. Consequently (T4) is replaced by

  TV <= min(1, (1/2)sqrt(-d log det R_m)).                  (T6)

This extension requires a specified valid Gaussian correlation matrix.
The delivered numerical certificate uses AR(1), not an unspecified R_m.

3. Transfer of testing and confidence guarantees
================================================
For a decision rule f, its equal-prior success is

  S(f)=[Pr_A(f=A)+Pr_B(f=B)]/2.

The two changes of law in (T4) each change the appropriate event
probability by at most delta. Averaging gives

  |S_dependent(f)-S_iid(f)|<=delta.                        (T7)

This also holds for randomized rules, by conditioning on the auxiliary
random seed. Taking suprema over rules proves

  |S_dependent^*(m)-S_iid^*(m)|<=delta.                    (T8)

There is no assumption that the iid optimal rule is also the dependent
optimal rule. In particular, extra information in temporal correlations
does not invalidate the necessary bound: it is controlled at the level
of the complete joint data laws before optimizing any decision rule.

Similarly, any fixed-m confidence procedure having failure probability
at most alpha under iid sampling has failure probability at most
alpha+delta under this sampled dependent model. One must budget this
loss; a statement of iid 95% by itself does not remain exactly 95%.

At the preserved detector point s=2 log 2, eta=1/512, b=0, the earlier
exact certificate supplies

  m0=112606153000000000,
  S_iid^*(m0)<0.94999999953644771487686387151626;

  m1=112608396000000000,
  S_iid(f_score,m1)>0.95000000025017165383294509403600.

The exact rational endpoints, not these shortened decimals, are used.
For |a|<=1/2 and ell=63, (T5) gives

  delta(m1)<1.432390752195073e-10,
  S_dependent(f_score,m1)>0.9500000001069325786134.

At m0, adding its corresponding delta to the iid optimal-success upper
bound is still strictly below 19/20. A decision rule for more observations
can always ignore later observations; the sampled process with fixed ell
has consistent prefixes. Hence optimal success is nondecreasing in m,
even with dependence. For every fixed |a|<=1/2 this proves

  m0+1 <= m_*(a,63) <= m1.                                 (T9)

The necessary half permits a to be known to the decision rule. The
sufficient rational score does not use a and works uniformly over its
stated range. These are equal-prior AVERAGE success statements; separate
95% success under each geometry is not claimed.

For m1 retained observations at ell=63, the inclusive base-grid span is

  63(m1-1)+1 = 7094328947999999938 slots.

This is an elapsed-grid span, not a requirement to record all intervening
counts. It has no physical time unit until the experiment specifies the
base sampling interval. Using intervening observations could improve
performance; (T9) makes no claim about that different experiment.

The exact program also certifies these sufficient spacings:

  known a_max       1/4    1/2    3/4    9/10    99/100
  ell                32     63    150     410      4290

Each is the smallest integer satisfying THIS two-endpoint transfer test
with the preserved margins. It is not claimed to be the minimum spacing
with which some other analysis or test could work.

4. Using every observation for finite-variance inference
=======================================================
Let Z_t=h(N_t) be any real statistic with finite second moment, and put
g(X_t)=E[Z_t|X_t]. Conditional detector independence gives, for k>=1,

  Cov(Z_t,Z_(t+k))=Cov(g(X_t),g(X_(t+k))).                  (T10)

For standardized Gaussian vectors with cross-covariance a^k Id_d, the
Hermite expansion proves

  |Cov(g(X_t),g(X_(t+k)))| <= |a|^k Var(g(X_t)).            (T11)

For completeness, use probabilists' multivariate Hermite polynomials
H_alpha and their generating function exp(u dot x-|u|^2/2).
The joint Gaussian exponential moment is exp(a^k u dot v). Matching
coefficients gives

  E[H_alpha(X_t)H_beta(X_(t+k))]
    =1_(alpha=beta) alpha! a^(k|alpha|).

Hermite polynomials form a complete orthogonal basis in Gaussian L2.
Expand g-Eg=sum_(|alpha|>=1) c_alpha H_alpha. Orthogonality, followed by
L2 convergence, yields

  Cov(g(X_t),g(X_(t+k)))
    =sum_(|alpha|>=1) c_alpha^2 alpha! a^(k|alpha|).

Taking absolute values and using |a|^(k|alpha|)<=|a|^k gives (T11).
This is the elementary finite-dimensional form of the established
Gaussian maximal-correlation inequality; it is not a new general
probability inequality. The generating-function calculation and complete
expansion identify exactly which version is used here.

Let a_max be any known upper bound on |a|. Expanding the variance of the
sample mean and summing the geometric covariance bound gives

  Var(m^(-1)sum_(t=1)^m Z_t)
    <= [E Var(Z_1|X_1)+kappa Var(g(X_1))]/m
    <= kappa Var(Z_1)/m,

  kappa=(1+a_max)/(1-a_max).                               (T12)

The finite-m multiplier before enlarging to kappa is

  1+2 sum_(k=1)^(m-1) (1-k/m)a_max^k <= kappa.

This holds for negative a as well, by the absolute covariance bound.
For a=0 it reduces to the independent variance calculation.

Thus, if V_r is any deterministic valid upper bound on Var(h_r(N)),
Chebyshev and a union bound give simultaneous fixed-m intervals

  |bar h_r-E h_r| <= sqrt(kappa V_r/(m alpha_r)),
  all r, with probability at least 1-sum_r alpha_r.        (T13)

The statistics within a count, or distinct sample groups, need not be
independent. Each marginal tail bound is unconditional, so the union
bound remains valid. This fact matters for preliminary gates followed by
main-data calibration: it would be incorrect to invoke a pilot-dependent
conditional iid law without justification, but no such step is needed
for the fixed-guard scheme in the adjacent calibration proof.

At a_max=1/2, kappa=3. Replacing an iid group's size m_iid by 3 m_iid and
using inflation 3 in its radii makes every radius exactly the same as
before. This applies separately to the preliminary mean, coarse calibration,
and final inference groups, including consecutive disjoint groups from the
same stationary process. The adjacent finite certificate uses iid group
sizes [10^10,10^18,10^20], which become [3*10^10,3*10^18,3*10^20].
Their total is 303000000030000000000 observations. Its fixed guards and
unconditional failure-budget argument still apply, so the same reference
point receives at least 95% joint calibration and identification success.
The model-uniform variance bounds on the relevant fixed parameter branch
must still be justified; (T12) supplies dependence control, not a new
proof of any chosen bound V_r.

5. A further refinement for the ordinary photon mean
====================================================
At the exact common null, the centered intensity has no linear Hermite
component. To see this without assuming that I(theta) is an even
function, give the phase vector a small mean mu. Direct expansion gives

  E I(theta+mu)
    = n(1-exp(-s))+exp(-s)|sum_j z_j exp(i mu_j)|^2.

Since sum_j z_j=0, every derivative with respect to mu_j vanishes at
mu=0. Gaussian integration by parts, valid for this smooth bounded
intensity with bounded derivatives, gives

  E[theta_j(I-EI)]=s E[partial_j I]=0.

Thus the first nonzero Hermite degree, if any, is at least 2, also after
projection to the contrast coordinates. Applying the preceding expansion
with powers |a|^(2k) gives

  Var(mean N) <= [E Lambda+kappa_2 Var Lambda]/m,
  kappa_2=(1+a_max^2)/(1-a_max^2),
  Lambda=b+eta I.                                          (T14)

At a_max=1/2, kappa_2=5/3. The Poisson noise contributes E Lambda/m
without a temporal inflation because emissions are conditionally
independent. Higher factorial statistics need not have Hermite rank 2;
the generic kappa bound is used unless their lower Hermite coefficients
are separately proved to vanish. Geometry errors can also remove the
exact-null rank cancellation. The delivered calibration transfer uses
the safe common factor 3 and does not depend on (T14).

6. A concrete score test using every consecutive observation
==========================================================
The total-variation transfer in Sections 2-3 leaves many base-time
observations unused. A separate fixed-sample analysis can use all of them.
It obtains a different sufficient bound, without applying an iid normal
approximation to the dependent score sum.

Use the preserved rational weights w_0,...,w_24 from
science_frontier/tighter_testing/tighter_testing_certificate.json and
define the bounded statistic

  Z_t=w_(N_t) if N_t<=24, and Z_t=0 if N_t>24.

This is an explicit score on the FULL count space. A count above 24
contributes zero; there is no conditioning on a no-tail event and no
unpaid m-fold tail approximation. For each G the pinned input encloses
the conditional probabilities R_G(k)=P_G(k)/(1-tau_G), with
0<=tau_G<=T_G. Hence

  (1-T_G) R_G^-(k) <= P_G(k) <= R_G^+(k),  0<=k<=24.

Multiplying each interval by its fixed signed rational weight, then
summing, encloses mu_G=E_G Z. Summing upper probability bounds times
w_k^2 encloses E_G Z^2. Subtracting the minimum of mu^2 over the mean
interval gives a rigorous variance upper bound V_G. The zero tail score
makes these formulas exact full-law moment identities.

Let mu_G^- and mu_G^+ be the enclosed mean endpoints, and set

  theta=(mu_A^-+mu_B^+)/2,
  d0=(mu_A^--mu_B^+)/2>0.

The true mean drift is at least d0 on each side of theta. With
kappa=(1+a_max)/(1-a_max), (T12) gives
Var(sum Z_t)<=m kappa V_G. Cantelli's one-sided variance inequality now
bounds the error of the rule "choose A iff sum Z_t>=m theta" by

  error_G <= kappa V_G/(kappa V_G+m d0^2).                 (T15)

For clarity, Cantelli follows by applying Markov's inequality to
(Y+c)^2 on {Y>=d} for a centered Y, then minimizing
(Var Y+c^2)/(d+c)^2 over c>=0; the minimizer is c=Var Y/d.
Apply the same result to -Y for the other tail. No independence is used
in that step. The preceding variance bound is where temporal dependence
has already been accounted for.

At s=2 log 2, eta=1/512, b=0, the exact score intervals give approximately

  mu_A in [4.805279447473817e-17,4.805279447538469e-17],
  mu_B in [-4.805266336319135e-17,-4.805266336255248e-17],
  V_A<9.610585117708734e-17,
  V_B<9.610506450407392e-17.

The executable retains the full rational endpoints. With a_max=1/2,
kappa=3, choosing

  m=max_G ceil(19 kappa V_G/d0^2)
   =2372403589637318817                                    (T16)

makes each error bound at most 1/20. This proves separate 95% success
under A and B, and hence also 95% equal-prior average success. The rule
does not use the true a. It needs the stated uniform bound |a|<=1/2.

This design records approximately 21.07 times as many counts as the
63-spaced score design, but its elapsed base-grid span is approximately
one third as long, because it uses consecutive times. Neither design is
proved optimal for elapsed time, retained-count number, or data-storage
cost. This comparison does not alter the iid optimal-sample bracket or
the bracket for the specific thinned experiment in (T9).

7. Why an unrestricted temporal-dependence claim would be false
==============================================================
Keep the correct independent Gaussian SITE distribution at each time,
but freeze the entire phase vector: theta_t=theta_1 for all t. This is
a stationary temporal process with the correct one-snapshot phase law.
With independent conditional Poisson emissions, the infinite count
record reveals at most the one latent intensity I_G(theta_1). In
particular, for every m, data processing gives

  S_m^* <= [1+TV(law(I_A),law(I_B))]/2.                     (T17)

The two intensity laws have overlap. In fact the wrapped Gaussian
density is strictly positive on the phase torus. Absorbing the fixed
phasor arguments into its phase coordinates gives two mutually
absolutely continuous measures on the same torus and the common map
phi -> |sum_j exp(i phi_j)|^2. Their pushforwards are mutually absolutely
continuous, so their TV is strictly less than 1. Thus the upper bound in
(T17) is strictly less than 1: repeated counts cannot consistently learn
the structural distribution from one frozen intensity realization.

This does not by itself give a numerical 95% obstruction at the saved
reference point, and no such number is asserted here. The previously
proved small-s limit TV(law(I_A),law(I_B))->0 does show that some allowed
interior phase widths cannot achieve 95% under frozen motion at ANY m.
Consequently marginal assumptions without temporal control cannot
support a uniform 95% finite-sample statement across that domain.

Even restricting to |a|<1 without a specified bound away from 1 does not
give a uniform arbitrarily-high-confidence rate: for any fixed finite m,
the AR path tends to the frozen path as a increases to 1. Each finite
count-vector probability then converges by bounded continuity of the
Poisson mixture kernel, and normalized countable probability masses
converge in total variation. The frozen obstruction is approached by
valid AR(1) models. A quantitative a_max<1 is substantive information.

8. Reproducibility, prior work, and remaining scope
=================================================
Run

  python3 science_advance/dependence/certify_temporal_dependence.py

The executable pins the complete prior iid endpoint certificate by
SHA-256, performs all spacing and success inequalities with exact
rational arithmetic, and encloses square roots using integer arithmetic.
It also checks small exact AR correlation determinants and Hermite
covariance identities. Those finite checks supplement, rather than
replace, the proofs for arbitrary m and Hermite degree above.

The calculation does not rerun the old count-probability or moment
engines. Their correctness and declared independent-review scope remain
the retained input dependency. The new contribution is the explicit
temporal transfer and its application to the saved scientific targets.

Relevant primary sources, read for attribution and normalization:

  Ivan Nourdin, Giovanni Peccati, Xiaochuan Yang (2019),
  "Berry-Esseen bounds in the Breuer-Major CLT and Gebelein's inequality,"
  Electronic Communications in Probability 24, paper 34.
  DOI 10.1214/19-ECP241. Proposition 5.1 and its Hermite proof.
  https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/24526/1/FullText.pdf

  Mark D. Reid and Robert C. Williamson (2009),
  "Generalised Pinsker Inequalities," arXiv:0906.1244.
  Equations (6)-(7) define their variational divergence as twice our TV
  and state KL>=V^2/2, hence KL>=2 TV^2 in our convention.
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.1244

These principles predate this project. The proof does not claim a novel
Pinsker or Gaussian maximal-correlation inequality. It supplies explicit
new consequences for the two fixed structures and retained certificates.

Unknown or nonspecified temporal dependence, nonstationary initialization,
time-varying phase law, detector memory, optional stopping, actual estimates
of a with coverage, and physical validation remain separate questions.
The total-variation transfer method does not license the iid Berry-Esseen
bound on all consecutive correlated observations. No model assumption is
claimed experimentally verified by this mathematical continuation.
