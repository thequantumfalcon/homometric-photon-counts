INDEPENDENT REVIEW OF THE SPECIFIED TEMPORAL-DEPENDENCE EXTENSION
21 October 2026

Disposition: PASS within the explicitly stated stationary Gaussian AR(1)
phase and conditionally independent Poisson detector model. No mathematical
defect was found in the proof or its reported numerical applications.
This is a separate computational/mathematical review in the present work,
not external human peer review and not an experimental validation.

Analytic review
---------------
1. Observable dimension and path comparison.
The common-phase cancellation is pathwise. Projecting the 63-dimensional
Gaussian phase to the orthogonal complement of the all-ones vector leaves
62 standard Gaussian contrasts after scaling, with exactly the same AR
coefficient. The intensity and conditional count kernel factor through
these contrasts, justifying dimension 62 rather than 63 in the path KL.
The conditional Gaussian relative entropy integrates to
-62 log(1-r^2)/2 per transition. The trace and mean terms cancel under
stationarity; the initial law is the same. Pinsker under TV=sup event
probability difference gives the factor 1/2 in the displayed square root.
The rational replacement -log(1-u)<=u/(1-u) has the correct direction.

2. Transfer of testing bounds.
Each hypothesis's dependent joint count law is within delta of its iid
joint law. Averaging two success probabilities costs delta, not 2delta.
Taking suprema over rules preserves this bound, so the lower endpoint
properly covers rules that use temporal correlation and know a. Prefix
consistency proves monotonic optimal success in the retained sample count.
The result is confined to the regular-spacing experiment; it is not a
lower bound for observing all intervening counts. The proof distinguishes
equal-prior average 95% success from separate per-hypothesis success.

3. Covariance and confidence transfer.
Conditional detector independence removes all off-diagonal emission noise.
The multivariate Hermite expansion gives the covariance series with terms
a^(k|alpha|), including signs when a<0. Absolute values give the claimed
maximal-correlation bound, and summing the geometric series gives
kappa=(1+a_max)/(1-a_max). Total variance yields the stated refinement with
uninflated conditional detector variance. These facts are sufficient for
unconditional Chebyshev bounds and union bounds over prespecified groups;
no conditional independence after a data-dependent pilot guard is invoked.
The adjacent three-group calibration design uses fixed guards and thus
fits this unconditional argument. Tripling every group at a_max=1/2 is
an exact variance/radius transfer, not an iid approximation.

4. Hermite-rank refinement for the raw mean.
Differentiating the exact iid-site intensity expectation under a phase mean
shift produces zero gradient at the common null. Gaussian integration by
parts therefore kills the first Hermite degree, also in the contrast
coordinates. The rank-2 factor (1+a_max^2)/(1-a_max^2) is valid for I and
the conditional mean of N. The proof correctly avoids extending this
rank claim automatically to higher factorial statistics or geometry errors.

5. All-consecutive score and Cantelli bound.
Defining the score to be zero above count 24 gives a well-defined statistic
on the complete count space. The old conditional probability intervals
are converted to unconditioned probability bounds by the paid one-count
tail bound. Signed weights are handled by endpoint ordering; a valid
variance upper bound subtracts a lower bound on the squared mean.
The common threshold and positive drift use the worst mean endpoints.
Cantelli with variance at most m*kappa*V gives the stated denominator
kappa*V+m*d0^2. The tie convention is harmless: A's error is a strict
lower tail, B's is a weak upper tail, both covered by the weak inequality.
The ceiling of 19*kappa*V/d0^2 therefore guarantees separate 95% success
under both structures. This is a sufficient bound for the fixed score,
not an optimal sample count for dependent data.

6. Frozen-motion obstruction.
With the phase frozen, the entire count record is a Markov-kernel output
of one latent intensity, so data processing limits its structural success
by the one-intensity total variation. Strictly positive wrapped Gaussian
phase densities remain mutually absolutely continuous after absorbing
the fixed clean phases. The common intensity map preserves mutual absolute
continuity; two equivalent probability measures cannot have TV equal to
one. Thus consistency from indefinitely repeated observations of a frozen
single realization is impossible. This alone does not prove success is
below 95% at the saved reference point, and the note correctly says so.
The retained small-width TV limit supplies widths where even 95% cannot
be reached. For a fixed finite record, a->1 makes the Gaussian path
converge to the frozen path; bounded continuous count-kernel probabilities
converge pointwise. Countable probability mass convergence then gives TV
convergence. This supports the stated absence of a uniform rate without
a known bound away from one.

Material limits retained
------------------------
The review does not rebuild the old iid count-distribution intervals from
the geometry. That remains an explicit retained input with its earlier
verification record. The AR model, stationarity, common coefficient bound,
time-local Poisson detector, and absence of detector memory are assumptions,
not experimentally demonstrated properties. An estimated autocorrelation
or shuffled data cannot substitute for those assumptions. No statement of
research priority, human peer review, physical time feasibility, or an
exact globally optimal dependent sample integer is made by this review.
