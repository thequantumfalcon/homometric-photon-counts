HIGHER-GAIN FINITE COUNT TEST WITH EXACT RATIONAL SCORES
21 October 2026

Result and limits
=================
At the same Gaussian phase width s=2 log(2), or t=1/2, but at zero-background
Poisson gain eta=1/28 instead of eta=1/512, an explicit test using four count
bins has error strictly below 1/20 under EACH of the two structures with

  m = 303181164969742 independent snapshots.

The four bins are N=0, N=1, N=2, and N>=3. No observation is discarded and
no unbounded-count tail is dropped. The test is an exact rational weighted
sum of the four bin multiplicities. Its weights and all bounds are saved
in detector_gain_certificate.json. This is a known-calibration result.

The full one-snapshot count laws obey

  TV(P_A,P_B) >= 1.3618598454351179908e-7,
  h^2(P_A,P_B) >= 1.9817192635699361486e-14,

where h^2=sum_k(sqrt(p_k)-sqrt(q_k))^2. The exact lower rational endpoints
are in the certificate; the shortened values above are rounded DOWN.
The optimal equal-prior likelihood-ratio test for these four bins has
average success at least95% with 232383066797468 snapshots, by affinity.
The larger303181164969742 bound supplies a fixed rational score and the
stronger separate error guarantee under each structure.

The preceding fixed-gain necessary bound at eta=1/512 was
112606153000000001. The new explicit sufficient count is over371 times
smaller. This comparison changes the detector gain; it does NOT improve
the enclosure of the exact optimal integer at the OLD operating point.
The absolute number remains extremely large and is not called practical.

Eta=1/28 is a convenient certified operating point. Five rational gains
were considered, and it gave the smallest proved budget with this chosen
interpolation and score construction. It is NOT a global detector optimum.
The already proved strict gain ordering means that the actual full-law
separation increases further at every larger finite gain. A weakening
numerical lower bound at larger gain reflects the certificate's looseness.
The best phase width, full latent maximum, and physical detector capability
are not determined by this calculation.

2. One-sided interpolation bounds for the count probabilities
===========================================================
For k=0,1,2, define the positive measure dnu_G,k(u)=u^k dP_G(u).
Then p_G,k=eta^k/k! times integral exp(-eta u)dnu_G,k(u).

Given n distinct positive rational nodes x_j, let H match f(u)=exp(-eta u)
and its first derivative at all nodes, with degree at most2n-1. The usual
Hermite remainder, obtained by repeated Rolle's theorem, is

 f(u)-H(u)=f^(2n)(xi) product_j(u-x_j)^2/(2n)! >=0.

If J additionally matches f at the simple node0, its degree is at most2n
and

 f(u)-J(u)=f^(2n+1)(xi) u product_j(u-x_j)^2/(2n+1)! <=0

for every u>=0. Thus integrating H and J against nu bounds its Laplace
transform from below and above. No optimal quadrature theorem is needed.

The nodes in interpolation_nodes.json are rounded numerical design choices.
The final certificate uses only their exact rational values, checks that
they are distinct and positive, and verifies the defining interpolation
conditions exactly. Its validity does not depend on how the nodes were
suggested or on their being close to orthogonal-polynomial zeros.

Every expectation of a basis polynomial is a rational linear combination
of the pinned moments. The derivative weights remain in the calculation:
one does not drop them as though the nodes were exact quadrature roots.
The resulting expressions are sums of(a_j-eta b_j)exp(-eta x_j).
All required moment indices are checked to be at most32.

Exponentials are enclosed by rational arithmetic. Repeated halving reduces
z>=0 to z<=1. The even Taylor partial sum through90 bounds exp(-z) from
above, and the odd sum through91 bounds it from below. Square the positive
interval to undo each halving. Rounding is outward on grid10^-80. No
floating-point exponential or quadrature value establishes a bound.

3. Four bins retain the entire count sample
==========================================
The first three probability intervals enclose P(N=0),P(N=1),P(N=2).
The last bin has probability exactly

 p_G,3plus=1-p_G,0-p_G,1-p_G,2.

Its interval is computed by this normalization, not by a factorial tail
bound. All four true probabilities sum exactly to1; their interval boxes
need not form a Cartesian product of probability distributions.

For any component with p in[a,b] and q in[c,d], put
delta=max(0,a-d,c-b). Since

 (sqrt(p)-sqrt(q))^2=(p-q)^2/(p+q+2sqrt(pq)),

a valid lower bound is delta^2/(b+d+2sqrt(bd)), with the denominator rounded
UP. A square-root upper bound is obtained by an integer square root with
a rational outward increment. Summing these four lower bounds gives a
lower bound on the four-bin squared Hellinger distance. Coarsening is a
common Markov map, so this is also a lower bound on the full count distance.
The analogous summed absolute-gap lower bound yields full-count TV.

4. A finite optimal-test bound without computing enormous products
=================================================================
For the exact four-bin laws write rho=sum_j sqrt(p_Aj p_Bj)=1-h^2/2.
For m independent observations, their affinity is rho^m. Their optimal
equal-prior average error satisfies

 error_*=(1/2)sum_data min(P_A^m(data),P_B^m(data))
          <=rho^m/2 <= exp(-m h^2/2)/2.

The rational number2.30259 exceeds log(10), established by a finite exact
positive Taylor lower bound showing exp(2.30259)>10. Substituting the
certified Hellinger lower bound and rounding up therefore gives
232383066797468 as a sufficient m for average success at least19/20.
This is a sufficient bound, not an assertion that this integer is optimal.

5. Explicit rational score and separate95% guarantees
=====================================================
An implementable test need not know the exact count probabilities. The
producer chooses rational score weights w_j close to half-log likelihood
ratios of the interval midpoints. The midpoint tail is defined by exact
normalization. Rational square-root and short logarithm-series calculations
produce the choices, rounded to the fixed grid10^-50. Their proximity to
the ideal likelihood ratio motivates them but is not an assumption in the
proof: the resulting four w_j are fixed rational numbers, verified below.

For bin multiplicities c_j with sum_j c_j=m, choose

 A if sum_j c_j w_j>=0; otherwise B.

This comparison uses four integer multiplicities and rational arithmetic.
It does not require materializing a product of m rational factors, and has
an exact tie convention. The same weights work for every m at least the
stated sufficient budget.

Let a_j be rigorous rational upper bounds on exp(-w_j), and b_j upper
bounds on exp(w_j), obtained by the same exponential enclosure and positive
reciprocals. Then E_A exp(-w(N))<=sum_j p_Aj a_j and
E_B exp(w(N))<=sum_j p_Bj b_j.

Exact normalization is essential to keep these expectations sharp. For
any four fixed numbers v_j,

 sum_j p_j v_j=v_3+sum_(j=0)^2 p_j(v_j-v_3).

Use the upper probability endpoint when(v_j-v_3)>=0 and the lower endpoint
when it is negative. This gives an upper bound for every true probability
vector in the recorded intervals, without pretending its entries vary
independently while retaining normalization. It also avoids paying an
unnecessary first-order sum of interval widths near the common value1.

The certificate establishes, for both geometries,

 E_A exp(-w(N))<=1-d,
 E_B exp(w(N))<=1-d,
 d>=9.8950737929231446303e-15>0.

The exact saved d is rounded downward. Independence multiplies the moment
generating functions. Markov's inequality gives

 Pr_A(sum w<0)<=(1-d)^m<=exp(-md),
 Pr_B(sum w>=0)<=(1-d)^m<=exp(-md).

The selected m is ceil(3/d). An exact positive Taylor partial sum proves
exp(3)>20, so exp(-3)<1/20. Both conditional errors are therefore strictly
below1/20. No central limit or asymptotic approximation is used.
