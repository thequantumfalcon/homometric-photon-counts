FOUR-COUNT BLIND GAINS: A GLOBAL LOWER BOUND AND A SECOND CERTIFIED ROOT
2026-10-21

Status
------
A new elementary theorem proves that there are at least four positive,
sign-changing four-count blind gains at EVERY finite positive Gaussian
phase variance. This is stronger than merely establishing a first blind
gain. At t=exp(-s/2)=1/2, a new exact rational certificate establishes
EXACTLY TWO zeros on (0,1/32], both simple. At least two more positive
blind gains therefore lie above 1/32.

The exact global number is still unknown. The previously proved global
finiteness result is retained, but is not needed for the new lower bound.
Nothing in this document is an observed physical measurement or a claim
of independent external mathematical review.

1. Model and previously verified inputs
--------------------------------------
The two existing 63-site structures have intensity laws P_A,P_B on [0,3969].
At fixed 0<s<infinity, site phases are iid centered Gaussian with variance s.
Intensity is a nonconstant real analytic phase function and its input has
a smooth positive density. Its law is absolutely continuous; in particular
P_G(I=0)=0. The first three raw intensity moments agree. The fourth gap is

 D4(s) = (455015761/20000) t^8 (1-t^2)^5
         * (t^6+3t^4+6t^2+6) > 0,  t=exp(-s/2).

Define, with zero background,

 F_k(eta)=E_A[I^k exp(-eta I)]-E_B[I^k exp(-eta I)],
 P_A(N=k)-P_B(N=k)=eta^k F_k(eta)/k!,  eta>0.

Thus zeros of F_4 are precisely four-count blind gains. The full count
laws remain different at every eta>0, since their fourth factorial moments
differ by eta^4 D4(s). A blind single count channel does not mean that the
whole detector has lost structure information.

2. A global orthogonality identity
---------------------------------
For j=0,1,2,3 and I>0, a change of variable u=eta I gives

 integral_0^infinity eta^j I^4 exp(-eta I) d eta
       = j! I^(3-j).

All four integrals are finite after expectation: for j<3 the right-hand
side is bounded because I<=3969; for j=3 it is 3!=6 almost surely. Tonelli's
theorem applies separately to each nonnegative model integral. Subtracting,

 integral_0^infinity eta^j F_4(eta) d eta
       = j! [E_A I^(3-j)-E_B I^(3-j)] = 0.             (1)

Moreover integral eta^j |F_4| is bounded by the sum of the two model
integrals, so each signed integral is absolutely convergent. The j=3 case
uses the absence of an atom at zero (or, more generally, equal such atoms).

F_4 is nonzero: F_4(0)=D4(s)>0. Bounded intensity makes F_4 entire, hence
real analytic on the positive axis. Its zeros are isolated there unless
it is identically zero, which has just been excluded.

3. Orthogonality forces at least four sign changes
------------------------------------------------
LEMMA. Let f be a nonzero real analytic function on (0,infinity). Suppose
integral_0^infinity x^j |f(x)| dx < infinity and
integral_0^infinity x^j f(x) dx = 0 for j=0,...,r-1.
Then f has at least r distinct positive sign-changing zeros.

PROOF. If there are already at least r sign changes, there is nothing to
show. Otherwise let a_1<...<a_m be all its sign-changing zeros, with m<r.
Zeros which do not change sign do not affect the following argument.
The polynomial q(x)=product_(i=1)^m (x-a_i) has the same sign alternation
as f. Multiplying q by -1 if needed makes q(x) f(x)>=0 everywhere away
from isolated zeros, and strictly positive on a nonempty interval since
f is nonzero and continuous. The absolute moment conditions make q f
integrable. Therefore integral q f>0. But deg q=m<=r-1, so the assumed
orthogonality implies integral q f=0. This contradiction proves the lemma.
It also covers the possibility of infinitely many sign changes, in which
case the desired lower bound is immediate.

THEOREM 1. At every fixed finite s>0 there are at least FOUR distinct
positive sign-changing four-count blind gains.

Apply the lemma with r=4 to (1). Sign-changing does not by itself mean
simple: an odd zero of multiplicity three or higher is allowed. We claim
simplicity only for the two zeros separately certified below.

Together with the retained qualitative finiteness proof, this yields

 4 <= number of distinct positive four-count blind gains < infinity.

The upper bound here is qualitative; it is not a computed integer.

4. General count-channel consequence
-----------------------------------
The same argument has a useful general form. Suppose two distinct bounded
nonnegative intensity laws have equal zero atoms and agree in moments
0,...,r-1, while their r-th moments differ. For every integer k>=1, the
k-count channel has at least min(k,r) distinct positive sign-changing
blind gains.

For 1<=k<r, integrate eta^j F_k for j=0,...,k-1; the results are
j! times differences of moments of orders k-j-1, all zero. Apply the lemma
with r replaced by k. For k>=r, set g(eta)=eta^(k-r) F_k(eta) and integrate
eta^j g for j=0,...,r-1. The results are

 (k-r+j)! [E_A I^(r-j-1)-E_B I^(r-j-1)] = 0.

The positive prefactor eta^(k-r) does not alter positive-axis zeros or
signs. Absolute integrability follows exactly as before.

These functions are nonzero. Let H(eta)=E_A exp(-eta I)-E_B exp(-eta I).
Its r-th derivative at zero is nonzero. If F_k=(-1)^k H^(k) were identically
zero, H would be a polynomial of degree at most k-1. But H is bounded on
the positive axis and tends to the difference of zero atoms, namely zero.
The only such polynomial is zero, a contradiction.

For the current pair, every count channel k>=4 has at least four blind
gains; channels k=1,2,3 have at least one, two, three respectively. There
is no analogous positive-root lower bound here for the zero-count channel.
These are statements about varying detector gain at one fixed s and b=0.

5. Why the new finite-interval certificate reaches farther
---------------------------------------------------------
The retained certificate used Taylor polynomials in eta and exact moments
through order 32. Its guaranteed exclusion stopped at eta=1/128.
The new certificate uses the SAME exact moments with Hermite interpolation
in intensity. This changes the remainder control substantially.

Let nu_r be the positive measure I^r dP_G(I), and let
R_G,r(eta)=integral exp(-eta u) dnu_r(u).
Choose n distinct positive rational nodes x_1,...,x_n.

LOWER POLYNOMIAL. Let H interpolate f(u)=exp(-eta u) and f'(u) at every
node (2n interpolation conditions, degree at most 2n-1). The Hermite
remainder is

 f(u)-H(u)=f^(2n)(xi)/(2n)! * product_j (u-x_j)^2 >= 0.

The intermediate xi is between u and the interpolation nodes. The sign
holds for every u>=0, because f^(2n) is nonnegative.

UPPER POLYNOMIAL. Add the simple node 0 and keep all n positive nodes
double. The interpolant J has degree at most 2n. Its remainder is

 f(u)-J(u)=f^(2n+1)(xi)/(2n+1)! * u product_j (u-x_j)^2 <= 0

for u>=0. At interpolation nodes these identities extend by continuity.
These remainder formulas follow by applying Rolle's theorem with
multiplicities to the interpolation residual minus a suitable multiple
of the node polynomial, so no distributional quadrature theorem is needed.

Integrating with respect to the positive measure nu_r yields

 integral H dnu_r <= R_G,r(eta) <= integral J dnu_r.  (2)

The nodes only need to be distinct positive rationals. They are convenient
approximations to Gaussian-quadrature nodes, but no assertion of optimality,
root accuracy, or exact orthogonality is used in the proof. This avoids
having to certify algebraic quadrature nodes. The derivative interpolation
weights are retained exactly; they are not discarded as if the nodes were
exact Gaussian nodes.

For order r=4, the certificate uses 14 double nodes for the lower bound
(degree27, needs moments through31), and zero plus14 double nodes for the
upper bound (degree28, needs moments through32).
For r=5, it uses 14 double nodes below (degree27, through32), and zero plus
13 double nodes above (degree26, through31).

6. Exact Hermite basis and exponential evaluation
------------------------------------------------
For the lower interpolant define

 L_j(u)=product_(l!=j) (u-x_l)/(x_j-x_l),
 h_j(u)=[1-2 L'_j(x_j)(u-x_j)] L_j(u)^2,
 k_j(u)=(u-x_j) L_j(u)^2.

Then H=sum_j [f(x_j) h_j+f'(x_j) k_j]. All polynomial coefficients and
all their integrals against nu_r are rational because the nodes and
input moments are rational. Since f'(x_j)=-eta exp(-eta x_j), the
expectation has the form

 sum_j (a_j-eta b_j) exp(-eta x_j).

For the upper rule set

 g_j(u)=(u/x_j) L_j(u)^2,
 d_j=1/x_j+2 sum_(l!=j) 1/(x_j-x_l),
 h_j(u)=[1-d_j(u-x_j)] g_j(u),
 k_j(u)=(u-x_j) g_j(u),
 h_0(u)=product_j (1-u/x_j)^2.

The upper interpolant is f(0)h_0+sum_j[f(x_j)h_j+f'(x_j)k_j]. This is
verified directly by its values and first derivatives at all nodes.

For every rational z>=0, exp(-z) is enclosed rationally: halve z until
0<=z<=1; the Taylor polynomial through degree90 is an upper bound and
through91 a lower bound; then square the nonnegative interval repeatedly.
Every rounding is outward onto the rational grid 10^-95. The code does
not use a floating-point exponential. Exact polynomial weights are rounded
outward onto grid10^-65 only after their exact evaluation, and all interval
operations preserve these enclosures.

7. Certifying entire gain intervals
----------------------------------
The lower/upper bounds in (2) are finite exponential polynomials. For any
such E(eta)=sum_j(a_j-eta b_j) exp(-x_j eta), the code bounds E on [a,b]
by a degree12 Taylor polynomial at a, plus an explicit order13 remainder.

Its k-th derivative term is

 [(-x_j)^k(a_j-eta b_j)-k b_j(-x_j)^(k-1)] exp(-x_j eta).

The zero-node term is affine and has zero order13 derivative. For x_j>0,
writing A_j=max|a_j| and B_j=max|b_j| over their rational intervals, an
absolute order13 derivative bound on [a,b] is

 exp(-x_j a) [x_j^13(A_j+b B_j)+13 B_j x_j^12].

Multiply the sum by (b-a)^13/13! for the Taylor remainder. The Taylor
polynomial is expressed in the Bernstein basis on [a,b]; its range lies
between the smallest lower and largest upper Bernstein coefficients.
All interval endpoints, coefficients, remainders, and sign comparisons
are rational. Adaptive subdivision is used only to make those exact
bounds sharp enough. Every leaf interval and its bounds are stored and
rechecked, with a contiguous-cover check.

For F_4=R_A,4-R_B,4 the upper bound is upper_A4-lower_B4; its lower bound
is lower_A4-upper_B4. For F'_4=-R_A,5+R_B,5, the lower bound is
lower_B5-upper_A5. One must bound the actual derivative this way; simply
differentiating a one-sided bound for F_4 would not justify a derivative
inequality.

8. What the certificate establishes
----------------------------------
The retained certificate already proves exactly one simple root on
(0,1/128], with F_4 negative from that root through1/128. The new exact
calculation certifies all three statements

 F_4<0 on [1/128,1/100],
 F'_4>0 on [1/100,1/90],
 F_4>0 on [1/90,1/32].

Its endpoint enclosures include F_4(1/100)<0 and F_4(1/90)>0. Thus there
is exactly one additional root in (1/100,1/90); it is simple. Deterministic
rational bisection supplies the sharper bracket in the JSON certificate.
No roots occur in the two side intervals.

THEOREM 2. At t=1/2, s=2 log(2), b=0, exactly two four-count blind gains
lie in (0,1/32], and both are simple. At least two other positive
sign-changing blind gains lie strictly above1/32, by Theorem1.

The exact global number, the locations of all further roots, and an
effective gain cutoff beyond the final root remain open. The interval
certificate cannot close those claims: its upper/lower bounds become
wider at larger gains. No numerical scan is used to infer absence of
additional roots outside the certified interval.
