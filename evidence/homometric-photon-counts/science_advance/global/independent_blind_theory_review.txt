1. Global lower bound.
The gamma-integral identity is correct for powers j=0,1,2,3. Separate
Tonelli applications justify subtraction, and absolute integrability
follows by bounding the signed integrand by the two nonnegative ones.
At j=3, the zero atom matters; it vanishes for this analytic Gaussian
intensity model. The first three moment equalities and total mass one
therefore give orthogonality to every cubic polynomial. F4 is not zero,
because its value at zero is the positive fourth-moment gap.

If there are fewer than four sign changes, their complete list is finite.
The product of their linear factors has the same sign alternation as F4;
zeros of even multiplicity do not interfere. Its product with F4 can be
made nonnegative and positive on an interval. The moment bounds make its
integral finite, while orthogonality forces that integral to vanish.
This proves four distinct positive sign-changing zeros. It does not
prove simplicity or exact total count. The argument also covers infinitely
many changes by immediate exclusion of the contrary fewer-than-four case.

2. General channel statement.
For k>=r the multiplier eta^(k-r) produces exactly the claimed moment
orders r-1 down to0. For k<r, integrating powers0 throughk-1 produces
moments k-1 down to0. Nonvanishing of Fk follows from the Laplace
difference: if its kth derivative were zero, it would be a polynomial,
bounded and tending to zero on the positive axis, hence zero. This
contradicts the differing rth moment. Equal zero atoms are retained.
