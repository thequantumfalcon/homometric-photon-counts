1. HOMOGENEOUS CONTROL

The full centered covariance really is common: raw iid displacements with
covariance sigma^2 Id become sigma^2 P, P=Id-11^T/n, after centering. P does
not depend on the geometry or labeling of the n sites. Centering changes the
field only by a phase of modulus one, for each realization, and therefore does
not change intensity. The iid mean identity holds at every frequency.

The cumulant proof is complete through intensity order four. At a common
clean null, charge-one blocks vanish. For p<=3 only zero-charge factors and
matched charges (+2,-2) or (+3,-3) survive. At p=4 the only new phase-sensitive
charge patterns are (+4,-2,-2) and its conjugate. Three pairings on each side
give the factor 6. With x=exp(-s), the multiplying coefficient is

  -6 x^4(1-x)^5(x^3+3x^2+6x+6).

It is strictly negative for 0<x<1. Under the theorem's assumptions, the FOURTH
MOMENT differs if and only if the displayed diagonal-invariant difference is
nonzero. This does not characterize equality of the full intensity laws when
the invariant vanishes; the proof correctly avoids that stronger claim.

The supplied motif was independently evaluated using paired-angle binomial
identities, not the supplied radical-field multiplication code. This recovers
M1,...,M5 exactly over Q(i sqrt(3)), including both clean nulls, and gives

  T_A = -455015761/240000,
  T_B = +455015761/240000.

Thus D4(s)>0 for every finite s>0. Its factorization and the coefficient
c5=455015761/1250 are correct. The common leading Gaussian radial law depends
on n and |M2|. The actual two-dimensional covariance matrices need not coincide
in a fixed real/imaginary coordinate frame; their eigenvalues coincide, which
is what is needed for the squared-modulus law. In complex covariance notation,
E|G|^2=n is common while E[G^2] can differ in phase.

2. FIFTH-MOMENT ORDER AND EXACT ARITHMETIC

The analytic argument for D5(s)=O(s^6) is valid. At a clean null,
I_G(s)/s converges pointwise under the Gaussian coupling to a squared complex
Gaussian modulus. The bound I_G(s)/s <= n ||PZ||^2 supplies an integrable
dominating variable in every fixed moment. The fifth leading coefficients
therefore agree. Each intensity moment is a finite exponential polynomial in
s, so analyticity upgrades o(s^5) to O(s^6).

Independently substituting t^k=exp(-ks/2) into the saved exact polynomials gives

  D4(s) = (455015761/1250)s^5 - (54146875559/20000)s^6 + O(s^7),
  D5(s) = (28665992943/50)s^6 + O(s^7).

All earlier coefficients vanish exactly. Independent remainder constants are

  A4 = 66213438524959/144000,
  A5 = 347797653430767447163/192000000,
  B5 = 2474316747472711680,
  B6 = 11223500766536220180480.

Here Aj=sum_m |a_jm| m^6/720 for D_j(s)=sum_m a_jm exp(-ms).
All exponents m are nonnegative, so the stated Taylor-remainder bounds hold
for all s>=0, not merely asymptotically. The Bp chi-square calculation is also
correct. These deliberately large bounds are certificates, not tight estimates.

3. ADDITIVE READOUT

Common independent additive noise with finite fourth absolute moment preserves
the first three moment equalities and exactly preserves D4 by the binomial
expansion. All relevant moments exist. This proves distinction at every finite
s>0, but does not provide a noise-uniform classification guarantee.

For fixed Gaussian readout standard deviation sigma>0, the L1 Taylor expansion
of g_sigma(y-I) has fourth term D4 g_sigma^(4)/24 and fifth term
-D5 g_sigma^(5)/120. The sixth-order remainder is bounded by
B6 s^6 ||g_sigma^(6)||_1/720. With TV=(1/2)||p-q||_1, the leading constant is
therefore correctly c5 ||g_sigma^(4)||_1/48.

The exact Hermite-integral expression for ||g_sigma^(4)||_1 and every displayed
Gaussian remainder/upper-bound factor were checked. The nonzero leading
function plus an O(s^6) L1 remainder implies the equality asymptotic for TV;
this is stronger than a bound from one statistic and needs no unproved exchange
of a pointwise expansion with integration. Constants can depend on fixed sigma.

4. PHOTON COUNTS AND PARITY

For fixed b>=0 and eta>0, the Poisson factorial-moment argument is correct.
The first three factorial moments match and the fourth difference is eta^4 D4.
The Banach-space derivative identity eta^j(S-Id)^j p_lambda is valid also at
zero background, with norm at most (2 eta)^j on nonnegative intensities.

The leading TV coefficient is correctly

  (c5 eta^4/48) ||(S-Id)^4 p_b||_1.

At b=0 the coefficient vector is (1,-4,6,-4,1), whose L1 norm is 16. Thus the
coefficient becomes c5 eta^4/3. The explicit zero-background remainder and
the simpler all-s upper bound have the correct factors 1/3, 2/15, and 2/45.

The even-count probability contrast has the same positive leading coefficient
as TV. The parity rule's success probability is 1/2 plus half this contrast;
the optimal equal-prior one-observation success probability is 1/2 plus TV/2.
Their ADVANTAGES above 1/2 have ratio tending to one. The proof correctly states
that the absolute advantage tends to zero and does not claim exact finite-s,
positive-background, or multiple-observation optimality.

The m-observation upper bound from TV tensorization is valid. Its resulting
Omega(s^-5) necessary sample count is not claimed to be a sharp rate; no
sufficiency or achievability conclusion follows from that argument.

5. SHARP HELLINGER AND INDEPENDENT-SAMPLE ORDERS

The completed sample_complexity_proofs.tex was read in full, not only its
outline. Its definition is h^2=int(sqrt(p)-sqrt(q))^2=2(1-rho). All three
leading coefficients and the corresponding sharp sample orders are correct:

  Gaussian, fixed sigma>0:
    h^2 ~ c5^2/(96 sigma^8) s^10;
  Poisson, fixed b>0 and eta>0:
    h^2 ~ c5^2 eta^8/(96 b^4) s^10;
  Poisson, b=0 and fixed eta>0:
    h^2 = eta^4 c5^2/(96 m4) s^6 + O(s^7).

The common Gaussian radial limit, moment domination, and analyticity of each
fixed intensity moment give D_k=O(s^(k+1)) for every fixed k, including the
needed D6=O(s^7). Independently expanding the real Gaussian component moments
confirms m4=24 n^4+72 n^2 R+9 R^2=75408578015121/160000.

The later measurement-results additions record this m4, the unit-parameter
Hellinger constants, and the sharp sample orders consistently. Their exact
rational values and current source/input hashes were independently checked.

In the Gaussian proof, subtracting the common mean makes Jensen's denominator
lower bound uniform in the observation. The centered intensities remain
bounded in absolute value by n^2. Compactness gives uniform sixth-derivative
bounds in the weighted Hilbert space, and their sixth absolute moments are
O(s^6). The Taylor remainder therefore has the claimed weighted-L2 order.
The passage to Hellinger uses L1 convergence of the weighted squared density
gap and a uniformly bounded reciprocal denominator tending pointwise to 1/4;
this step is justified. The kernel identity gives the exact 4!/sigma^8 norm.

For positive-background counts, the kernel identity exp(uv/b), compact kernel
derivative bounds, and the uniform likelihood-ratio lower bound exp(-eta n^2)
provide the analogous complete argument. The derivative norm 4!/b^4 and the
factor 1/4 from the square-root denominator give the stated constant. The
proof explicitly excludes a joint b-to-zero limit.

For zero background, the k=4 probability is eta^4 m4 s^4/24+O(s^5) and its
contrast is eta^4 c5 s^5/24+O(s^6); this gives the leading s^6 Hellinger term.
Counts k=0,1,2,3 contribute O(s^10), O(s^9), O(s^8), O(s^7), respectively;
k=5 and k=6 contribute O(s^7) and O(s^8). The factorial-moment bound on the
entire k>=7 tail is O(s^7), so no nonuniform infinite-series argument is used.

The binary indicator of exactly four counts retains the full leading
Hellinger coefficient; its complementary-event contribution is O(s^10).
Its empirical frequency is sufficient for that Bernoulli experiment and can
therefore attain the same sample-order scaling under the calibrated model.
By contrast, parity alone has h^2=c5^2 eta^7 s^9/(36n)+O(s^10). This correctly
distinguishes one-observation TV optimality from information retained for
many observations. No finite-noise detector optimum follows.

The product-affinity bounds 1-rho^m <= TV(P^m,Q^m) <= sqrt(1-rho^(2m))
give matching necessary and sufficient orders for any fixed target success
strictly between 1/2 and 1. The logarithm signs and ceilings in the source
are correct. Hence the minimum independent sample count is Theta(s^-10) for
fixed Gaussian readout or fixed positive-background counts, Theta(s^-6) for
full zero-background counts (also the four-count indicator), and Theta(s^-9)
for parity-only zero-background observations. These are sharp exponents,
not sharp optimal prefactors or finite experimental acquisition times.
