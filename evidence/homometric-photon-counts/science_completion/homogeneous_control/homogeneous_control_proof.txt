HOMOGENEOUS IID GAUSSIAN DISPLACEMENT: AN EXACT DISTRIBUTION-LEVEL CONTROL
Date: 2026-10-20

STATUS AND SCOPE

This is a new exact derivation for the point-set definitions below. It does
not rely on a recovered historical certificate. It establishes a mathematical
statement inside an ideal scattering model, not an experimental observation
or publication-priority claim. Its conclusions were checked with exact
rational arithmetic, including two independent moment-computation methods.

The central result is stronger than a control that assigns different local
variance profiles to the two structures. Here every raw displacement has the
same Gaussian distribution, every centered displacement has the same marginal
variance, and the entire centered displacement covariance matrix is identical
for the two structures. The clean homometry preserves the mean intensity at
every frequency and every noise level. At the selected clean amplitude null,
the first three raw intensity moments also match exactly. Nevertheless, the
fourth intensity moment differs at every finite positive noise level.

1. GENERAL THEOREM

Let A and B be finite point sets with the same number n of unit-weight sites.
For a real observation frequency q, define

  z_(X,j) = exp(i q x_(X,j)),
  M_(X,k) = sum_j z_(X,j)^k,
  F_X(s) = sum_j z_(X,j) exp(i sqrt(s) Z_j),
  I_X(s) = |F_X(s)|^2,

where X is A or B, s>=0, and the Z_j are independent N(0,1) variables. The
displacement mechanism is identical at every site of both structures. Put

  t = exp(-s/2).

Assume:

  (i)  M_(A,1)=M_(B,1)=0;
  (ii) |M_(A,k)|=|M_(B,k)| for k=2,3,4.

Condition (ii) follows, in particular, when the clean point sets are
homometric at all frequencies. Define

  D = Re[M_(A,4) conjugate(M_(A,2))^2]
      - Re[M_(B,4) conjugate(M_(B,2))^2].

Then, exactly for all s>=0,

  E[I_A(s)^p] = E[I_B(s)^p],  p=0,1,2,3,

and

  E[I_A(s)^4] - E[I_B(s)^4]
    = 6 kappa_(4,0)(t) kappa_(2,0)(t)^2 D,

where

  kappa_(2,0)(t) = t^4 - t^2,

  kappa_(4,0)(t)
    = t^16 - 4 t^10 - 3 t^8 + 12 t^6 - 6 t^4
    = t^4 (t^2-1)^3 (t^6+3t^4+6t^2+6).

Consequently, if D is nonzero, the intensity distributions are different for
every finite s>0. The sign of the fourth-moment difference is -sign(D).
This is a sufficient condition, not a characterization of every homometric
pair. A pair with D=0 might still differ in higher moments or at other
frequencies; this theorem does not decide that case.

The invariant in D is a real diagonal bispectral invariant of the CLEAN
structure. With Fourier convention M_k=F_clean(kq), it is the real part of
the conjugate of M_2^2 conjugate(M_4). It is third order in structural Fourier
amplitudes. The observed statistic I^4 is the fourth intensity moment and
the eighth field moment. These orders must not be conflated.

2. PROOF OF THE GENERAL THEOREM

2.1 Single-site moments and cumulants

Let w=exp(i sqrt(s) Z), Z~N(0,1). The Gaussian characteristic function gives

  mu_(a,b) = E[w^a conjugate(w)^b]
           = exp[-s(a-b)^2/2] = t^((a-b)^2).

Use the formal bivariate cumulants kappa_(a,b) defined by the coefficients of
log E[exp(u w + v conjugate(w))]. Because |w|=1, the generating function
exists near the origin and the usual moment-cumulant identities apply.

For a deterministic unit complex number z, the mixed cumulant of a copies
of zw and b copies of conjugate(zw) is

  z^(a-b) kappa_(a,b).

Independence makes cumulants additive. Thus the sum F_X has mixed cumulants

  K_(X,a,b) = kappa_(a,b) M_(X,a-b),

where M_(X,0)=n and M_(X,-k)=conjugate(M_(X,k)). Since each mu_(a,b) is real
and symmetric in a,b, so is kappa_(a,b).

The pure second and fourth cumulants are the ordinary algebraic cumulants
of w:

  kappa_(2,0)=mu_(2,0)-mu_(1,0)^2=t^4-t^2;

  kappa_(4,0)=mu_(4,0)-4 mu_(3,0)mu_(1,0)-3 mu_(2,0)^2
             +12 mu_(2,0)mu_(1,0)^2-6 mu_(1,0)^4.

Substitution gives the formulas in the theorem, including the displayed
factorization. The factorization can be verified by multiplying out.

2.2 Classification of all structural factors through intensity order four

Expand E[F_X^p conjugate(F_X)^p] by partitions of p labelled positive slots
and p labelled negative slots. A block with a positive and b negative slots
contributes kappa_(a,b) M_(X,a-b). Call a-b its charge.

All charges sum to zero. A block of charge +1 or -1 contributes zero because
M_(X,1)=0. Charge-zero blocks contribute the common factor n. The total
positive charge is at most p, since it cannot exceed the total number of
positive slots. The same statement holds for the absolute negative charge.

For p<=3, nonzero surviving charges are only a matched pair (+2,-2) or
(+3,-3), with possible additional zero-charge blocks. The structural factors
therefore depend only on n, |M_2|^2, and |M_3|^2. They agree for A and B.

For p=4, the complete list of nonzero charge multisets is

  empty;
  (+2,-2), (+3,-3), (+4,-4);
  (+2,+2,-2,-2);
  (+4,-2,-2), (-4,+2,+2).

Zero-charge blocks may occur where the slot budget permits. The first five
types depend only on n and magnitudes |M_k| and thus cancel in A minus B.

For charge multiset (+4,-2,-2), all eight slots have already been used:
the +4 block contains the four positive slots, and the two -2 blocks split
the four negative slots into two unordered pairs. There are exactly three
such pairings. Its contribution is

  3 kappa_(4,0) kappa_(0,2)^2 M_4 conjugate(M_2)^2.

The conjugate charge multiset contributes its complex conjugate. Their sum
is 6 kappa_(4,0) kappa_(2,0)^2 Re[M_4 conjugate(M_2)^2].
Subtracting the two structures proves the identity.

For 0<t<1, kappa_(2,0)^2>0 and kappa_(4,0)<0. Hence D!=0 gives a strictly
nonzero fourth-moment gap for every finite positive s. The intensities lie
in [0,n^2], so identical laws would have identical fourth moments. This proves
distributional nonidentity.

3. THE CONCRETE 63-SITE HOMOMETRIC PAIR

Set

  theta = arcsin(1/(5 sqrt(3))),
  phi   = -arcsin(1/(8 sqrt(3))),
  a = 3 theta/pi,  b = 3 phi/pi.

Define the 21-site motif U by

  U = {3j+1+(-1)^j a : j=0,...,9}
      union {3j+delta_j : j=0,...,9}
      union {30},

where delta_0=delta_1=0 and delta_j=(-1)^j b for j=2,...,9. Let

  V_A = {0,31,63},  V_B = {0,32,63}=63-V_A,
  A=U+V_A,  B=U+V_B.

The motif lies in [0,30]; its listed sites are distinct. The translates are
disjoint, so each point set has n=63 sites. One can see the distinctness
without numerical rounding: 0<a<1/4 and -1/4<b<0, and the base positions
within U have integer spacing at least one.

For any real q, let F_S(q)=sum_(x in S) exp(i q x). Then

  F_A(q)=F_U(q) F_VA(q),
  F_B(q)=F_U(q) exp(i 63q) conjugate(F_VA(q)),

so |F_A(q)|=|F_B(q)| for every real q. This proves clean homometry directly.

Now choose omega=pi/3 and define M_(X,k)=F_X(k omega). Exact phase arithmetic
uses

  cos(theta)=sqrt(222)/15, sin(theta)=sqrt(3)/15,
  cos(phi)=sqrt(573)/24,   sin(phi)=-sqrt(3)/24,

and the sixth roots of unity for the integer base positions. The required
power sums simplify to the smaller field Q(i sqrt(3)):

  M_(A,1)=M_(B,1)=0;

  M_(A,2)=71/40 + i (413/40) sqrt(3),
  M_(B,2)=131/8 + i (171/40) sqrt(3);

  M_(A,3)=-1 + i (1183/400) sqrt(3),
  M_(B,3)= 1 - i (1183/400) sqrt(3);

  M_(A,4)=123901/48000 - i (470783/48000) sqrt(3),
  M_(B,4)=6145/384 - i (173441/48000) sqrt(3).

In particular |M_(A,2)|^2=|M_(B,2)|^2=129187/400. Multiplying the exact
expressions gives

  Re[M_(A,4) conjugate(M_(A,2))^2] = -455015761/240000,
  Re[M_(B,4) conjugate(M_(B,2))^2] = +455015761/240000,

and therefore

  D = -455015761/120000.

This real invariant is unchanged by any common spatial translation or
reflection of a point set: translation phases cancel, and reflection
conjugates the product without changing its real part. Its unequal values
also give an exact certificate that A and B are not related by a translation
or reflection.

The general theorem now gives the explicit, strictly positive factorization

  Delta_4(t) := E[I_A^4]-E[I_B^4]
    = (455015761/20000) t^8 (1-t^2)^5 (t^6+3t^4+6t^2+6),

for every 0<t<1. This is the first differing raw intensity moment at the
chosen frequency: moments of orders one, two, and three are exactly equal.
Since the first three raw moments agree, the fourth central moments and
the fourth cumulants differ by the same amount.

4. WHY THE DISPLACEMENT CONTROL IS IDENTICAL

Give every site, in either structure, an independent displacement
xi_j~N(0,sigma^2), using the same sigma^2. Optional centering is

  u_j=xi_j-bar(xi),  P=Id-11^T/n.

Its covariance is sigma^2 P in either structure. Every marginal is
N(0,sigma^2(n-1)/n), every off-diagonal covariance is -sigma^2/n, and the
centered mean square displacement parameter is

  v=trace(Cov(u))/n=sigma^2(n-1)/n.

Centering multiplies the complex field by exp(-i q bar(xi)), whose modulus
is one, so it leaves the intensity unchanged for every realization.

At omega=pi/3 and n=63,

  s=omega^2 sigma^2 = (7 pi^2/62) v,
  t=exp(-s/2)=exp(-7 pi^2 v/124).

For any observation frequency q, the independent-noise characteristic
function yields

  E[I_X(q)] = n(1-exp(-q^2 sigma^2))
              + exp(-q^2 sigma^2) |F_X(q)|^2.

Clean homometry therefore gives exactly equal ensemble means for ALL real
frequencies and ALL nonnegative noise variances. At omega the null reduces
the common mean to n(1-t^2).

This theorem isolates a difference in clean structural phase information
under one common noise law. It does not attribute the gap to different
local variance profiles, to graph-dependent covariance, or to differing
centered single-site marginals: none of those differences is present here.

5. EXPLICIT FINITE-NOISE WITNESSES AND TOTAL-VARIATION LOWER BOUNDS

Use total variation TV(P,Q)=sup_E |P(E)-Q(E)|. For any measurable f taking
values in [0,1], |E_P f-E_Q f|<=TV(P,Q). Since 0<=I<=n^2, f(I)=I^4/n^8
gives the exact lower bound

  TV(Law(I_A),Law(I_B)) >= Delta_4(t)/n^8 > 0.

The equality of the first three moments gives an improved bounded
polynomial witness. Put y=I/n^2 in [0,1] and

  f(y)=(1+T_4(2y-1))/2
      =64 y^4-128 y^3+80 y^2-16 y+1,

where T_4(x)=8x^4-8x^2+1=cos(4 arccos(x)) on [-1,1]. Thus 0<=f<=1. The
lower-order moment terms cancel, giving

  TV(Law(I_A),Law(I_B)) >= 64 Delta_4(t)/n^8.

The factor 64 is optimal WITHIN the class of polynomials of degree at most
four taking values in [0,1] on the full allowed normalized intensity interval
[0,1]. To prove this, let g(y) have leading coefficient a_4 and put
p(x)=2g((x+1)/2)-1, so |p(x)|<=1 on [-1,1] and its leading coefficient is
a_4/8. Suppose first a_4>64. Then alpha=a_4/64>1 and
h(x)=p(x)-alpha T_4(x) has degree at most three. At the five successive
extrema x_j=cos(j pi/4), T_4(x_j)=(-1)^j and |p(x_j)|<=1<alpha. Thus h
alternates strictly in sign at these five points and must have at least
four distinct zeros, a contradiction. Replacing p by -p handles a_4<-64.
Therefore |a_4|<=64. The expectation contrast of g is a_4 Delta_4/n^8
because its lower-order terms cancel. The displayed Chebyshev polynomial
attains the bound. This is NOT optimality over all bounded test functions,
over the full total variation, or over physical experiments.

At t=1/sqrt(2), equivalently

  s=log(2),  v=62 log(2)/(7 pi^2),

the exact gap is

  Delta_4 = 35946245119/81920000 = 438.79693748779296875,

and the improved total-variation bound is

  TV >= 35946245119/317639398742426880000
     approximately 1.1316683403039915 * 10^(-10).

At t=1/2, equivalently s=2 log(2) and v=124 log(2)/(7 pi^2),

  Delta_4 = 54510433152039/335544320000
          = 162.45375022899806499481201171875,

  TV >= 224322770173/5354119247938191360000
     approximately 4.1897230858162913 * 10^(-11).

The second point was additionally checked by direct site-by-site exact
moment convolution, independently of the cumulant calculation. The first
point is an exact rational evaluation of the independently checked moment
polynomial because it contains only even powers of t.

These bounds are deliberately interpreted as certificates of nonidentity.
Their numerical size does not certify practical detectability, a useful
classification accuracy, or an affordable sample size. They are lower
bounds, not estimates of the actual total variation.

6. SMALL-NOISE AND HIGH-NOISE LIMITS

Writing x=t^2=exp(-s),

  Delta_4(s)=(455015761/20000) exp(-4s) (1-exp(-s))^5
             * [exp(-3s)+3exp(-2s)+6exp(-s)+6].

Therefore

  Delta_4(s)=(455015761/1250) s^5 + O(s^6), as s down to zero,

and

  Delta_4(s)=(1365047283/10000) exp(-4s) [1+o(1)], as s to infinity.

Both endpoint gaps vanish. Strict positivity holds at every finite s>0,
which is compatible with equality of the limiting distributions.

In particular the leading small-noise distribution is the SAME for A and B
in this homogeneous control. The linearized complex field has covariance
n and pseudocovariance of modulus |M_2|. Homometry makes |M_2| equal, so
both limits are the same weighted two-degree Gaussian square law. The new
distinction occurs beyond that leading Gaussian approximation; the fourth
moment gap is of order s^5 while each fourth moment begins at order s^4.

At high noise the independent wrapped Gaussian phases approach independent
uniform phases, giving the same finite-n uniform-phasor intensity law for
both structures. There is no contradiction with a nonzero gap at all finite
positive s.

7. FIFTH-MOMENT CONTROL FOR SUBSEQUENT DETECTOR ANALYSIS

At a clean null, centering the phase vector changes no intensity. For a
standard real Gaussian vector Z in R^n,

  I(s)=|sum_j z_j [exp(i sqrt(s) (PZ)_j)-1]|^2
      <= n sum_j |exp(i sqrt(s) (PZ)_j)-1|^2
      <= n s ||PZ||^2.

The first inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz, and the second uses
|exp(i x)-1|<=|x|. Since P is an orthogonal projector of rank n-1,
||PZ||^2 has the chi-square distribution with n-1 degrees of freedom. Its
fifth raw moment is product_(j=0)^4(n-1+2j). Consequently each structure
satisfies

  E[I_X(s)^5] <= n^5 s^5 product_(j=0)^4(n-1+2j).

The sum for A and B is bounded by twice this expression. This elementary
bound uses the same s=raw Gaussian phase variance as the exact factorization.

The accompanying JSON also records exact individual intensity-moment
polynomials through order five, their sums and differences, and evaluations
at both finite-noise witnesses. No detector theorem is asserted here; that
analysis should state its observation model separately.

The exact fifth-moment sum has small-noise expansion

  E[I_A(s)^5]+E[I_B(s)^5]
    = (1081761176507823/3200) s^5 + O(s^6).

All coefficients of s^0 through s^4 vanish exactly. The leading coefficient
also equals 240 n^5+1200 n^3 R+450 n R^2 with n=63 and R=129187/400,
as obtained from the common Gaussian linearization.

For assessing the particular Chebyshev witness, the JSON additionally
records exact intensity moments through order eight at t=1/2 and the exact
mean and variance of f(I/n^2). These were calculated by fixed-t cumulant
recursion. The moments through order five additionally agree with the
independent direct-site method. The higher moments are not represented as
an independent experimental or sampling verification.

8. REPRODUCTION AND VERIFICATION

Run:

  python homogeneous_control.py

Only the Python standard library is required. The script writes
homogeneous_control_results.json beside itself. It does all of the following
with exact Fraction arithmetic:

  * Rebuilds all 63 phases for each point set from the displayed definition.
  * Verifies unit modulus, exact amplitude nulls, and equal |M_k| through k=5.
  * Evaluates the diagonal bispectral invariants exactly.
  * Generates single-site mixed cumulants from mu_(a,b)=t^((a-b)^2).
  * Reconstructs all intensity moments through order five by the
    distinguished-block moment-cumulant recursion.
  * Checks that differences through order three vanish as polynomials.
  * Checks the fourth-moment difference against its claimed factorization.
  * Checks every computed moment through order five at t=1/2 using a second
    method: direct convolution of all individual-site mixed moments.

The direct method expands (F+zw)^a(conjugate(F)+conjugate(z)conjugate(w))^b
at each added site. It does not use cumulants or the precomputed power sums.
Agreement is exact, not a floating-point tolerance test or Monte Carlo fit.

For reference, the recursion used for the first method is, for a>=1,

  H_(a,b) = sum_(i=1)^a sum_(j=0)^b
            binom(a-1,i-1) binom(b,j) K_(i,j) H_(a-i,b-j),

where H_(0,0)=1 and H_(a,b)=E[F^a conjugate(F)^b]. If a=0, use the
one-variable version with a distinguished negative slot. Solving the same
identity for the single-site cumulant yields the kappa recursion. Every
factor and combinatorial multiplicity follows from the block containing
one fixed labelled slot.

All reported polynomials, exact rational witness values, and the source
SHA-256 are retained in the JSON. Decimal values describe already certified
rational numbers; the proof of positivity is the exact factorization.
