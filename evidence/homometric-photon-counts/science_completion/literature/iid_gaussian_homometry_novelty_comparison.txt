TARGETED LITERATURE COMPARISON: IID GAUSSIAN JITTER AND FINITE HOMOMETRY
Prepared: 2026-10-20
Status: research note; not a proof certificate or a priority determination.

1. QUESTION UNDER INVESTIGATION

Let A and B be fixed, finite, equally weighted point sets with the same complete
clean diffraction, |F_A(q)|^2 = |F_B(q)|^2 for every frequency q. Apply independent,
identically distributed, homogeneous Gaussian displacements to every point of
either set. At a selected common clean null F_A(q0) = F_B(q0) = 0, investigate
whether the first three single-frequency intensity moments agree exactly for all
noise variances while the fourth differs through

    T_G = Re[M_4(G) conjugate(M_2(G))^2],
    M_k(G) = sum_j exp(i k q0 . r_j).

The project initially supplied this as a candidate direction. A subsequent
internal AI proof review checked the general moment identity and independently
rebuilt the exact 63-site power sums, clean null, and nonzero T_A-T_B. See
../independent_completion_review.txt and the homogeneous_control proof files.
This literature note assesses priority separately from that mathematical review;
neither is external peer review or experimental validation.

Overall assessment: the mean-scattering identity, random-phasor formulation,
finite Gaussian phasor calculations [S25], bispectral phase invariant, and
principle that higher statistics can distinguish homometric systems are
established. The possible contribution is the exact
finite example and the selection rule identifying the first distinguishing
single-frequency intensity moment under identical Gaussian displacement laws.
Priority for that precise combination remains unverified. A search that does not
find the same theorem cannot establish that it has never appeared.

2. DEFINITIONS THAT MUST NOT BE CONFLATED

Write S = sum_j z_j w_j, z_j = exp(i q0 . r_j), I = |S|^2, and
w_j = exp(i sigma X_j), with independent standard normal X_j. Here sigma^2 is
the variance of the projected phase q0 . u_j, not necessarily the raw spatial
displacement variance. In one dimension with displacement variance v it is
q0^2 v; in d dimensions it is q0^T C q0 for displacement covariance C.

Gaussian PHASE errors do not make S exactly complex Gaussian for finite n.
Replacing S by a Gaussian approximation can erase the finite higher cumulants
that the candidate theorem depends on. The labels below refer to different
objects:

  - Fourth intensity moment: E[I^4] = E[S^4 conjugate(S)^4].
  - Eighth field moment: another description of that same expectation.
  - Third-order structural Fourier invariant: F(2q0)^2 conjugate(F(4q0)).
  - Fourth spatial density correlation: a different observable, usually a
    function of several spatial offsets, not E[I(q0)^4].

For the usual structural bispectrum B(q1,q2) = F(q1) F(q2)
conjugate(F(q1+q2)), the proposed T_G equals Re[B(2q0,2q0)]. The complex
conjugation convention changes its imaginary part, not its real part. Therefore
the invariant itself is a familiar diagonal bispectrum. Translation invariance
follows because the three frequencies sum to zero. Reflection conjugates the
bispectrum and leaves this real part unchanged. These are direct algebraic
observations, not claims that a cited source studied this exact measurement.

4. WHAT THE EXISTING RESULTS ALREADY IMPLY

The iid mean identity is a direct specialization of established displacement
theory [G04, KKT20]. For n fixed points and iid displacements with characteristic
function phi(q), separating the diagonal j=k terms yields exactly

    E I_G(q) = n + |phi(q)|^2 (|F_G(q)|^2 - n).

Thus identical clean diffraction remains identical in the ensemble mean under
the same iid displacement distribution. Gaussianity is not necessary. For the
Gaussian projected phase variance sigma^2, |phi(q)|^2 = exp(-sigma^2).

The candidate higher-moment calculation can also be formulated with completely
standard finite algebra. If alpha and beta range over nonnegative integer n-tuples
with |alpha| = |beta| = p, multinomial expansion and the Gaussian characteristic
function give

  E I^p = sum_(alpha,beta) [p!/alpha!][p!/beta!]
          z^(alpha-beta) exp[-(sigma^2/2) ||alpha-beta||_2^2].

This formula is an independent derivation in this note. It is a way to compute or
prove the candidate identity, not evidence of a new probability principle. Its
combinatorial terms can be regrouped into structural Fourier invariants. The
common clean null M_1=0 removes terms containing M_1 or its conjugate. Equality
of complete clean diffraction supplies |M_k(A)| = |M_k(B)| for every integer k.
The charge classification in the completed proof establishes the exact equality
through intensity order three and the fourth-order difference. This was checked
by a separate internal AI review and independent exact power-sum arithmetic.

The bispectral identification [JK03] also does not automatically prove that this
particular real diagonal bispectrum differs for the supplied pair. A full
bispectrum contains information at many frequency pairs; the project uses one
specific real-valued sample. No general phase-retrieval theorem guarantees that
this sample alone determines either set or distinguishes every homometric pair.

5. INTERNAL PROOF CROSS-CHECK, NOT EXTERNAL REVIEW

An independent cumulant argument gives the following checked coefficient.
At M_1=0, let x = exp(-sigma^2). For w = exp(i sigma X), ordinary non-conjugate
cumulants are

  kappa_2(w) = x^2 - x,
  kappa_4(w) = x^2 (x-1)^3 (x^3 + 3x^2 + 6x + 6).

In the partition expansion of E[S^4 conjugate(S)^4], the partitions with charges
(4,-2,-2) and (-4,2,2) contribute

  6 kappa_4(w) kappa_2(w)^2 Re[M_4 conjugate(M_2)^2]
  = -6 x^4 (1-x)^5 (x^3 + 3x^2 + 6x + 6) T_G.

The coefficient is nonzero for every 0 < sigma^2 < infinity. Charge-one blocks
vanish at the clean null. The remaining balanced charge patterns use only n and
magnitudes |M_2|, |M_3|, |M_4|, as verified by the complete charge classification.
The exact difference of fourth intensity moments is consequently that
coefficient times T_A-T_B. Independent exact arithmetic recovers
T_A=-455015761/240000 and T_B=+455015761/240000 for the supplied instance.
These are internally checked mathematical results, not experimental findings.

A precise way to position the result is therefore:
an exact finite measurement-selection theorem, using a known bispectral
invariant, with a demonstrable example under a common homogeneous noise law.
Novelty and mathematical correctness are separate assessments.

8. REMAINING NOVELTY WORK

Before publication, compare the final audited theorem, not a changing project
description, against the finite nonuniform-phase literature; inspect
[BA81, BA83, BA88] and their cited predecessors in full; follow the references in
[MBP24]; and check whether a general exact moment/cumulant theorem already gives
the claim as a direct corollary. Search equivalent descriptions: Gaussian
position jitter, wrapped-normal phase noise, homometric arrays, partially
developed speckle, bispectral invariants, k-decks, and null-depth statistics.

The result can still be valuable if its ingredients are standard. An explicit,
transparent, reproducible example can establish a useful limitation of ordinary
diffraction and show exactly which additional measurement resolves it. Such
value does not require claiming a new invariant or a new physical law.

This note records a targeted search of primary publications and author
preprints. It is not an exhaustive systematic review, a patent search, a
guarantee of publication, or proof that no earlier equivalent construction
exists. No external contacts were made.

9. DETECTOR CONSEQUENCES ADDED AFTER THE INITIAL SEARCH

The completed internal proof also shows that common independent additive noise
with a finite fourth absolute moment preserves the fourth-moment distinction.
For a fixed Gaussian readout density g_sigma, the measured total variation is
(c5/48)||g_sigma^(4)||_1 s^5+O(s^6). For fixed-gain Poisson counts with background
b, its coefficient is (c5 eta^4/48)||(S-Id)^4 p_b||_1. At zero background this
is c5 eta^4/3, and the even/odd count decision rule is asymptotically optimal
for one observation with equal priors, in the ratio of advantages above chance.
Here c5=455015761/1250, and s denotes phase variance, not phase standard deviation.

The subsequent sample_complexity_proofs.tex establishes sharp sample ORDERS for
these calibrated models. With h^2=int(sqrt(p)-sqrt(q))^2, the leading coefficients
are c5^2/(96 sigma^8) times s^10 for Gaussian readout,
c5^2 eta^8/(96 b^4) times s^10 for fixed positive Poisson background, and
c5^2 eta^4/(96 m4) times s^6 for zero background, where
m4=75408578015121/160000. Product affinity yields minimum independent sample
orders Theta(s^-10), Theta(s^-10), and Theta(s^-6), respectively, for any fixed
target success strictly between 1/2 and 1 under equal priors. The exactly-four-
count indicator retains the zero-background leading Hellinger term; parity
alone instead has sample order Theta(s^-9). These statements were independently
checked in the internal review.

These detector statements are consequences of the established moment identity
and classical binomial, Taylor, weighted-Hilbert-space, Poisson, affinity-product,
and binary-testing methods. The present search has not settled priority for
their exact specialization or for the selection theorem itself. Do not present
them as a new invariant, a universal classification algorithm, an optimized
finite-noise experimental design, or experimentally validated performance.
