FRESH GLOBAL PROOF: EXACTLY TWO SIMPLE POSITIVE MEAN-CONTRAST ROOTS

1. The theorem and the object being proved
========================================

Let D(x)=sum_i c_i exp(-r_i x), where the 2005 exact rational rates and
algebraic real coefficients are the terms in input_premises.json.  This
input is an unchanged copy of the fresh grouped reconstruction from the
fixed 63-site model.  Its real embedding uses the positive square roots
of 3, 74 and 191 and the specified bit-mask basis.

The new certificate proves:

  D has exactly two roots in (0,infinity), and both roots are simple.
  D has a double zero at the origin.
  Its signs on the three complementary positive intervals are +, -, +.

For the original control, x=7*pi^2*v/62.  The marginal-preserving control
has the exact identity

  Delta_star(v)=exp(7*pi^2*v/(62*61))*D(7*pi^2*v/61).

Consequently both controls have exactly two simple positive roots, and
the star roots are exactly 61/62 times the original-v roots.

This is a fresh proof, not restoration of an earlier certificate's bytes
or execution history.  No historical root count, interval cover, root
simplicity claim, or root localization is assumed.  Diagnostic values
were used only to choose candidate proof regions.  Every accepted region
and root endpoint is subsequently established by outward arithmetic.

The correspondence between this explicit D and the graph/Gaussian model
is the exact grouped reconstruction in the earlier continuation.  The
copied input records and hashes that construction.  The present proof is
a global root certificate for that exact supplied function; it does not
claim a root count for other covariance models or other frequencies.

2. Positive rescaling and proof partition
========================================

Put r0=min_i r_i=121483/242972 and lambda_i=r_i-r0, and define

  F(x)=exp(r0*x)D(x)=sum_i c_i exp(-lambda_i*x).

The lambda_i are strictly increasing, lambda_0=0, and lambda_i>0 for
i>0.  Multiplication by exp(r0*x) preserves every zero, multiplicity and
sign.  The first two coefficients and the first positive shifted rate are

  c0=(sqrt(573)-1)/60>0,
  c1=-146/75,
  lambda_1=189/242972.

The complete proof uses the following adjacent regions:

  (0,1]       F(x)/x^2 > 17/1000.
  [1,10]      F(x)>0, certified by three closed interval cells.
  [10,11]     F'(x)<0, with F(10)>0 and F(11)<0.
  [11,2048]   F(x)<0, certified by nine closed interval cells.
  [2048,infinity) F'(x)>0, and F(x) tends to c0>0.

Endpoints are included in the stated finite cells.  Their exact rational
values are checked for adjacency and full coverage.  There is no uncovered
gap, and all shared boundaries 1, 10, 11 and 2048 are nonzero points of F.

The first root therefore lies in (10,11) and is unique there by strict
decrease.  The second lies in (2048,infinity) and is unique there by strict
increase, the negative left value and the positive limit.  These regions
exhaust the positive axis.  The derivative signs at both roots prove
simplicity for F, and hence for D and both positive-factor rescalings in v.

3. Arithmetic enclosures
========================

All certificate endpoints are integers divided by S=2^256.  Addition,
subtraction, multiplication, reciprocal and squaring use exact integer
floor or ceiling in the necessary direction.  The four corner products
bound interval multiplication; intervals crossing zero use lower bound
zero when squared.  No floating-point arithmetic or Decimal exponential
is used for a mathematical decision.

A positive radical sqrt(q) is bounded by

  floor(sqrt(q*S^2))/S and ceil(sqrt(q*S^2))/S,

using integer square root.  Rational field coefficients are then multiplied
and summed with outward arithmetic.  Every coefficient interval excludes
zero, so every coefficient's actual real sign is certified.

To bound exp(-u), u>=0, range reduction divides by 2^s until the reduced
argument lies in [0,1/8], apart from at most one endpoint rounding unit.
The resulting interval remains within [0,1].  On [0,1], the alternating
exponential series has decreasing term magnitudes.  Its odd partial sum
of degree 81 is a lower bound, and its even partial sum of degree 80 is
an upper bound.  Natural outward interval arithmetic evaluates these
polynomials, after which s outward squarings undo range reduction.
For an argument interval, the lower exponential endpoint is obtained at
the upper argument and the upper exponential endpoint at the lower one.

Very small exponentials are not rounded silently to zero.  The exact
positive Taylor inequality

  exp(7/10) > 1+7/10+(7/10)^2/2+(7/10)^3/6 > 2

gives log(2)<7/10.  Hence if u>=256*7/10 then exp(-u)<2^-256, and the
certified interval [0,1]/S is used.  The nonzero upper endpoint propagates
through every subsequent operation, including derivative remainders.

Pi is enclosed by Machin's identity with 96 terms in each arctangent
series.  An even number of terms ends at a negative term, so that partial
sum is a lower bound; adding the next positive term gives an upper bound.
The final coefficient 16 and negative coefficient -4 are combined with
the correct outward directions.

4. Taylor enclosure lemma used on every finite cell
===================================================

For a cell [a,b], let m=(a+b)/2 and h=(b-a)/2.  Define

  C_k(m)=sum_i c_i exp(-lambda_i*m)*(-lambda_i)^k/k!,

and

  B_q(a)=sum_i |c_i| exp(-lambda_i*a)*lambda_i^q/q!.

Since lambda_i>=0, Taylor's theorem gives, for |delta|<=h,

  |F(m+delta)-sum_{k=0}^p C_k(m)*delta^k|
       <= B_{p+1}(a)*|delta|^(p+1).

Indeed every intermediate point is at least a, and the magnitude of the
(p+1)-st derivative of each exponential term is bounded by its value at a
times |c_i|*lambda_i^(p+1).  Summing the absolute remainders proves the
displayed formula.

For the derivative, the corresponding expansion and remainder are

  F'(m+delta)=sum_{k=0}^p (k+1) C_{k+1}(m)*delta^k + remainder,
  |remainder| <= (p+2) B_{p+2}(a)*|delta|^(p+1).

The factor p+2 is essential: B_{p+2} has denominator (p+2)!, whereas the
Taylor remainder for F' has denominator (p+1)!.  The source includes this
factor explicitly.

The stored coefficient intervals and upper bounds for B therefore give
whole-cell value and derivative enclosures.  Point evaluations at the
cell endpoints use the same polynomial with its signed delta and rigorous
remainder.  If the derivative enclosure has one sign, endpoint monotonicity
gives a second value enclosure for the entire cell.  Intersecting two valid
enclosures is valid and improves efficiency.

The finite cover uses p=12.  Failed cells are bisected; they make no claim
about the function until their children are certified.  A maximum-depth
failure would terminate the program rather than produce a root count.
The certificate records every accepted cell, Taylor coefficient, remainder,
whole-cell range, derivative range and endpoint range.

5. The origin is treated analytically, without division by a tiny interval
========================================================================

The source recomputes the exact coefficientwise identities

  sum_i c_i=0,   sum_i r_i*c_i=0

using Fraction arithmetic on all eight basis coefficients.  Thus D(0)=0
and D'(0)=0 exactly; hence F(0)=F'(0)=0 as well.  Interval containment of
zero is not used to infer an exact identity.

Use the Taylor expansion of F at zero through degree p=24.  For 0<x<=1,
division by x^2 is justified symbolically by those two exact cancellations:

  F(x)/x^2 >= lower(C_2(0))
             -sum_{k=3}^24 upper(|C_k(0)|)-upper(B_25(0)).

The last term is valid because the undivided remainder is at most
B_25(0)*x^25, whose quotient by x^2 is at most B_25(0).  The saved exact
rational lower bound is strictly greater than 17/1000.  In particular,
C_2(0)>0 and the origin has multiplicity exactly two.  This handles every
arbitrarily small positive x, not a grid truncated away from zero.

7. The infinite tail is proved in one analytic inequality
========================================================

Differentiate F and remove the first decaying exponential:

  exp(lambda_1*x)F'(x)
    = -c1*lambda_1
      +sum_{i>=2} (-c_i*lambda_i)
                    exp[-(lambda_i-lambda_1)*x].

For c_i<0, the corresponding summand is positive and can be discarded
in a lower bound.  For c_i>0 it is negative; because lambda_i>lambda_1,
its magnitude decreases with x.  Therefore, for every x>=2048,

  exp(lambda_1*x)F'(x)
    >= -c1*lambda_1
       -sum_{i>=2,c_i>0} c_i*lambda_i
                    exp[-(lambda_i-lambda_1)*2048].

Every term on the right is enclosed outward.  The certificate's exact
rational lower bound is greater than 1/1000.  Consequently

  F'(x) > (1/1000)*exp(-lambda_1*x) > 0  (x>=2048).

The last finite negative cell proves F(2048)<0.  Since all lambda_i for
i>0 are strictly positive and the sum is finite, F(x) tends to c0>0.
Strict increase and the intermediate value theorem now give exactly one
simple root on the entire infinite interval.  No finite numerical cutoff
is being substituted for a proof at infinity.
