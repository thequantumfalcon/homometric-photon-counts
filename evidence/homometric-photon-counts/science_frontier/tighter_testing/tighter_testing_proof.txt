RIGOROUSLY TIGHTER FINITE SAMPLE COUNTS FOR THE FULL PHOTON LAW
20 October 2026

RESULT
======
For the same fixed Gaussian phase and zero-background Poisson detector point
as the preceding record,

  n=63, t=1/2, s=2 log 2, eta=1/512, b=0,
  I=|sum of 63 unit phasors|^2, 0<=I<=3969,
  N conditional on I ~ Poisson(eta I),

let m_* be the smallest number of independent snapshots for which the
optimal equal-prior A/B decision has success probability at least 19/20.
The competing models and detector parameters are known. Then

  112,606,153,000,000,001 <= m_* <= 112,608,396,000,000,000.

The width divided by the lower endpoint is less than

  0.000019918983 = 0.0019918983 percent.

The earlier certified interval was

  69,121,152,393,630,388 <= m_* <= 191,671,944,702,842,206.

The new interval is a rigorous finite-sample enclosure. It is not a normal
approximation declared exact, and it does not determine the exact optimal
integer m_*. The large observation count is unchanged in practical scale.

A concrete test with rational weights attains the new sufficient bound.
All weights and its exact threshold are in tighter_testing_certificate.json.
If every count is at most 24, it compares the weighted count sum with the
specified rational threshold. If any count exceeds 24, it chooses A.
The complete count tail and its accumulation over approximately 10^17
snapshots are included in the guarantee.

1. INPUT DEPENDENCY AND NOTATION
==============================
The calculation accepts the exact count-probability intervals and
order-32 intensity-moment table retained in:

  science_extension/finite_detection/finite_detection_certificate.json
  science_extension/finite_detection/fixed_t_moments.json

Their SHA-256 values are pinned in the source and copied into the result.
No old producer or moment engine is imported. This continuation does not
independently regenerate the high-order geometric moments. The original
record supplies their derivation and its declared independent-check scope.

Let P and Q denote the full photon-count distributions for A and B. Let
K=24 and define tail probabilities tau_G=P_G(N>K). The retained factorial
moment bound is

  tau_G <= T_G = eta^25 E_G[I^25]/25!.

This follows from 1{N>=25} <= (N)_25/25! and the conditional Poisson
factorial moment E[(N)_25 | I]=(eta I)^25. The sum T_A+T_B is reproduced
exactly from the retained moments and agrees with the old certificate.
It is less than 1.030e-26.

Define the CONDITIONAL laws on the 25-element alphabet {0,...,24}:

  R_G(k)=P_G(k)/(1-tau_G).

These are conditional laws, not censored laws with an additional tail
symbol. If l_Gk<=P_G(k)<=u_Gk, then

  l_Gk <= R_G(k) <= u_Gk/(1-T_G).

All these probabilities are strictly positive. The executable uses these
enclosures, widened by outward rational rounding.

2. EXACT TRANSFER FROM CONDITIONAL TO FULL LAWS
==============================================
The m-fold law R_G^m is precisely P_G^m conditioned on all m counts being
at most K. Consequently

  TV(P_G^m,R_G^m)=1-(1-tau_G)^m <= m*tau_G <= m*T_G.

For any decision rule f, its equal-prior success is an average of one event
probability under P^m and one under Q^m. Replacing the two laws by their
conditional counterparts changes that success by at most

  c_m = m*(T_A+T_B)/2.

Taking suprema over rules preserves the bound:

  |S_m^*(P,Q)-S_m^*(R_A,R_B)| <= c_m.

The same error applies to any fixed test, extended arbitrarily to samples
containing a count above K. The supplied explicit test chooses A in that
case. At the final two endpoints c_m is approximately 5.7941e-10.

This step is necessary: a small one-observation tail cannot simply be
discarded when the number of observations is enormous.

3. CONDITIONAL LOG-LIKELIHOOD STATISTIC
======================================
Let

  L(k)=log(R_A(k)/R_B(k)).

For equal priors, the optimal test under the conditional models chooses
A when sum_i L(N_i)>=0 and B otherwise. Choosing A on a tie causes no
loss in optimal equal-prior success.

For G=A,B define

  mu_G=E_G L,
  v_G=Var_G L,
  r_G=E_G |L-mu_G|^3,
  beta_G=r_G/v_G^(3/2).

The interval calculation establishes mu_A>0>mu_B and v_A,v_B>0.
The following are descriptive summaries; exact interval endpoints are
retained in the JSON:

  mu_A in [4.805279388961882668e-17, 4.805279506057441150e-17],
  mu_B in [-4.805266394766577191e-17,-4.805266277800851049e-17],

  v_A in [9.610585117590239684e-17,9.610585117889689947e-17],
  v_B in [9.610506450289066217e-17,9.610506450587941191e-17],

  r_A < 8.002690098663927e-22,
  r_B < 8.002372511968484e-22,

  beta_A < 849.398041208,
  beta_B < 849.374761648.

These means are about seven orders smaller than some of the individual
weighted log terms. Ordinary double-precision summation or ratio/log
evaluation would not safely determine them. All bounds here use exact
rational interval arithmetic with rigorous logarithm remainders.

4. THE EXTERNAL BERRY--ESSEEN THEOREM USED
========================================
The only non-elementary probability inequality newly invoked is
Shevtsova (2011), Theorem 1:

  sup_x |F_m(x)-Phi(x)|
      <= (208/625)*(beta_3+429/1000)/sqrt(m).

It holds for iid standardized centered variables with a finite third
absolute moment beta_3. Here each conditional alphabet is finite, its
variance is strictly positive, and observations are iid, so all hypotheses
hold separately under A and B. The paper uses left-continuous distribution
functions, which directly accommodates the tie convention. Right-continuous
versions also obey the same bound by limits and continuity of Phi.

Primary source:
Irina Shevtsova, "On the absolute constants in the Berry--Esseen type
inequalities for identically distributed summands", arXiv:1111.6554v1,
28 November 2011, Theorem 1, PDF page 3.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.6554

This is an established theorem applied here, not a new claim about a
universal Berry--Esseen constant. Its proof is not re-created by this
certificate. The finite source-to-result calculation below is re-created.

5. TWO-SIDED FINITE SUCCESS ENCLOSURES
=====================================
Put

  a_A(m)=sqrt(m)*mu_A/sqrt(v_A),
  a_B(m)=sqrt(m)*(-mu_B)/sqrt(v_B),
  e_G(m)=(208/625)*(beta_G+429/1000)/sqrt(m).

Under A the success event is sum L>=0, and under B it is sum L<0.
Applying the preceding theorem gives

  S_m^*(R_A,R_B)
    in [(Phi(a_A)+Phi(a_B))/2 -(e_A+e_B)/2,
        (Phi(a_A)+Phi(a_B))/2 +(e_A+e_B)/2].

Replacing all quantities by correctly oriented interval endpoints and
adding/subtracting c_m gives a rigorous enclosure of S_m^*(P,Q).
The Berry--Esseen errors are about 8.428e-7 per hypothesis near the final
sample threshold; they are retained, not treated as negligible.

At

  m_0=112,606,153,000,000,000,

the resulting upper bound is

  S_m0^*(P,Q) < 0.949999999536447714877 < 19/20.

Optimal success is nondecreasing in m, because a rule may ignore additional
observations. Therefore EVERY m<=m_0 is insufficient and

  m_* >= m_0+1.

The conditional likelihood calculation also certifies 95% success at
112,608,399,000,000,000 observations. The explicit rational-score rule in
the next section improves that sufficient endpoint slightly.

The source chooses endpoints by integer bisection on a grid of 10^9
observations. Every endpoint claim is checked independently of any
monotonicity of the approximate bounding expression. Monotonicity of the
actual optimal success is the only monotonicity needed for the necessary
claim about all smaller m.

6. AN EXPLICIT RATIONAL-SCORE TEST
=================================
For each retained count k, let [L_k^-,L_k^+] be its certified log-likelihood
interval. Define a fixed rational weight w_k by taking their midpoint and
rounding DOWN to the grid 10^-60. This deterministic rounding is part of
the test definition; no assertion that w_k is the exact log likelihood is
made.

The source separately bounds, for the now-fixed score w,

  nu_G=E_{R_G} w, V_G=Var_{R_G} w,
  R3_G=E_{R_G}|w-nu_G|^3.

It chooses the exact rational per-observation threshold

  theta=(lower_bound(nu_A)+upper_bound(nu_B))/2.

The certified mean intervals lie on opposite sides of theta. Given m
observations:

  - If any count exceeds 24, choose A.
  - Otherwise choose A when sum_i w_{N_i} >= m*theta, and B otherwise.

Apply the same Berry--Esseen argument to the centered fixed score, using
drifts nu_A-theta and theta-nu_B. Transfer from conditional to full laws
with the same c_m.

At

  m_1=112,608,396,000,000,000,

the certified equal-prior success lower bound is

  0.9500000002501716538329 > 19/20.

Thus m_*<=m_1. This is a constructive sufficient result, even though it
does not identify the exact finite-sample optimal test numerically.

The fixed score has less interval dependency than an interval-valued exact
log likelihood. It can therefore produce a slightly stronger CERTIFIED
sufficient endpoint, even though the exact conditional likelihood rule is
mathematically optimal for the conditional problem. There is no conflict.

The guarantee is for average success with equal priors. It does not assert
that both conditional success probabilities separately exceed 95%.

7. RIGOROUS TRANSCENDENTAL ENCLOSURES
====================================
The source uses fractions and integer arithmetic for every enclosure and
every decision. Decimal arithmetic is used only to print summaries.

Logarithm:
For x>0 put u=(x-1)/(x+1), so

  log x = 2 sum_{j>=0} u^(2j+1)/(2j+1).

After j=J the absolute remainder is at most

  2 |u|^(2J+3)/[(2J+3)(1-u^2)].

All needed |u| are below 1/2. The program stops only when this rational
remainder is below 10^-75 and then rounds outward to a 10^-70 grid.

Square roots:
For rational x>=0 and integer Q=10^70, let

  n=floor(sqrt(floor(x Q^2))).

Then n/Q <= sqrt(x) <= (n+1)/Q. Both squared inequalities are checked
exactly after each construction.

Pi:
The program bounds arctan(1/5) and arctan(1/239) by their alternating
power sums and uses Machin's identity

  pi = 16 arctan(1/5)-4 arctan(1/239).

For completeness, writing a=arctan(1/5), b=arctan(1/239), the tangent
addition formulas give tan(4a)=120/119 and tan(4a-b)=1. The angles lie
in the interval selecting 4a-b=pi/4. Alternating arctangent bounds follow
by integrating the finite geometric identity for 1/(1+x^2).

Normal CDF:
For 0<=z<=2,

  Phi(z)=1/2 + (1/sqrt(2 pi))
          sum_{j>=0} (-1)^j z^(2j+1)/(2^j j! (2j+1)).

The upper sum ends at j=32 and the lower at j=33. These inequalities follow
by integrating the global even/odd Taylor bounds for exp(-x) with
x=t^2/2 over 0<=t<=z. They do not depend on a floating-point normal-CDF
implementation. Negative arguments use Phi(-z)=1-Phi(z).

Interval moment arithmetic:
Each score interval is multiplied by its probability interval; the second
raw moment is bounded similarly. The variance is E[L^2]-(E L)^2, with
interval squaring handled correctly when an interval straddles zero.
The third centered absolute moment is bounded termwise by the probability
upper bound times the largest possible |L-mu|^3. All these enclosures may
be conservative; losing dependency can widen them but cannot invalidate
their inclusion.

8. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT CLOSE
===================================
Closed here:
  A much tighter rigorous interval for the optimal finite sample count
  at the specified previously certified full-count operating point, and
  an explicit rational-score test meeting its sufficient endpoint.

Still open:
  The exact integer m_*, the full optimal power curve at all m, all-parameter
  detector optimization, estimation of unknown detector parameters, and
  experimental feasibility.

The result assumes iid snapshots, the fixed known candidates, their stated
common Gaussian phase law, the calibrated zero-background Poisson model,
and the preserved intensity normalization. It depends on the mathematical
validity of the retained input tables and the cited external probability
theorem. It is not external peer review or formal proof-assistant checking.
