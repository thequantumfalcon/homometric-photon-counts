2. CONDITIONING AND THE FULL-COUNT TAIL
====================================
The 25-bin model is correctly defined by conditioning, not by silently
discarding mass or by adding a censored tail bin. With tau_G=P_G(N>=25),

  R_G(k)=P_G(k)/(1-tau_G),  k=0,...,24.

The factorial-moment inequality is valid pointwise for integer N:

  1{N>=25} <= (N)_25/25!.

Conditional Poisson factorial moments give

  tau_G <= T_G = E_G[I^25]/(512^25*25!).

The separate replay recomputed both T_G values from the retained moment
table and matched the old and new tail sums exactly. The sum is below
1.030e-26. Because 0<=tau_G<=T_G<1, the probability enclosures

  p_G(k)^- <= R_G(k) <= p_G(k)^+/(1-T_G)

are correctly oriented. They are not required individually to form a
normalized vector: interval moments include the one actual normalized
vector. Loss of dependence between interval entries only widens the bounds.
The replay additionally checked that the sum intervals contain 1.

For m independent observations, R_G^m is the full law P_G^m conditioned on
all observations being at most 24. Thus the exact total-variation distance is

  TV(P_G^m,R_G^m)=1-(1-tau_G)^m <= m*T_G.

For any fixed binary decision rule, equal-prior average success changes by
at most

  c_m = m*(T_A+T_B)/2.

The same bound applies to the two optimal successes: if every fixed-rule
success changes by at most c_m, taking suprema over those rules preserves
that bound. This argument also permits any extension of a finite-alphabet
rule to samples containing a tail observation. Choosing A on every such
sample is therefore a legitimate explicit rule.

The exact tail transfers at the audited endpoints are approximately

  m_0: 5.7940804788906076257e-10,
  m_1: 5.7941958910787333637e-10.

They were included in the independently recomputed success intervals. The
accumulation of tiny one-snapshot tail probabilities over roughly 10^17
snapshots is handled explicitly and with the correct factor of one half.

3. LIKELIHOOD, TIES, AND THE NECESSARY BOUND
=========================================
On the conditional alphabet, let L(k)=log(R_A(k)/R_B(k)). The equal-prior
optimal test chooses A when sum L>=0, B otherwise. On a zero likelihood-
ratio sum, the two joint probabilities agree, so choosing A is optimal.

Write mu_G=E_G L, v_G=Var_G L, r_G=E_G|L-mu_G|^3, and
beta_G=r_G/v_G^(3/2). The producer and replay rigorously establish
mu_A>0>mu_B and v_A,v_B>0. The conditional alphabet is finite and each
retained probability is positive, so all score moments exist.

Under A, success is the complement of the strict lower-tail event at
-sqrt(m)*mu_A/sqrt(v_A). Under B, success is the strict lower-tail event at
sqrt(m)*(-mu_B)/sqrt(v_B). With the left-continuous CDF convention, both
success probabilities consequently lie within e_G of Phi(a_G), where

  a_A=sqrt(m)*mu_A/sqrt(v_A),
  a_B=sqrt(m)*(-mu_B)/sqrt(v_B),
  e_G=(208/625)*(beta_G+429/1000)/sqrt(m).

No unaccounted lattice-atom or tie error is needed. A right-continuous
Berry--Esseen statement would give the same inequalities by one-sided
limits because Phi is continuous.

The producer averages the two conditional success enclosures and widens
them by c_m. This rigorously bounds optimal full-count average success.
Near the endpoints the per-hypothesis Berry--Esseen errors are about
8.428e-7; these are retained. The normal approximation is not asserted to
be exact.

At m_0=112,606,153,000,000,000, the independent replay obtains

  optimal full success <=
  0.9499999995364477148768638715162573211181

  19/20 - this upper bound =
  4.635522851231361284837426788819e-10 > 0.

The actual optimal success is nondecreasing in the sample size, since a
rule can ignore extra observations. Therefore every integer m<=m_0 is
insufficient, which proves m_*>=m_0+1. Strictness matters at precisely this
step, and was checked with rational arithmetic.

The producer selects candidate endpoints by bisection on a 10^9 grid.
It need not prove that every approximate lower/upper bounding expression
is monotone: it recomputes and verifies the selected endpoint inequality.
The necessary bound on every smaller sample size uses monotonicity of
the actual optimal success, not of a numerical approximation. The replay
does not repeat that bisection; it independently verifies its selected
endpoints directly.

4. THE EXPLICIT RATIONAL RULE AND THE SUFFICIENT BOUND
====================================================
The certificate fixes 25 rational weights w_k. Each is the midpoint of
the producer's certified log-likelihood interval, rounded down to a 10^-60
grid. The replay checks every exact weight against that definition. There
is no need to assert that any weight equals an exact logarithm.

The rational threshold is also part of the fixed test definition:

  theta=(saved lower bound for E_A w + saved upper bound for E_B w)/2.

The replay checks this identity and independently recalculates score
moments using the exact saved weights and the independently reconstructed
conditional probability intervals. The newly obtained means satisfy

  lower(E_A w) > theta > upper(E_B w).

The test chooses A if any observation exceeds 24. Otherwise it chooses A
when sum_i w_{N_i}>=m*theta, and B otherwise. For this fixed rule the same
Berry--Esseen argument applies with drifts E_A w-theta and theta-E_B w.
The full-law lower bound follows after subtracting c_m.

At m_1=112,608,396,000,000,000, the independent replay obtains

  explicit-rule full average success >=
  0.9500000002501716538329450940360010574721

  this lower bound - 19/20 =
  2.501716538329450940360010574721e-10 > 0.

An explicit rule achieving this success establishes the same sufficient
bound for optimal success. This result is constructive; it does not claim
that the rational rule is exactly optimal at finite sample size.

The conditional-likelihood rule also has an independently verified
sufficient point at 112,608,399,000,000,000, with full optimal success lower
bound 0.9500000004439620539356800052670440986014. The rational rule's slightly
smaller certified sufficient point is consistent with conditional
likelihood optimality. The rational score's interval calculation loses
less dependence; a tighter certificate for a particular rule can exceed
a looser lower certificate for the optimal rule.

The guarantee concerns the average of the A and B success probabilities.
For example, the independently certified A-side conditional lower bound
for the rational rule is approximately 0.9499996537, while the B-side lower
bound is approximately 0.9500003480. These individual lower bounds do not
prove that both hypotheses separately attain 95%. The proof and result
correctly avoid that stronger claim.

5. AUDIT OF THE PRODUCER'S INTERVAL ARITHMETIC
===========================================
The code uses fractions and integer arithmetic for all decisions.
Decimal rendering is descriptive only. The following operations were
checked both structurally and against the independent replay:

  - Positive probability intervals are divided with the correct endpoint
    orientation. Conditional probabilities are rounded outward.

  - Logarithms use 2*atanh((x-1)/(x+1)) with a valid absolute geometric
    remainder. The series stopping test is rational. Outward rounding
    preserves the enclosure.

  - Integer square-root bounds enclose sqrt(x) because the square of the
    lower endpoint is at most x and that of the upper endpoint is at least
    x. Both inequalities are checked exactly.

  - Machin's pi identity and alternating arctangent remainders correctly
    enclose pi; reciprocals and square roots correctly enclose 1/sqrt(2pi).

  - The normal-CDF lower and upper sums follow by integrating the global
    odd/even Taylor bounds for exp(-t^2/2). Their validity does not require
    an unsupported assumption that all initial alternating terms decrease.
    Negative arguments use exact symmetry. Argument intervals use CDF
    monotonicity with the correct endpoint orientation.

  - Mean and second raw moments use interval products. Variance is raw
    second moment minus the square of the mean interval, with zero-crossing
    intervals squared correctly. The computed variance lower bounds are
    positive.

  - For the third absolute centered moment, each probability upper endpoint
    multiplies the largest possible absolute centered score cubed. Dividing
    its upper bound by a lower bound on v^(3/2) gives a valid beta upper
    bound. All relevant signs are known.

  - Standardized drifts divide positive quantities by positive standard
    deviations. Berry--Esseen errors use an upper numerator and lower
    sqrt(m). Success intervals and tail widening are correctly oriented.

The likelihood means are about 4.805e-17, obtained by cancellation of
larger signed terms. Using ordinary floating-point cancellation as the
decision criterion would be insufficient. Both implementations avoid it.

6. HOW THE SEPARATE REPLAY DIFFERS
================================
The separate source is independent_tighter_testing_replay.py. It does not
import or execute certify_tighter_testing.py. It uses the same mathematical
input probability intervals, but applies distinct transcendental enclosure
formulas and finer interval-moment grids:

Logarithms:
For x>=1, use the alternating series for log(1+u), u=x-1. For x<1, use
log(x)=-log(1/x). Every required u lies in (0,1/2). Successive alternating
partial sums enclose the answer; the next term must be below 10^-100.
Results are rounded outward to a 10^-90 grid.

Square roots:
Use an integer square-root enclosure on the binary grid 2^340, rather
than the producer's decimal grid. Verify the two square inequalities.

Pi:
Use pi=8*atan(1/3)+4*atan(1/7), with 100-term alternating bounds. The tangent
of 2*atan(1/3) is 3/4; adding atan(1/7) gives tangent 1 in the correct
quadrant. This differs from the producer's Machin identity.

Normal CDF:
Use the positive-series representation, for z>=0,

  Phi(z)=1/2 + exp(-z^2/2)/sqrt(2pi)
                 * sum_{k>=0} z^(2k+1)/(2k+1)!!.

This follows by solving R'=1+zR, R(0)=0, for
R(z)=exp(z^2/2)*integral_0^z exp(-t^2/2)dt. Retain k=0,...,60. The positive
tail is bounded by the next term divided by 1-z^2/125, because subsequent
term ratios decrease. Bound exp(z^2/2) using its positive Taylor series
through degree 80 and the geometric remainder with ratio at most
(z^2/2)/82. Invert the positive exponential enclosure for exp(-z^2/2).
All arguments lie in [-2,2]. Round CDF arguments and output outward to a
10^-40 grid, still far finer than the approximately 10^-10 decisive margins.

The replay checks that its independently recomputed probability, log,
mean, variance, standard-deviation, and normalizing-constant intervals lie
inside the producer's saved intervals, and that its upper bounds on third
moments and Berry--Esseen numerators are no greater than the saved ones.
For each of the three audited endpoints it also checks inclusion of the
standardized-drift, normal-CDF, and final full-success intervals and exact
agreement of the tail-transfer quantity. It recomputes the final endpoint
inequalities using its different CDF method. The output is saved in
independent_tighter_testing_replay.json.
