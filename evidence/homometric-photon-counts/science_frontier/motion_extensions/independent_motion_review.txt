INDEPENDENT INTERNAL REVIEW OF MOTION AND GEOMETRY EXTENSIONS
20 October 2026

Verdict: PASS for the stated model-level claims and sufficient tolerances.
This is a separate reasoning and arithmetic review within this session,
not external human peer review or physical validation.

Reviewed material: motion_extension_proofs.txt, verify_motion_extensions.py,
and motion_extension_results.json. The independent_motion_replay.json file
records the exact reviewed input hashes and independently replayed checks.
The replay imports neither the producer nor the foundation moment engine;
it evaluates the retained foundation polynomial table directly. It therefore
shares that previously verified table as an explicit dependency.

1. Common-mode invariance is pathwise: multiplying all complex summands by
one unit factor preserves their squared norm. The factor may depend on the
residual vector. Subtracting the sample mean of a real phase vector removes
only such a common factor, so the Gaussian projection statement follows
without an independence assertion between mean and contrasts.

2. With centered phases, or more generally P mu=0, the covariance eigenvalues give the exact equicorrelation domain
[-1/62,1]. For every admissible rho, PCP=s(1-rho)P, agreeing with the
contrast law of independent Gaussian phases at variance s(1-rho). This
includes negative rho and the singular lower endpoint. The rho=1 case
collapses to zero intensity at the clean null. Equal projected parameters
are sufficient for equal laws; the note correctly avoids claiming their
necessity or separate identification of s and rho. The centered/projected-mean
qualification was made explicit after the final report review; arbitrary
nonzero P mu would change the effective geometry and is not included in
the iid-equivalence statement.

3. The mean formulas follow directly from expanding the intensity. The
heterogeneous two-site example uses the same indexed perturbation in both
geometries, so it is a genuine failure of a blanket robustness claim for
lower-moment equality. Elementary sixth-root arithmetic independently gives
pair norms 3 and 1, means 749/16 and 747/16, and gap 2d^2. Geometry rotation
instead gives a clean amplitude factor exp(i epsilon)-1 and mean gap
8 exp(-s) sin^2(epsilon/2). At epsilon=pi/3, s=log2, the values are 33 and 32.

4. The L8 argument bounds the difference of E|F|^8 by the difference of
eighth powers of amplitude norms. Minkowski supplies the norm difference;
convexity bounds either an increase or a decrease by (R+d)^8-R^8. Thus
subtracting one such loss per geometry is valid even for different or
dependent couplings. Independently evaluating the retained reference
polynomials and expanding the increment by the binomial theorem gives
the reported margin 202.724168424011...>202.

5. For general Gaussian covariance, C_perp and P commute and share the
one-dimensional null common-mode subspace. On the contrast subspace P is
the identity times one, so the square-root Frobenius bound reduces to
the scalar inequality |sqrt(lambda)-sqrt(s0)|<=|lambda-s0|/sqrt(s0).
This remains valid at singular contrast covariance. The row standard
deviations sum to at most sqrt(63)||D||_F. Combining the Gaussian L8 factor
105^(1/8)<2 with sqrt(63/log2)<10 justifies the constant 20. Large common
covariance cancels exactly and need not be small.

6. The deterministic, standard-deviation, variance, and covariance bounds
each consume the same 1/200000 amplitude budget. They are alternative
sufficient bounds; the proof correctly supplies a combined inequality
when several perturbations coexist. A coordinate tolerance is expressed
only in the mathematical units of q0=pi/3, not an asserted physical length.

7. For f in [0,M], the layer-cake bound is M*TV, not 2M*TV under the
stated convention. Applying it to I^4 in [0,63^8] and then to product
measures gives the claimed exact joint and per-site margins. Close
marginals alone would not suffice; the rigid-common-motion example
correctly exposes that failure.

Limits retained: these are single-snapshot statements. Arbitrary temporal
dependence does not inherit the iid sample guarantees. Perturbations can
destroy exact lower-moment equality while preserving a positive fourth
gap. The margins are conservative sufficient conditions, not optimal
physical tolerances. No unqualified extension to all motion laws is claimed.
