DEPENDENT MOTION, UNEQUAL SITE NOISE, AND GEOMETRY ERRORS
20 October 2026

Scope
-----
These are extensions of the fixed 63-site, unit-weight homometric construction
at q0=pi/3. They concern the intensity law in one snapshot. The extensions to
dependence below concern dependence BETWEEN SITES inside that snapshot.
Arbitrary residual dependence BETWEEN SNAPSHOTS is not covered by the earlier
independent-sample testing rates.

Write z_Gj=exp(i q0 x_Gj), F_G=sum_j z_Gj exp(i theta_j), I_G=|F_G|^2,
G=A,B, and n=63. The supplied exact construction satisfies sum_j z_Gj=0.
The reference phase law in the robustness results is iid N(0,log 2) before
wrapping. Intensities are unnormalized: 0<=I_G<=n^2=3969.

All original proof sources and certificates remain unchanged. The accompanying
script rebuilds the required reference moments with the foundation's exact
cumulant engine, checks the two explicit examples directly in its exact
number field, and certifies the numerical margins with rational arithmetic.
This is model-level mathematical work, not experimental validation or a claim
that the sufficient tolerances below are maximal.

1. EXACT INVARIANCE UNDER A COMMON RANDOM PHASE
---------------------------------------------
Let U_1,...,U_n be any jointly distributed unit-circle variables. Let V be any
unit-circle random variable, including one dependent on all the U_j. Put
W_j=V U_j. Then on every realization,

  |sum_j z_j W_j|^2 = |V|^2 |sum_j z_j U_j|^2
                   = |sum_j z_j U_j|^2.                       (M1)

Thus an arbitrary common random phase is exactly invisible to every intensity
statistic. No independence, Gaussianity, or finite unwrapped phase variance
of V is required. A common deterministic translation of the geometry is
likewise invisible at the fixed frequency.

In particular, taking all W_j=V gives I_G=|sum_j z_Gj|^2=0 at the common
null, for BOTH geometries, even if V itself has the Gaussian wrapped marginal
law used in the reference iid experiment. Equal single-site marginals alone
therefore cannot replace the site-independence assumption.

This extends the foundation's already recorded observation that subtracting
the average iid displacement does not change intensity. The common-factor
argument itself is elementary and is not claimed as a historically new
general principle.

2. A COMPLETE GAUSSIAN DEPENDENCE CLASS: PROJECTED COVARIANCE
----------------------------------------------------------
Let 1 denote the n-vector of ones and define

  P=Id-11^T/n.

For every real phase vector theta,

  theta=P theta + (1^T theta/n)1.

Equation (M1) shows that its intensity equals that of P theta, sample by
sample. If theta is Gaussian with mean mu and positive semidefinite
covariance C, then P theta is Gaussian with mean P mu and covariance P C P.
It follows that:

THEOREM M1. For fixed geometry, the pair (P mu,P C P) determines the entire
Gaussian-phase intensity distribution. Two Gaussian phase models with equal
projected mean and projected covariance have identical intensity laws.

This is a sufficient equivalence condition; it does NOT say that intensity
identifies every entry of P C P. Additional geometric symmetries can create
further equivalences.

Adding tau 11^T to C leaves P C P unchanged whenever the resulting matrix
is a valid covariance matrix. More generally, a difference of the form
1a^T+a1^T is annihilated by P on both sides. The argument needs only validity
of the two covariance matrices, not a decomposition into independent common
noise with a nonnegative coefficient.

Equicorrelation, including negative correlations
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
For centered phases, or more generally P mu=0, consider

  C=s[(1-rho)Id+rho 11^T],      s>0.

Its eigenvalues are s(1-rho), with multiplicity n-1, and
s[1+(n-1)rho], with multiplicity one. It is positive semidefinite exactly
when

  -1/(n-1) <= rho <= 1.

Since P C P=s(1-rho)P, Theorem M1 compares this model to independent
Gaussian phases with variance

  s_eff=s(1-rho).                                           (M2)

COROLLARY M1. For every -1/62<=rho<1 and P mu=0, the full intensity law of the
equicorrelated 63-site model is exactly the iid Gaussian intensity law at
variance s_eff. Consequently every foundation identity, every phase-family
intensity conclusion appropriate to Gaussian motion, and every subsequent
fixed-detector distribution identity carries over after replacing s by
s_eff. This includes first-three-moment equality and the strict fourth gap
for every s_eff>0, not merely the mean.

The rho=1 endpoint is rigid common motion and gives I=0 at the exact common
null. At rho=-1/62 the phase vector has deterministic zero sum, yet the
intensity law still equals the iid law with variance 63s/62. Thus a real
"independent residual plus common Gaussian" construction is not required;
the projection proof handles the whole admissible negative-correlation
range as well.

IDENTIFIABILITY LIMIT. Under this enlarged centered-phase model, s and rho are not
separately identifiable from intensity: any two admissible pairs with the
same s(1-rho) produce the same full law. An earlier identification statement
for a single iid phase variance identifies s_eff here, not both parameters.

3. EXACT MEAN FORMULAS AND FAILURE OF LOWER-MOMENT CANCELLATION
------------------------------------------------------------
For a general jointly Gaussian phase vector, the characteristic function
of theta_j-theta_k gives

  E I_G =
    sum_{j,k} z_Gj conjugate(z_Gk)
      exp(i(mu_j-mu_k))
      exp[-(C_jj+C_kk-2C_jk)/2].                            (M3)

This follows by expanding |F_G|^2 and taking the expectation of each
exp[i(theta_j-theta_k)]. It remains valid for singular Gaussian covariance
matrices. Clean homometry alone does not require equality of this
pair-weighted sum when the phase-difference variances vary by indexed pair.

For independent, possibly nonidentical unit-circle phases, put
m_j=E W_j. Directly separating diagonal and off-diagonal terms gives

  E I_G = sum_j (1-|m_j|^2) + |sum_j z_Gj m_j|^2.            (M4)

Here m_j can be complex. Both models may use exactly the same indexed array
of site means without sharing the same unweighted null cancellation.

An exact two-site heterogeneity counterexample
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Use the natural site labels induced by U+V_G in the foundation construction.
The last site of U in the first block is label 21, at coordinate 30 in both
structures. The corresponding site in the second block is label 42:
coordinate 61 in A and 62 in B. Their fixed-frequency phases satisfy

  z_A,21=z_B,21=1,
  Re(z_A,21 conjugate(z_A,42))=1/2,
  Re(z_B,21 conjugate(z_B,42))=-1/2,

and hence

  |z_A,21+z_A,42|^2=3,       |z_B,21+z_B,42|^2=1.          (M5)

Let every site's phase mean initially be a common m, and change the means
at labels 21 and 42 to m+d, identically for A and B. Take real
0<m,m+d<1, so that independent centered Gaussian phases realize these
means using site variances -2 log(m_j).

Because each unweighted structural sum vanishes,

  sum_j z_Gj m_j = d(z_G,21+z_G,42).

The first term in (M4) agrees between models, and (M5) therefore gives

  E I_A-E I_B = 2d^2 >0       when d!=0.                    (M6)

This effect occurs for arbitrarily small nonzero d. Thus the first-three
moment cancellation is not an open property under unrestricted sitewise
heterogeneity, even if the same indexed heterogeneity is applied to both
structures.

For the exact example m=1/2,d=1/4, the 61 unchanged sites have phase
variance 2 log 2 and the two changed sites have variance -2 log(3/4).
The means are

  E I_A=749/16,       E I_B=747/16,       difference=1/8.

An exact geometry-error counterexample
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Keep a common iid centered Gaussian phase law, with t^2=exp(-s), but rotate
the two geometry phases at labels 21 and 42 by the same exp(i epsilon)
in each structure. These are position perturbations epsilon/q0 at those
two indexed sites.

Since the old structural sum is zero, the new clean amplitude is

  F_G,clean'=(exp(i epsilon)-1)(z_G,21+z_G,42).

The iid mean formula then yields

  E I_A'-E I_B'
    = 8 exp(-s) sin^2(epsilon/2).                          (M7)

This is strictly positive for every epsilon not equal to an integer
multiple of 2pi, including arbitrarily small errors. At s=log 2 and
epsilon=pi/3 the means are exactly 33 and 32.

Equations (M6)-(M7) distinguish two questions: exact equality of the lower
moments can fail under arbitrarily small heterogeneity or geometry errors,
while DISTINCTNESS of the full laws can nevertheless be robust. The next
theorem proves the latter with an explicit margin.

4. A GENERAL COUPLING BOUND FOR FOURTH-MOMENT ROBUSTNESS
------------------------------------------------------
For a complex random amplitude F, write ||F||_8=(E|F|^8)^(1/8).
Let F' be a perturbed amplitude on a coupling with the reference amplitude,
and suppose

  ||F'-F||_8 <= d,       ||F||_8 <= R.

The L8 triangle inequality gives | ||F'||_8-||F||_8 |<=d.
For nonnegative a,b with |a-b|<=d, convexity and monotonicity of x^8 give

  |b^8-a^8| <= (a+d)^8-a^8 <= (R+d)^8-R^8

when a<=R. For b<a the decrement over an interval below a is at most the
increment over an interval of the same length above a. Thus

  |E(I')^4-E I^4| <= (R+d)^8-R^8.                          (M8)

This argument does not require independence between the perturbation and
the original phases, or independence across perturbed sites.

Suppose the two reference structures have fourth gap Delta0>0 and satisfy
the same radius R. If their perturbations admit possibly different couplings
with budgets d_A,d_B, then

  Delta4' >= Delta0
             -[(R+d_A)^8-R^8]
             -[(R+d_B)^8-R^8].                            (M9)

This gives a sufficient condition for distinct perturbed intensity laws,
even when lower moments no longer match.

Exact reference constants
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
At s0=log 2 the exact reference moments, rebuilt by the accompanying script,
are

  E I_A^4 = 933840736464644496547/37748736000000,
  E I_B^4 = 933824172434893661347/37748736000000,
  Delta0  = 35946245119/81920000.

Both fourth moments are strictly below (42/5)^8. Set

  R=42/5,       d_A,d_B <= 1/200000.                       (M10)

Then rational arithmetic gives the universal sufficient margin

  Delta4' >= Delta0 - 2[(42/5+1/200000)^8-(42/5)^8]
          = 259486935582734347769603011268528972786559999
             /1280000000000000000000000000000000000000000
          = 202.72416842401120919500235255353825998949999921875
          > 202.                                         (M11)

The exact fraction, not a floating-point approximation, certifies the sign.

5. EXPLICIT CONSEQUENCES OF THE COUPLING BUDGET
---------------------------------------------
Each of the following conditions can be checked separately for each
structure. Applying one condition to A and another to B is also permitted
if both satisfy the same budget (M10).

5a. Deterministic geometry errors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Let z_j'=z_j exp(i epsilon_j) and use the same phase vector in the coupling.
Then

  |F'-F| <= sum_j |exp(i epsilon_j)-1|
           <= sum_j |epsilon_j|.

Consequently sum_j |epsilon_j|<=1/200000 is sufficient for (M11).
For a uniform per-site bound it suffices that

  |epsilon_j| <= 1/12600000   radians, for all 63 sites.    (M12)

With q0=pi/3, the corresponding coordinate error is

  |delta x_j| <= 1/(4200000 pi).

These are bounds in the mathematical coordinate system, not a calibrated
physical length tolerance. Any common phase rotation can first be removed
using (M1); a rigid translation of all sites need not consume this budget.

5b. Unequal independent Gaussian site variances
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Assume centered phases for these variance-only conditions. Couple reference
theta_j=sqrt(s0) Z_j and perturbed theta'_j=sqrt(s_j) Z_j
using independent standard normal Z_j. The elementary inequality
|exp(iu)-exp(iv)|<=|u-v| and Minkowski's inequality give

  ||F'-F||_8 <= (E Z^8)^(1/8)
                 sum_j |sqrt(s_j)-sqrt(s0)|
              < 2 sum_j |sqrt(s_j)-sqrt(s0)|,              (M13)

because E Z^8=105<2^8. Thus it suffices that

  |sqrt(s_j)-sqrt(log 2)| <= 1/25200000

uniformly. A sufficient bound directly on the variances is

  |s_j-log 2| <= 1/31500000,       s_j>=0.                 (M14)

Indeed, |sqrt(s_j)-sqrt(s0)|<=|s_j-s0|/sqrt(s0), and
log 2>2/3>16/25 implies 1/sqrt(s0)<5/4. The resulting budget is
(5/2)*63/31500000=1/200000 exactly.

The arrays s_j may differ between A and B. These sufficient conditions
preserve fourth-moment distinction, not exact equality of lower moments.

5c. General correlated Gaussian motion, including mean and geometry errors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
For a target Gaussian phase model (mu,C), define C_perp=P C P and let
B=sqrt(C_perp) be its positive semidefinite square root. Couple the
projected target and reference vectors as

  theta_target = P mu+B Z,
  theta_reference = sqrt(s0) P Z,

where Z has n independent standard normal entries. These projected vectors
have the correct intensity laws by Theorem M1. Write

  D=B-sqrt(s0)P.

The j-th row of DZ is a centered normal variable with standard deviation
||D_j,*||_2. With deterministic geometry phase errors epsilon_j,

  ||F'-F||_8
    <= sum_j |epsilon_j+(P mu)_j|
       +105^(1/8) sum_j ||D_j,*||_2
    <= sum_j |epsilon_j+(P mu)_j|
       +2 sqrt(n) ||D||_F.                                (M15)

Here ||.||_F is the Frobenius matrix norm. The matrices C_perp and P commute,
because C_perp has zero common-mode row/column component. On the n-1
dimensional contrast subspace, sqrt(s0)P is a scalar. Diagonalizing there
and using

  |sqrt(lambda)-sqrt(s0)|
     =|lambda-s0|/(sqrt(lambda)+sqrt(s0))
     <=|lambda-s0|/sqrt(s0)

gives

  ||D||_F <= ||C_perp-s0 P||_F/sqrt(s0).                   (M16)

This remains valid if C_perp is singular. Since
sqrt(63/log 2)<10, equations (M15)-(M16) imply the convenient sufficient
budget

  sum_j |epsilon_j+(P mu)_j|
     +20 ||P C P-(log 2)P||_F <= 1/200000.                (M17)

Under (M17), separately for A and B, the fourth gap satisfies (M11).
With exact geometry and zero projected mean, a sufficient covariance
tolerance is therefore

  ||P C P-(log 2)P||_F <= 1/4000000.                      (M18)

Arbitrarily large common-mode covariance is allowed because it is projected
out. The condition controls the part of the covariance that affects relative
phases.

The geometry-only, variance-only and covariance-only rows are alternative
sufficient tolerances. They must not be treated as independently available
budgets and then added without checking the combined inequality.

6. ROBUSTNESS FOR ARBITRARY NEARBY JOINT OR NONIDENTICAL PHASE LAWS
---------------------------------------------------------------
The L8 coupling results have an alternative that does not require Gaussian
perturbations. Let Q0 be the full iid Gaussian phase-vector law at s0=log 2.
For fixed exact geometry, the function I_G^4 lies in [0,n^8].

For the convention TV(P,Q)=sup_E |P(E)-Q(E)|, a function f in [0,M]
satisfies |E_P f-E_Q f|<=M TV(P,Q). This follows from the layer-cake formula
E f=integral_0^M P(f>u)du.

If arbitrary perturbed joint phase laws Q_A,Q_B satisfy

  TV(Q_G,Q0)<=epsilon_G,

then

  Delta4' >= Delta0-n^8(epsilon_A+epsilon_B).              (M19)

No independence within either perturbed phase vector is required. If both
epsilon_G<=1/10^13, the exact lower margin is

  62266525029486203/160000000000000
  = 389.16578143428876875 >389.                            (M20)

For independent but nonidentical site laws nu_G,j, telescoping the product
measures gives

  TV(product_j nu_G,j, product_j mu0)
      <= sum_j TV(nu_G,j,mu0).

If every one of the 63 site laws in both structures has single-site
TV distance at most 1/10^14 from the reference phase law, then

  Delta4' >= Delta0-2 n^9/10^14
          = 100896523480573207/800000000000000
          = 126.12065435071650875 >126.                    (M21)

These broad tolerances are intentionally conservative; they are not claims
about the greatest admissible perturbations. In (M19)-(M21) the geometry
remains exact. The independent product result does not follow from close
marginals without an independence or joint-distance condition: rigid common
motion in Section 1 is an explicit counterexample.

7. WHAT THIS CLOSES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
---------------------------------------
Completed mathematical extensions:

* An exact pathwise common-mode invariance, with arbitrary dependence of
  the common phase on the residual phase vector.
* A full-law equivalence theorem for Gaussian projected means/covariances,
  and the entire admissible equicorrelation family, including negative rho.
* An exact nonidentifiability result for separate s and rho.
* Explicit arbitrarily small site-noise and geometry perturbations that
  destroy exact equality of the first moments.
* Quantitative positive fourth-gap guarantees for deterministic geometry
  errors, unequal Gaussian site variances, general Gaussian contrast
  covariance/mean perturbations, and arbitrary nearby joint phase laws.

The broad phrase "all dependent motion" is not solved by these theorems:
arbitrary dependence can erase the distinction exactly. Nor do these
single-snapshot results provide arbitrary temporal-dependence sample bounds.
All sample-complexity transfers from the foundation still require their
original independent-observation assumptions, applied to the effective
residual process. External physical calibration and experimental validation
are not supplied here.
