GLOBAL DETECTOR OPTIMIZATION AND EXISTENCE OF AN INTERIOR NOISE OPTIMUM
2026-10-20

Scope and status
---------------
These are mathematical results for the existing two 63-site geometries under
independent, identically distributed Gaussian phase motion and the stated
Poisson observation model. They are not experimental validation, a proof of
scientific priority, or a numerical determination of the best phase variance.
All new arguments are given below. Existing exact geometric moment identities
are inputs and remain in their original files.

1. Model, conventions, and inputs
--------------------------------
Let G be A or B. Write z_Gj for its 63 unit phasors and

  I_G(s) = |sum_j z_Gj exp(i sqrt(s) Z_j)|^2,
  Z_j independent N(0,1),  0 < s < infinity.

Then 0 <= I_G <= M = 3969. Put t = exp(-s/2). The previously established
geometric identities needed here are

  sum_j z_Gj = 0,
  |sum_j z_Gj^2|^2 = 129187/400, for both G,

and the first three intensity moments agree while

  D4(s) = E_A[I^4] - E_B[I^4]
        = (455015761/20000) t^8 (1-t^2)^5
          (t^6 + 3t^4 + 6t^2 + 6) > 0.

The detector is N | I ~ Poisson(b + eta I), with eta > 0 and b >= 0.
Write P_G(s) for the latent intensity law and Q_G(s,eta,b) for the count law.

Our distance conventions are

  TV(P,Q) = (1/2) integral |p-q| dmu,
  h^2(P,Q) = integral (sqrt(p)-sqrt(q))^2 dmu
           = 2 - 2 rho(P,Q),
  rho(P,Q) = integral sqrt(p q) dmu,

where mu dominates both laws. Thus 0 <= TV <= 1 and 0 <= h^2 <= 2.

Both count laws have strictly positive mass at every nonnegative integer.
Indeed, the intensity is a nonconstant real analytic function of the phases,
so its zero set has phase probability zero. Hence b+eta I > 0 almost surely.
The two count laws are distinct: their fourth falling-factorial moments differ
by eta^4 D4(s), since E[(N)_4] = E[(b+eta I)^4] and the lower intensity
moments agree.

2. Strict ordering of all finite gains and backgrounds
----------------------------------------------------
THEOREM 1. At each fixed 0 < s < infinity, both TV and h^2 of the full
count distributions strictly increase with eta and strictly decrease with b.

PROOF, GAIN. Fix 0 < eta1 < eta2 and set r = eta1/eta2. Starting with a
count at gain eta2 and background b, independently retain each count with
probability r, then add an independent Poisson((1-r)b) count. Conditional
on I, the result is Poisson(b+eta1 I). This is the same Markov kernel K
under both structures.

Let p_j and q_j be the high-gain count probabilities and Delta_j=p_j-q_j.
For TV, summing the triangle inequality

  |sum_j K(k|j) Delta_j| <= sum_j K(k|j) |Delta_j|

proves contraction. There is at least one positive and one negative Delta_j,
because the laws are distinct probability laws. Furthermore,

  K(0|j) = exp(-(1-r)b) (1-r)^j > 0

for every finite j. Therefore the inequality for output k=0 is strict.
Summing over outputs gives strict TV contraction.

For Hellinger affinity, Cauchy-Schwarz gives, at each output k,

  sqrt[(sum_j K(k|j)p_j)(sum_j K(k|j)q_j)]
      >= sum_j K(k|j) sqrt(p_j q_j).

At k=0, every input has positive weight. Equality would force p_j/q_j to
be constant for all j. Positivity and normalization would then imply p=q,
contrary to the fourth factorial moment. Affinity therefore strictly
increases, which means h^2 strictly decreases.

PROOF, BACKGROUND. For b2>b1, add an independent Poisson(b2-b1) count.
The resulting kernel has K(k|j)>0 whenever k>=j. Choose one index with
Delta_j>0 and one with Delta_j<0, then choose a finite k at least as large
as both. The TV triangle inequality is strict at that output. For affinity,
choose two indices where p_j/q_j differ, and again an output at least as
large as both. Cauchy-Schwarz is strict there. This proves the theorem.

This is a direct, strict data-processing proof. The general statistical
comparison via a common random transformation is usually called Blackwell
ordering; no external theorem about strictness is being assumed here.

CONSEQUENCES.

(a) If eta is constrained to (0,eta_max] and b to [b_min,infinity), with
    eta_max finite, the maximizer in these two detector coordinates is
    eta=eta_max, b=b_min, for either distance and every fixed s.

(b) If gain is unrestricted, no finite gain maximizes either distance,
    even if s and b are also allowed to vary.

(c) At every fixed finite gain, b=0 is the maximizing allowed background.
    All fixed finite backgrounds nevertheless have the same infinite-gain
    limiting separation.

These statements concern separation per observed count snapshot. They do
not optimize acquisition time, total dose, saturation, detector cost, or any
other penalty, because such constraints are not present in this model.

3. The infinite-gain supremum is exactly the latent separation
-------------------------------------------------------------
THEOREM 2. At fixed s and fixed finite b,

  lim_(eta->infinity) TV(Q_A,Q_B) = TV(P_A,P_B),
  lim_(eta->infinity) h^2(Q_A,Q_B) = h^2(P_A,P_B).

PROOF. For either structure, construct I and N conditionally as in the model.
Then

  E[(N/eta-I)^2]
    = E[I]/eta + b/eta^2 + b^2/eta^2 -> 0.

Consequently the law of N/eta converges weakly to the intensity law. The
same holds for the other structure. Scaling the integer count support by
1/eta is one-to-one and does not change either distance between the two
count laws. Joint lower semicontinuity of TV and h^2 under weak convergence
therefore gives

  D(P_A,P_B) <= liminf D(Q_A,Q_B),  D in {TV,h^2}.

On the other hand, the count laws are obtained from the intensity laws
through a common Poisson kernel, so data processing gives

  D(Q_A,Q_B) <= D(P_A,P_B).

The two bounds prove the claimed limit.

For completeness, the lower-semicontinuity fact follows from the variational
representations, on a Polish space,

  TV(P,Q) = sup_{f continuous, 0<=f<=1} |integral f d(P-Q)|,

  h^2(P,Q) =
    sup_{f continuous bounded, inf f>0}
      [2 - integral f dP - integral (1/f) dQ].

For the second identity, the pointwise arithmetic-geometric mean inequality
gives the upper bound. Approximate sqrt(q/p) by its positive bounded
truncations and then by positive continuous functions in L1(P+Q), with
their lower and upper bounds preserved, to obtain equality. The first
identity follows similarly by approximating a Hahn-set indicator using
regularity of finite Borel measures. Each expression inside either
supremum is continuous under joint weak convergence; a supremum of
continuous functions is lower semicontinuous.

WARNING ABOUT THE MODE OF CONVERGENCE. Each N/eta law is discrete; the
intensity law here is absolutely continuous. Their individual TV distance
is therefore one at every finite eta. The proof uses weak convergence of
each individual law and lower semicontinuity of the distance BETWEEN THE
TWO COMPETING LAWS. It does not assert TV convergence of a discrete law
to a continuous law.

4. A total-variation delta lemma at a full-rank linear limit
----------------------------------------------------------
LEMMA. Suppose Z in R^n has a smooth probability density. Suppose
f_epsilon:R^n->R^d converges in C^1 on every compact set to a linear map
L of rank d as epsilon->0. Then

  TV(law(f_epsilon(Z)), law(LZ)) -> 0.

PROOF. Choose invertible linear coordinates Z=S(u,v), with u in R^d,
such that L S(u,v)=u. The transformed density g(u,v) is smooth and
integrable. It can be approximated in L1 arbitrarily well by a smooth
compactly supported nonnegative subdensity g_R.

Choose a smooth compactly supported cutoff chi which equals one on a
neighborhood of the support of g_R. Set

  T_epsilon(u,v)
    = (u + chi(u,v)[f_epsilon(S(u,v))-u], v).

The perturbation T_epsilon-identity is compactly supported and has
derivative converging uniformly to zero. For sufficiently small epsilon,
it has Lipschitz constant less than one. Thus T_epsilon is a global
C^1 diffeomorphism: injectivity follows from the Lipschitz inequality,
and, for each target y, the equation x=y-(T_epsilon(x)-x) has a unique
solution by the contraction theorem. The inverse derivative exists
because the derivative perturbation has norm less than one.

The image of g_R under T_epsilon has density

  g_R(T_epsilon^{-1}(y)) /
  |det DT_epsilon(T_epsilon^{-1}(y))|.

This converges in L1 to g_R. In fact all supports lie in one compact
set, inverse maps converge uniformly to the identity, and Jacobians
converge uniformly to one. Project onto the u coordinate; projection
cannot increase TV. On the support of g_R this projection is exactly
f_epsilon(S(u,v)). Finally, pushforward cannot increase the L1 error
between g and g_R. Let that error tend to zero. This proves the lemma.

5. Both latent endpoints vanish, with a rigorous small-noise argument
-------------------------------------------------------------------
THEOREM 3. For D either TV or h^2, let

  d_D(s) = D(P_A(s), P_B(s)).

Then d_D is continuous and strictly positive on (0,infinity), and

  lim_(s->0) d_D(s) = lim_(s->infinity) d_D(s) = 0.

PROOF OF POSITIVITY. D4(s)>0, so the two bounded intensity laws differ.
Both distances are zero exactly when the laws agree.

PROOF OF CONTINUITY FOR s>0. On the circle, a Gaussian phase modulo
2 pi has density

  w_s(theta) = (1/(2 pi))
               [1 + 2 sum_(k>=1) exp(-k^2 s/2) cos(k theta)].

On every compact positive s interval the series converges uniformly.
Hence w_s varies continuously in L1, as does its 63-fold product density.
Each geometry's fixed measurable map from phases to intensity contracts
TV. The triangle inequality gives continuity of the TV distance between
the two intensity laws. For Hellinger, h=sqrt(h^2) is a metric and
h^2 <= 2 TV, which gives the corresponding continuity.

PROOF AT s->infinity. The phase density converges in L1 to uniform
measure on the circle. The 63-fold product also converges in TV. Adding
fixed site phases to independent uniform phases leaves that product
measure unchanged, so both geometries have the same uniform-phase
limiting intensity law. Triangle inequalities give convergence to zero
for TV and therefore for h^2. One explicit, though not sharp, TV bound is

  TV(P_A(s),P_B(s)) <= 2*63*sum_(k>=1) exp(-k^2 s/2).

PROOF AT s->0. Put epsilon=sqrt(s). Using sum_j z_Gj=0, define the
normalized complex amplitude, regarded as an R^2-valued map,

  f_G,epsilon(Z)
    = sum_j z_Gj [exp(i epsilon Z_j)-1]/epsilon.

It converges in C^1 on compact sets to

  L_G Z = i sum_j z_Gj Z_j.

The covariance eigenvalues of this centered complex Gaussian, viewed as
a real two-dimensional Gaussian, are

  (63 + |M_G2|)/2 and (63 - |M_G2|)/2,
  M_G2 = sum_j z_Gj^2.

They are positive because |M_G2|^2=129187/400 < 63^2. The lemma therefore
proves convergence of each normalized amplitude law in TV to L_G Z.
The two covariance matrices may have different orientations, but have
the same eigenvalues. Consequently |L_A Z|^2 and |L_B Z|^2 have identical
laws. Squared norm is a measurable map, so

  TV(law(I_G(s)/s), law(|L_G Z|^2)) -> 0.

A common positive rescaling preserves TV. Applying the triangle
inequality to the common limiting squared-norm law gives

  TV(P_A(s),P_B(s))
    = TV(law(I_A(s)/s),law(I_B(s)/s)) -> 0.

Again h^2 <= 2 TV completes the argument.

It would have been invalid to infer this small-s TV limit merely from
the weak convergence of both unscaled intensities to zero. The normalized
amplitude argument and the rank-two covariance condition are essential.

6. What global optimization is now closed, and what remains
----------------------------------------------------------
COROLLARY. For either distance, there exists at least one
s_star in (0,infinity) attaining

  max_(0<s<infinity) D(P_A(s),P_B(s)) > 0.

PROOF. Choose any s0>0, where the distance is positive. The two endpoint
limits imply that outside some compact [a,b] subset of (0,infinity),
the distance is less than half its value at s0. Continuity gives an
attained maximum on [a,b], which is also a global maximum.

For fixed finite eta>0 and b>=0, the count distance also has a positive
global maximum at a finite interior s. Continuity follows from contraction
of the continuous phase distributions through the common intensity and
Poisson kernels. Both endpoint limits follow by data processing from
Theorem 3. The fourth factorial moment gives positivity.

For unrestricted detector gain and nonnegative background,

  sup_(s>0, eta>0, b>=0) D(Q_A(s,eta,b),Q_B(s,eta,b))
       = max_(s>0) D(P_A(s),P_B(s)).

The upper bound is data processing; the lower bound follows from
Theorem 2 at any maximizing s_star and b=0. No finite eta attains this
supremum, by the strict gain ordering.

STILL UNRESOLVED:

* The numerical or exact maximizing phase variance for the full latent
  TV or Hellinger separation.
* Its value, the number of maximizing widths, and uniqueness.
* Maximizers for resource-constrained detector objectives, which require
  the resource model to be specified.

Nothing in these arguments identifies the maximizer of the fourth-moment
gap with the maximizer of full-distribution TV or Hellinger distance.
