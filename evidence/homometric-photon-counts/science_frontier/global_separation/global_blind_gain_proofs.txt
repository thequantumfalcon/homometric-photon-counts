FOUR-COUNT BLIND GAINS: FINITENESS AND EXACT INTERVAL EXCLUSION
2026-10-20

Scope and status
---------------
We consider the existing two 63-site geometries, independent identically
distributed Gaussian phase motion at a fixed 0<s<infinity, and zero
background Poisson detection. The global qualitative finiteness theorem
below depends on two stated, published mathematical theorems. The interval
root certificate is an exact rational computation from the previously
verified moment table at t=exp(-s/2)=1/2.

The exact number of positive blind gains on the whole positive axis is
NOT determined here.

1. The quantity whose zeros matter
---------------------------------
Let P_A,P_B be the two intensity laws on [0,M], M=3969. Define

  F_s(eta) = E_A[I^4 exp(-eta I)] - E_B[I^4 exp(-eta I)].

For N|I ~ Poisson(eta I),

  Pr_A(N=4)-Pr_B(N=4) = eta^4 F_s(eta)/24.

Thus a positive gain is blind to the four-count indicator exactly when
F_s vanishes. Full count laws still differ at every eta>0, because their
fourth falling-factorial moments differ by eta^4 D4(s)>0.

Since I is bounded, F_s extends to an entire function of complex eta.
It is not identically zero: F_s(0)=D4(s)>0.

2. The finite-sign-change property of the intensity density difference
----------------------------------------------------------------------
For this section, s is fixed and strictly positive. A circle Gaussian
phase has density

  w_s(theta) = (1/(2 pi)) sum_(k in Z) exp(-k^2 s/2) exp(i k theta).

This is real analytic on a neighborhood of [-pi,pi]. Indeed its complex
Fourier series converges uniformly on every bounded complex strip.
The product density W_s(theta)=product_j w_s(theta_j) is consequently
analytic on a neighborhood of the compact phase cube C=[-pi,pi]^63.

Each intensity map

  f_G(theta)=|sum_j z_Gj exp(i theta_j)|^2

is real analytic on a neighborhood of C and is nonconstant. It is
nonconstant even independently of the chosen clean geometry: by changing
the phase variables one may align all phasors, or instead vary one
relative phase.

The intensity law is absolutely continuous. To see this, at least one
partial derivative of f_G is a nonzero analytic function, whose zero set
has Lebesgue measure zero. Hence the critical set {gradient f_G=0} also
has measure zero. At every regular point, the inverse function theorem
gives a local coordinate chart with f_G as its first coordinate.
In that chart, pushforward of an absolutely continuous input measure
is absolutely continuous on the first coordinate. A countable collection
of these charts covers the regular set, and the remaining input set has
probability zero. This proves the claim.

Let H_G(u)=Pr(f_G(theta)<=u), 0<=u<=M. It is the parameter integral

  H_G(u)=integral_(R^63)
           W_s(theta) 1_C(theta) 1_{f_G(theta)<=u} dtheta.

The integrand, extended by zero outside the phase cube, is globally
subanalytic in (u,theta). This follows because the density and intensity
map are restrictions of analytic functions to a compact cube; interval
indicators and sublevel sets of these restricted analytic functions are
definable in the restricted-analytic structure.

EXTERNAL THEOREM A: Cluckers and Miller (2011), Theorem 1.3, proves
stability of constructible functions under parameter integration.
Here "constructible" means finite sums of products of globally
subanalytic functions and logarithms of positive globally subanalytic
functions. Applying that theorem to the displayed bounded integrand
shows that H_G is constructible on [0,M].

Such a function is definable in the real field with restricted analytic
functions and exponentiation, usually denoted R_an,exp, since logarithm
is definable as the inverse of exponentiation on positive reals.

EXTERNAL THEOREM B: van den Dries, Macintyre and Marker (1994),
Corollary 5.13, proves that R_an,exp is o-minimal. In particular, every
definable subset of the real line is a finite union of intervals and
points.

The set where H_G has a derivative, and that derivative on its domain,
are definable: the usual epsilon-delta definition of a finite derivative
is a first-order quantified condition over the real field. Absolute
continuity implies differentiability almost everywhere. Therefore the
definable exceptional set has measure zero and, by o-minimality, is
finite. Define h_G(u)=H'_G(u) where the derivative exists and set h_G=0
at the exceptional points. It is a definable density of P_G.

Consequently

  w(u)=u^4 [h_A(u)-h_B(u)]

is an integrable, definable signed function on (0,M). Its positive and
negative sets are finite unions of intervals and points. Ignoring
isolated points, which do not affect integration, w therefore has only
finitely many changes of sign. Also w is not zero almost everywhere,
because integral w = F_s(0)>0.

IMPORTANT LIMITATION OF THIS ARGUMENT. We have not asserted that
F_s(eta), with unrestricted eta, is itself definable by a general closure
of R_an,exp under integration. No such closure is used. Only the bounded
phase integral defining the CDF is treated by Theorem A. The next step is
a separate, elementary property of Laplace transforms.

3. Elementary variation-diminishing argument
-------------------------------------------
LEMMA. If a nonzero integrable signed function w on a compact interval
has m changes of sign, then its Laplace transform

  F(eta)=integral exp(-eta u) w(u) du

has at most m real zeros, counted with multiplicity.

Here sign changes are counted after zero intervals and sets of measure
zero are discarded. Choose increasing cut points a_1,...,a_m between
successive nonzero sign blocks. A cut may be placed anywhere inside a
zero interval separating opposite signs.

PROOF. Suppose F has real zeros eta_1,...,eta_l with multiplicities
at least r_1,...,r_l, where sum_i r_i=m+1. If it has more zeros or
larger multiplicities, simply discard excess conditions. Bounded support
allows differentiation under the integral, so

  integral u^k exp(-eta_i u) w(u) du = 0,  0<=k<r_i.

The real exponential-polynomial space

  V = span{u^k exp(-eta_i u): 1<=i<=l, 0<=k<r_i}

has dimension R=sum_i r_i=m+1, and every nonzero member has at most
R-1 real zeros counted with multiplicity. Here is an elementary proof
of this latter fact. A member is sum_i P_i(u) exp(lambda_i u), with
distinct real lambda_i and deg P_i<r_i. These functions are linearly
independent: as u tends to positive infinity, the largest exponent
with a nonzero coefficient polynomial cannot be cancelled by the
smaller exponents. For the zero bound, divide by one exponential and
differentiate. The polynomial attached to that exponent loses one
degree (or disappears), while each other polynomial retains at most
its previous degree. The resulting function belongs to a space of
dimension R-1 of the same form. Induction, starting with R=1, and
Rolle's theorem with multiplicities give the bound. If the derivative
is identically zero, the divided function is a nonzero constant and
has no zeros, which is already the required conclusion.

There is a nonzero g in V vanishing at all m cut points, since these
are m homogeneous linear conditions on a space of dimension m+1.
The zero bound shows that its prescribed m zeros are all simple and
that it has no others. Multiplying g by -1 if necessary, its alternating
signs match those of w on every nonzero sign block. Hence g(u)w(u)>=0
almost everywhere and is strictly positive on a set of positive measure.
Because g is bounded on the compact interval, integral g w>0. But g
is a linear combination of the displayed functions whose integrals
against w all vanish, so integral g w=0, a contradiction.

THEOREM 4 (GLOBAL QUALITATIVE FINITENESS). For each fixed finite positive
Gaussian phase variance s, there are only finitely many positive
four-count blind gains at zero background.

PROOF. The density construction gives a finite number m_s of sign
changes of w(u)=u^4(h_A-h_B). Apply the lemma to F_s. In fact the number
of real zeros, counted with multiplicity, is at most m_s. The entire
function F_s is nonzero, so every multiplicity is well defined and finite.

This is a non-effective finiteness result. It does not compute m_s,
give a certified gain after which no zeros occur, or determine the
integer number of positive roots. It also does not assert a bound
uniform over all s, nor address positive background.

4. Exact result at t=1/2: one root on the entire (0,1/128]
--------------------------------------------------------
Write mu_G,k=E_G[I^k] and D_k=mu_A,k-mu_B,k for the exact rational
moments at t=1/2. Moments through order 32 are available in

  science_extension/finite_detection/fixed_t_moments.json.

For x>=0, Taylor's theorem gives global one-sided exponential bounds.
If S_27(x)=sum_(j=0)^27 (-x)^j/j!, then

  0 <= exp(-x)-S_27(x) <= x^28/28!.

Therefore the polynomial

  U(eta)=sum_(j=0)^27 (-1)^j D_(4+j) eta^j/j!
          + mu_A,32 eta^28/28!

is an upper bound on F(eta), for every eta>=0. Notice that this preserves
the cancellation between the two structures before bounding the remainder.

Likewise, for the even Taylor polynomial S_26,

  -x^27/27! <= exp(-x)-S_26(x) <= 0.

Since F'(eta)=-E_A[I^5 exp(-eta I)]+E_B[I^5 exp(-eta I)], the polynomial

  V(eta)=sum_(j=0)^26 -(-1)^j D_(5+j) eta^j/j!
          + mu_A,32 eta^27/27!

is an upper bound on F'(eta), for every eta>=0.

To certify a polynomial p is negative on [a,b], rewrite it in the
Bernstein basis of degree d:

  p(a+(b-a)y)
    = sum_(i=0)^d beta_i binom(d,i)y^i(1-y)^(d-i), 0<=y<=1.

These basis functions are nonnegative and sum to one, so

  p([a,b]) <= max_i beta_i.

All coefficients and endpoints here are rational. The exact conversion
from p(x)=sum_j c_j x^j is

  q_k=(b-a)^k sum_(j=k)^d c_j binom(j,k) a^(j-k),
  beta_i=sum_(k=0)^i q_k binom(i,k)/binom(d,k).

The new script certify_global_blind_interval.py verifies, by Fraction
arithmetic, that every Bernstein coefficient is negative for

  V on [0,1/512],
  U on [1/512,1/128].

The maximum coefficients have the following decimal approximations
(the certificate stores the exact rational values):

  max Bernstein(V on [0,1/512]) = -30648.2084857291410014352207917...
  max Bernstein(U on [1/512,1/128]) = -8.67056780351911520895093576857...

It follows that F is strictly decreasing on [0,1/512], and strictly
negative everywhere on [1/512,1/128].

The script also independently rechecks positive and negative Taylor
enclosures at the endpoints of the existing rational root bracket

  a = 61217055127150107/36893488147419103232,
  b = 38260659454468817/23058430092136939520.

These have decimal approximations

  a = 0.00165929160405052584701356133134...
  b = 0.00165929160405052585243457219377...

with 0<a<b<1/512, F(a)>0 and F(b)<0. Also F(0)=D4>0.
The intermediate value theorem, the strict derivative bound, and the
second interval exclusion establish:

THEOREM 5 (EXACT INTERVAL ROOT COUNT). At t=1/2 and b=0, F has exactly
one zero in (0,1/128]. It is simple and lies in [a,b]. F is positive
below that root and negative above it through 1/128.

No numerical grid is used to infer this statement. Two exact Bernstein
convex-hull bounds certify the full intervals.

5. Reproduction and the remaining global question
-------------------------------------------------
From the repository/workspace root:

  python science_frontier/global_separation/certify_global_blind_interval.py
  python science_frontier/global_separation/certify_global_blind_interval.py --verify-only

To recompute the input moments from the original geometric phase engine
as well as verifying the new result:

  python science_frontier/global_separation/certify_global_blind_interval.py --rebuild-input

The output blind_interval_certificate.json contains the input hashes,
exact rational bounds, endpoints, and complete certificate coverage.
Only new files in science_frontier/global_separation are written.

Combining the two new results gives:

  At t=1/2 the global number of positive blind gains is finite and at
  least one, and exactly one lies in (0,1/128].

Additional roots above 1/128 have not been excluded or certified. The
exact global integer count remains unresolved. An unproved assertion of
"only one positive blind gain" would go beyond these results.
