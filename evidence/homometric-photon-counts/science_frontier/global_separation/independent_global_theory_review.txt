1. FIXED-s DEFINABILITY: THE WRAPPED-GAUSSIAN DENSITY IS ALLOWED
============================================================
For every fixed s>0, the Fourier series for the wrapped Gaussian converges
uniformly on each bounded complex strip: the Gaussian decay in k dominates
the factor exp(|k|*|Im theta|). Its restriction to the compact phase cube
therefore extends analytically to a neighborhood of that cube. The same
is true of the finite product density and the intensity map.

After a linear rescaling of the cube, each such restriction is a restricted
analytic function. The fact that the density was initially described by
an infinite Fourier series is no obstacle: R_an contains every analytic
restriction of this kind, not only finite elementary formulas. Real
constants and parameters fixed by the chosen s are permitted.

The indicator of the cube, and the indicator of the restricted-analytic
sublevel set f_G(theta)<=u, are globally subanalytic functions. Multiplying
by the restricted density and extending by zero outside the cube preserves
that property. The integrand is bounded and compactly supported in theta,
so it is Lebesgue integrable for every u in the parameter domain [0,M].

Cluckers--Miller Theorem 1.3 thus applies exactly as used and makes the CDF
H_G(u) constructible. A constructible function is definable in R_an,exp,
because positive logarithms are definable using exponentiation. The cited
o-minimality theorem then applies to the CDF and its definable derivative.

Crucially, this is a fixed-s argument. It does not assert that the density
is jointly restricted analytic as s approaches 0, or that the entire
Laplace transform is definable for unrestricted eta. No unproved closure
of general R_an,exp-definable functions under integration is being used.
The proof correctly separates the CDF integration step from the subsequent
elementary Laplace-transform step.

2. ABSOLUTE CONTINUITY AND FINITELY MANY SIGN BLOCKS
=================================================
The intensity map is nonconstant real analytic on connected R^63. Hence
some partial derivative is not identically zero. The zero set of that
analytic partial derivative has Lebesgue measure zero, and the critical
set is a subset of it. The input phase distribution is absolutely
continuous, so the critical set has probability zero.

Near a regular point, use f_G itself as one coordinate, together with
62 appropriate original coordinates. The inverse function theorem gives
a local diffeomorphism. Change of variables followed by Fubini shows
that a one-dimensional null set has zero pushforward probability in
this chart. A countable chart cover of the regular set establishes
absolute continuity of the entire intensity law. Cube-boundary points
and critical points do not create hidden atoms.

Consequently H_G is an absolutely continuous CDF. Finite differentiability
holds almost everywhere. For a definable H_G, both the domain and graph
of its finite derivative are definable by the epsilon-delta formula.
The exceptional set is a definable null subset of the real line, hence
finite by o-minimality. Endpoints can simply be included in that finite
exceptional set. Assigning zero there gives a definable density version.

The signed density u^4(h_A-h_B) is integrable. Its positive and negative
sets each have finitely many connected components, so only finitely many
nonzero sign blocks remain after zero intervals and null sets are ignored.
The density is not zero almost everywhere because its integral is the
accepted positive fourth-moment gap. This supplies precisely the input
needed for the next lemma, without requiring a bounded density near
critical values.

3. LAPLACE ZERO COUNT: MULTIPLICITIES ARE HANDLED CORRECTLY
========================================================
For bounded support, differentiation of the Laplace transform under the
integral is valid to every order. A zero of multiplicity r at eta gives
orthogonality to u^k exp(-eta u), k=0,...,r-1. If there were more than m
zeros counting multiplicity, one could select exactly m+1 such conditions.

The associated real exponential-polynomial space has dimension m+1.
Independence follows by selecting the largest real exponent with a nonzero
polynomial coefficient and comparing growth as u tends to positive
infinity. The stated zero bound on this space is valid with multiplicity:
divide by one exponential, differentiate, and reduce the total allowed
polynomial dimension by one. Rolle's theorem gives at least R-1 derivative
zeros from R zeros counted with multiplicity. If the derivative vanishes
identically, the original divided function is a nonzero constant and
already has no zeros. This covers the induction's possible degenerate case.

Choose one cut between each pair of successive opposite nonzero sign
blocks, placing it in a zero gap if one is present. There is a nonzero
space element vanishing at the m cuts by linear algebra. The zero bound
forces these prescribed zeros to be simple and excludes all additional
real zeros. Its signs therefore alternate exactly at those cuts.

After one global sign choice, the product with the signed density is
nonnegative almost everywhere and positive on a set of positive measure.
Its integral is strictly positive; boundedness of the space element on
the compact support guarantees integrability. The same integral is zero
by the selected transform-derivative conditions, a contradiction.

This proves the at-most-m real zero bound including multiplicities, not
merely a bound on distinct sign-crossing roots. It therefore implies the
stated finiteness of positive four-count blind gains. The positive value
F_s(0)=D4(s) also ensures the entire transform is not identically zero.

The conclusion remains non-effective: this proof does not evaluate m,
locate the last positive root, bound the count uniformly in s, or exclude
additional roots above the numerically certified interval.

4. THE TOTAL-VARIATION DELTA LEMMA IS STRONG ENOUGH
=================================================
The full-rank hypothesis permits invertible linear coordinates (u,v)
in which the limiting linear map is projection onto u. The transformed
input has a smooth integrable density g. A compact smooth subdensity can
be obtained explicitly as g_R=g*psi_R with a smooth cutoff 0<=psi_R<=1;
its omitted mass tends to zero.

On a fixed compact set containing this support, the C^1 convergence of
f_epsilon to projection makes both the displacement and its derivative
small. Multiplying the displacement by another smooth cutoff that equals
one around the support yields a globally defined perturbation of identity
with derivative norm strictly less than one for small epsilon.

The global inverse used in the proof exists: the equation x=y-H_epsilon(x)
is a contraction for each y. Injectivity follows from the same Lipschitz
bound. The inverse function theorem provides its C^1 inverse since
I+DH_epsilon is invertible everywhere. No unstated global injectivity
assumption on the original f_epsilon is needed.

The cutoff perturbation tends uniformly to zero; its inverse does too.
Its Jacobian determinant converges uniformly to one, and the images of
the compact subdensity support remain in a fixed compact set. The explicit
change-of-variables densities thus converge in L1. Projecting to u
contracts total variation and agrees with f_epsilon on the retained
subdensity's support. The omitted mass produces an arbitrarily small
error under either pushforward. One then lets the retained mass tend
to one. This proves genuine TV convergence, not only weak convergence.

5. APPLICATION TO THE SMALL-NOISE INTENSITY LIMIT
==============================================
At epsilon=sqrt(s), sum z_j=0 gives the exact identity

  I_G(s)/s = |sum_j z_Gj*(exp(i epsilon Z_j)-1)/epsilon|^2.

The normalized amplitude converges in C^1 on every compact Z-set to
i*sum_j z_Gj Z_j. Its real two-dimensional covariance eigenvalues are
(63 +/- |sum z_Gj^2|)/2. The accepted common value
|sum z_Gj^2|^2=129187/400 is strictly below 63^2, so both limiting linear
maps have full rank two and the lemma applies.

The Gaussian covariance matrices may have different orientations, but
their eigenvalues coincide. Orthogonal rotations preserve squared norm,
so the limiting squared-norm distributions are identical. Measurable
squared norm contracts TV. A common positive rescaling preserves TV
exactly. Therefore the triangle inequality around that common limiting
law proves TV(P_A(s),P_B(s))->0 as s decreases to zero. The inequality
h^2<=2*TV supplies the corresponding squared-Hellinger limit.

There is no inference of TV convergence merely from convergence to a
point mass. The full-rank normalized amplitude and the explicit TV lemma
are the necessary additional steps, and both are present and valid.
