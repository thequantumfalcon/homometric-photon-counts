1. What this pass changes
-------------------------
New primary full-text comparisons were added for fluorescence factorial
cumulant analysis: Muller (2004), Wu and Muller (2005), and Scales and
Swain (2019), together with the latter paper's 2025 correction. These are
direct precedents for extracting unknown parameters and distinguishing
components using a few photon-count factorial cumulants.

Therefore, the new project result should not be described as the invention
of factorial-cumulant calibration, separation using four photon moments,
or removing Poisson shot noise through factorial cumulants. The specific
candidate contribution is the globally invertible map for THIS homometric
Gaussian-phase family, together with the first-three/fourth-moment structural
selection theorem and the explicit confidence procedure.

The Ainsleigh (2017) full-text access gap remains. Its official abstract and
bibliography were rechecked. The project's intensity is algebraically a
special case of the wrapped-variable quadratic forms described there.

No exact earlier statement of the project's common-null theorem or its
specific monotone calibration polynomial was found in the passages inspected
in this pass. That is a report of the search/read scope, not proof of absence.

2. Freeze the claims before judging their priority
--------------------------------------------------
C1. For the supplied two finite, unit-weight homometric geometries at a
common clean null, identical independent Gaussian site phases give equality
of the first three SINGLE-FREQUENCY RAW INTENSITY MOMENTS and a nonzero
fourth-moment difference for every finite positive variance.

C2. The fourth-moment difference selects the real diagonal bispectral
invariant Re[M4 conjugate(M2)^2] with the stated exact Gaussian coefficient.
The invariant itself is familiar. The proposed contribution is the precise
measurement-order cancellation and coefficient for this experiment.

C3. For the two supplied 63-site candidates and N|I~Poisson(b+eta I),
the first three photon factorial cumulants identify x=exp(-s), eta, and b:

  c3^2/c2^3 = C(x)^2/V(x)^3.

The project proves that this ratio is strictly increasing on 0<x<1 by
checking all coefficients of 2C'V-3CV' are positive. The fourth factorial
moment then identifies the structure. This is a global population inversion
for the stated family, not a claim about arbitrary unknown phase laws.

C4. Truncation plus simultaneous bounded-moment confidence intervals gives
a fixed-sample joint confidence procedure with an 'unresolved' outcome.
The reported m=10^36 example is an explicit sufficient existence result,
not an optimized or practically demonstrated acquisition requirement.

C5. Strict detector ordering, the infinite-gain limit, interior width-optimum
existence, and the finite-root theorem are applications of probability,
analysis, and established external theorems. Their application and exact
certificates may be useful parts of the project; no broad method-priority
claim is justified merely by deriving them here.

3. Claim-level comparisons with inspected primary sources
---------------------------------------------------------
R13. Sylvain Ravy, "Homometry in the light of coherent beams,"
Acta Crystallographica A 69 (2013), 543-548.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1107/S0108767313022733
Author preprint: https://arxiv.org/html/1303.3792v3
Read in this pass: sections III-V and their definitions of Bragg, diffuse,
and coherent homometry; speckle-intensity distributions and four-point order.
The arXiv version history is March 2013; a regenerated 2026 HTML body date
must not be treated as its publication date.

The paper distinguishes structures with matching diffuse diffraction through
different speckle statistics, including Rudin-Shapiro/Bernoulli comparisons.
It also separates this situation from complete coherent homometry of a fixed
basis. Thus the broad statement that fluctuations can expose information
hidden by mean diffraction is prior art. Its inspected comparison is not
the present pair of finite exactly clean-homometric geometries subjected
to the same Gaussian phase law at one common null. Spatial four-point
correlations and fourth raw moments of one intensity are distinct objects.
This supplies close context for C1-C2, not an external proof of them.

A17. Phillip Ainsleigh, "A Method for Computing Moments of Quadratic Forms
Involving Wrapped Random Variables," SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and
Applications 38(2) (2017), 554-573.
DOI and official page: https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082019
https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/16M1082019
Read: official abstract, metadata, bibliography. Full-text endpoint:
https://epubs.siam.org/doi/pdf/10.1137/16M1082019
It was inaccessible in this pass. Author/title/DOI and institutional-domain
searches did not supply a usable primary full text.

The abstract describes analytic higher moments of quadratic forms in
independent wrapped variables, using characteristic functions and matrix
symmetries, with wrapped-normal examples. The direct project specialization
is our algebraic inference: W_j=exp(i theta_j), a=conjugate(z), and
I=|z^T W|^2=W^*(a a^*)W. It is a rank-one Hermitian positive quadratic form.
Hence analytic finite wrapped-phase intensity-moment computation cannot be
claimed as a new general method. Whether the unread article already implies
C1-C2 as an immediate specialized corollary remains an open comparison.

S25. Kenji Shimizu, "Process-based modelling of nonharmonic internal tides
using adjoint, statistical, and stochastic approaches - Part 1,"
Ocean Science 21 (2025), 2233-2253.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.5194/os-21-2233-2025
Full text:
https://os.copernicus.org/articles/21/2233/2025/os-21-2233-2025.html
Read: Appendix A, equations A2-A3 and A14, plus the finite/limiting-model
distinction in the discussion.

Its finite-component characteristic function allows deterministic phase
offsets and component-specific phase spreads, including wrapped-normal
Fourier coefficients. Choosing fixed unit amplitudes, equal spreads, and
structural offsets gives this project's random-phasor model; the total
centering cancels at the common null. That specialization is our inference
from the formulas. The many-source Gaussian approximation must not replace
the exact finite model in C1-C2. These inspected sections provide a general
finite-phasor framework, not the project's moment-cancellation theorem.

M04. Joachim D. Muller, "Cumulant Analysis in Fluorescence Fluctuation
Spectroscopy," Biophysical Journal 86 (2004), 3981-3992.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1529/biophysj.103.037887
Author-hosted full text:
https://groups.spa.umn.edu/2-photon/publications/JM04BJ.pdf
Read: photon-detection theory, equations 1-6 and 12-13, and "Resolving
species with cumulants" (published pp. 3982-3985).

This paper relates photon factorial cumulants to integrated-intensity
cumulants and determines brightness/concentration parameters of a binary
fluorescent mixture from four factorial cumulants, with error analysis.
Thus using a few photon cumulants for joint parameter inference and
component resolution is established. Its latent fluorescence-mixture
parameters differ from the Gaussian phase width, gain, background, and
fixed homometric geometry in C3. It does not establish the specific
monotonicity polynomial or our global nuisance inversion.

WM05. Bin Wu and Joachim D. Muller, "Time-Integrated Fluorescence Cumulant
Analysis in Fluorescence Fluctuation Spectroscopy,"
Biophysical Journal 89 (2005), 2721-2735.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1529/biophysj.105.063685
Author-hosted full text:
https://groups.spa.umn.edu/2-photon/publications/BW05BJ.pdf
Read: equations 1-3, binning-function theory overview, and calibration
discussion on published p. 2733. All estimation proofs were not audited.

Equation 3 states the photon factorial-cumulant/intensity-cumulant identity.
The paper treats finite integration time and estimates brightness,
occupation, and diffusion parameters; it also calibrates higher point-spread
shape factors. This is prior art for the general principles behind C3 and
for treating integration-time effects. Its formulas concern integrated
fluorescence, not the specific fixed-phase-snapshot family. The project's
snapshot model requires a separate argument when motion occurs during
the count gate.

SS19. Nathan Scales and Peter S. Swain, "Resolving fluorescent species by
their brightness and diffusion using correlated photon-counting histograms,"
PLOS ONE 14(12) (2019), e0226063.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0226063
Full text:
https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0226063
Read: "Moments," "Moments of moments," and analytic parameter-inversion
subsection, equations 34-36 and 59-62.

The text explicitly uses the fact that a constant Poisson background
contributes only to the first factorial cumulant. It also analytically
inverts four factorial cumulants to infer a two-species brightness and
concentration model, with label exchange accounting for symmetry.
Thus background removal above order one and analytic few-cumulant inversion
are prior principles. C3 adds a different family-specific global uniqueness
argument. The paper's sampling-error approximations do not supply C4's
explicit fixed-sample simultaneous guarantee for our model.

Correction checked in full:
Scales and Swain (2025), PLOS ONE 20(3), e0320511.
https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0320511
It corrects funding information, a summation-rendering error in equation 36,
and supplies previously omitted supporting information. We do not use an
uninspected variance formula or detector correction from that supplement.

SA95. Theofanis Sapatinas, "Identifiability of mixtures of power-series
distributions and related characterizations,"
Annals of the Institute of Statistical Mathematics 47 (1995), 447-459.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00773394
Official full text: https://www.ism.ac.jp/editsec/aism/pdf/047_3_0447.pdf
Read again: Theorem 2.1 and Note 1.

Arbitrary Poisson-mixture identifiability is established there. Recovering
the rate distribution from the complete count law is therefore prior
mathematics. It does not by itself show that three factorial cumulants
recover the particular phase width/gain/background in C3, nor that four
moments distinguish an arbitrary unknown mixing law.

5. Resulting priority position and next concrete comparison
----------------------------------------------------------
The exact finite homometric measurement-selection theorem and its explicit
Gaussian-phase calibration map remain candidates for a specific contribution.
There is no basis here for claiming priority for the broad fluctuation idea,
the bispectral invariant, random-phasor moment computation, Poisson-mixture
identifiability, or factorial-cumulant inference.

The next priority check is concrete: obtain Ainsleigh's full article and the
remaining Barakat full texts through legitimate access; specialize their
moment formulas to W^*(a a^*)W at sum_j z_j=0; compare surviving fourth-
intensity-moment invariants and coefficients; then check whether the project's
family-specific three-cumulant inversion already follows from a cited special
case. Record exact theorem/equation matches and any assumptions that differ.
Only after this comparison should the final theorem's novelty wording be
settled with a qualified external reviewer.
