2. Questions for a mathematical/statistical reviewer
----------------------------------------------------
R1. Reconstruct the clean homometry, common null, and exact structural
power sums from the site coordinates. Check that the first-three-moment
equality and fourth-moment coefficient follow for all finite positive
Gaussian phase variances, not merely sampled widths. Check the distinction
between a fourth intensity moment and a fourth spatial correlation.

R2. Independently derive the shared Var(I) and kappa_3(I) polynomials.
Check positivity of c2,c3, the derivative of C^2/V^3, all coefficients of
2C'V-3CV', and the global inversion domain. Check the recovery of eta and b,
the excluded boundary cases, and the fourth-factorial-moment structural gap.
Under equicorrelated Gaussian motion, verify that only s_eff=s(1-rho) is
identified, not s and rho separately.

R3. Audit the finite-sample coverage event, falling-factorial tail bound,
parameter-box inclusion, and structure rejection rule. Confirm that reuse
of the same data for calibration and structure selection is covered by the
single simultaneous event. Check the distinction between unconditional
coverage/error control and conditional correctness after selection. Verify
the finite existence example without describing m=10^36 as an optimal or
practically realistic sample count.

R4. Audit the tighter testing bounds against their precise experiment:
known nuisance parameters, equal priors where stated, independent snapshots,
finite count truncation with a rigorous tail, and outward numerical bounds.
Identify exactly which results bound the optimal sample count and which
would be needed to determine its exact integer value.

R5. Check strict gain/background ordering and the infinite-gain argument.
In particular, distinguish weak convergence of N/eta from the false claim
of individual TV convergence to a continuous latent law. Audit the
nondegenerate C^1 normalized-amplitude lemma used at small noise and the
matching limiting covariance eigenvalues.

R6. Independently replay the exact Bernstein interval certificate. For the
global finiteness proof, check restricted analyticity at FIXED s, applicability
of Cluckers-Miller Theorem 1.3 to the bounded sublevel integrand, absolute
continuity of the intensity law, derivative definability, o-minimal finite
sign changes, and the exponential-polynomial zero bound with multiplicity.
Do not substitute arbitrary R_an,exp integration closure. Confirm that the
proof gives no numeric global root count or effective final root cutoff.

R7. Check projected-covariance equivalence, the complete admissible
equicorrelation range, the two exact counterexamples to lower-moment
cancellation, and the L8/TV robustness margins. Distinguish site dependence
within a snapshot from temporal dependence across snapshots.

R8. Compare the frozen claims with the primary literature, especially the
full Ainsleigh and Barakat papers. Specify any theorem that already implies
the exact result. Mathematical correctness and publication priority should
receive separate assessments.

Requested return format: for each R1-R8, report checked scope, a proof or
counterexample for any disputed step, required changes, unresolved questions,
and whether the reviewer independently ran the arithmetic. A reviewer may
accept one component and leave another unresolved. An internal rerun or
another AI response must not be relabeled external peer review.

PART B. MEASUREMENTS AND MODEL CHECKS
====================================

3. Define the physical realization before choosing an apparatus
--------------------------------------------------------------
The mathematical observable is

  I_G=|sum_(j=1)^63 z_Gj exp(i theta_j)|^2, 0<=I_G<=3969,
  N | I_G ~ Poisson(b+eta I_G).

The unit amplitudes, known structural phases, Gaussian residual phase law,
and count kernel are substantive assumptions. The experimental team must
state what physical quantity implements each term.

Record whether the realization is a controlled phasor-sum demonstration,
a displaced-scatterer system, or another specified platform. A successful
phasor demonstration would validate that realization and its measurement
model; it would not alone establish applicability to arbitrary materials.
No particular platform, hardware, length scale, cost, or acquisition
duration is assumed here.

The foundation uses q0=pi/3 in its coordinate convention. For a spatial
realization, supply the map from those coordinates to physical positions
and the actual wavevector. The relevant phase error is

  epsilon_j = q_actual . r_actual,j - q0 . r_target,j,

after a removable common phase has been accounted for. A dimensionless
coordinate tolerance is not a physical length tolerance until that map
and its uncertainty are supplied.

4. Required records
-------------------
Geometry/amplitudes:
  Target and measured site coordinates or phases; site labels; complex
  amplitude weights and their uncertainty; wavevector and acceptance
  bandwidth; illuminated sites; and the measured clean-null residual.
  Verify matching clean diffraction within stated resolution/uncertainty,
  using a predetermined scan or equivalent calibrated characterization.
  A finite scan supplies evidence within its coverage, not proof of an
  exact all-frequency mathematical identity.

Motion:
  Site-resolved phase or displacement records where available; commanded
  and achieved phase distributions; projected means and covariances;
  site-to-site variation; time correlations; and changes during each
  exposure. A common phase is unobservable in intensity. The relevant
  Gaussian covariance is P C P with P=Id-11^T/63.

Detector:
  Raw nonnegative integer counts; timestamps; count-gate lengths; dead-time,
  afterpulse, clipping and overflow flags if present; dark/reference runs;
  gain and background calibration records with uncertainty; and evidence
  for the permitted conditional rate range.

Run organization:
  Structure/control code, condition code, acquisition order, run/reset
  identifier, external calibration version, source/intensity-monitor
  information where available, and all exclusions with their reasons.
  Preserve ordered raw observations; histograms alone lose temporal checks.

5. Checks that distinguish the intended experiment from a different model
------------------------------------------------------------------------
5a. Equal amplitudes and common null.
Determine whether measured unequal weights, phase offsets, or wavevector
spread fit inside a justified perturbation model. The mathematical
counterexamples show that arbitrarily small site-specific noise or geometry
errors can create lower-moment differences. Observing such a difference
does not by itself demonstrate the ideal fourth-order selection mechanism.

The existing explicit robustness margin at s=log(2) applies to its stated
coupling budget and preserves a fourth-moment distinction. It does not
preserve exact lower-moment equality. Do not transfer its numeric tolerance
unchanged to the t=1/2 reference, which has s=2 log(2).

5b. Gaussian phase law and dependence.
Compare measured circular Fourier coefficients E exp(i k theta_j) with
exp(-k^2 s/2), with prespecified uncertainty bands and harmonic orders.
For moments through I^4, individual phase harmonics through order four are
directly relevant, but matching those marginals alone does not establish
the joint law. Check cross-site dependence and projected covariance.
Pure common motion is a decisive negative control: it leaves the clean-null
intensity identically zero in the ideal model.

If phase measurements are available only after wrapping, do not silently
treat an arbitrary unwrapping as measured Gaussian covariance. State the
circular model or justified phase-tracking procedure. If motion is
equicorrelated, report an effective residual variance unless an independent
measurement separates the common component.

5c. Counts conditional on a fixed intensity.
Use reference conditions with known or independently monitored fixed
intensity to check mean-response linearity and the complete conditional
count distribution over the intended range. The fluctuating signal's
unconditional count variance can exceed its mean under the valid model;
that alone is not evidence against conditional Poisson detection.
Characterize background variation and dependence on the signal.

A dark-count mean should not simply be subtracted from every integer count
and then treated as a background-free Poisson observation. Use the actual
Poisson-background model, or a separately derived valid transformation.
The certified four-count blind gain assumes b=0; positive background
requires a new prediction, not reuse of that number.

5d. Motion during a gate and temporal independence.
A detector generally responds to intensity integrated over its count gate.
If the phase configuration changes appreciably during the gate, the latent
variable is the integrated intensity rather than the snapshot I in the
current theorem. Specify a regime justifying the frozen-snapshot model or
derive the time-integrated replacement before interpreting moment ratios.
Finite integration effects are an established issue in photon cumulant
analysis [WM05 in priority_update.txt, equations 1-3].

Check ordered phase and count records for serial dependence and drift.
Use independently generated/reset snapshots when that independence is part
of the procedure's justification. Shuffling a correlated sequence does not
make the original information content independent. A fitted effective
sample size or an insignificant autocorrelation test is not a replacement
for a finite-sample bound under a specified dependence model.

5e. Bounds used by the confidence procedure.
The truncation procedure requires a justified deterministic rate bound B.
With exact unit amplitudes, an independently justified range eta<=eta_max,
b<=b_max gives B=b_max+3969 eta_max. A finite pilot's maximum observed
count is not such a bound, because Poisson counts are unbounded.
Uncertainty in any asserted calibration bound must be propagated or given
its own coverage allocation.

PART C. PREANALYSIS PROTOCOL
============================

6. Pilot and freezing the analysis
---------------------------------
Use pilot/calibration observations to assess whether the actual device can
resolve the predicted gap and whether the count/motion model is adequate.
Select the final regime using that pilot, then freeze the confirmatory
protocol before viewing the structure comparison or blinded labels.

The concrete reference t=1/2 (s=2 log 2), eta=1/512, b=0 is an exact
mathematical benchmark, not an assertion that a device can attain it.
Use measured feasible settings and regenerate prediction bounds for those
settings. Do not call the benchmark globally optimal.

Freeze the geometry/phase files and hashes; candidate models; physical-unit
mapping; phase-width and gain ranges; gate length and reset schedule;
calibration dataset; sample counts per condition; exclusion rules; count
cutoff K and tail rule; exact/software precision; confidence allocation;
model-check/equivalence margins; and stopping rule.

An experimentalist must supply the attainable parameter range and noise/
systematic-error budget before a realistic sample requirement can be
computed. The mathematical m=10^36 existence certificate supplies no
practical feasibility claim.

7. Primary inference
---------------------
Prespecify the primary task as joint calibration and A/B identification
for an independent blinded confirmatory sequence. Use the simultaneous
four-factorial-moment confidence construction in calibration_proof.txt.
Report the full parameter box and the surviving candidate set:

  {A}: A retained alone;
  {B}: B retained alone;
  {A,B}: unresolved;
  empty set: inconsistent with the tested model/confidence set.

Do not force a binary decision when the prescribed procedure is unresolved.
For each sequence, fit phase width/gain/background through moments 1-3 and
use moment 4 for the structural check exactly as specified by the method.
The mathematical confidence proof already handles dependence among the
four estimated moments from the same observations.

Use an overall prespecified false-exclusion budget, for example 0.05 for
the family of confirmatory claims. If multiple structures, settings, or
formal diagnostics receive inferential claims, allocate budgets alpha_j
with sum alpha_j<=0.05 before acquisition. For the four bounded moment
statistics in one sequence, the generic Hoeffding choice is

  epsilon_j >= sqrt(log(8/alpha_j)/(2m_j)),

with a rigorous upward enclosure, and the same stated truncation bounds.
The existing hard-coded 95% example must be recomputed if its allocation
changes. Do not combine several separate 95% outputs and call the joint
result 95%.

Use a fixed sample size. Optional continuation after inspecting a result
requires a separately validated sequential procedure; ordinary fixed-sample
coverage does not automatically survive repeated stopping decisions.

8. Distribution and mechanism checks
-------------------------------------
Retain all count bins, including a declared overflow/tail bin. Calculate
predicted bin probabilities across the nuisance confidence set and compare
with simultaneous observed-bin intervals, with their own prespecified
coverage allocation. A valid composite-model compatibility check retains
the model if at least one admissible parameter vector fits all selected
constraints; separate marginal interval overlaps alone need not imply a
jointly feasible fit. Conservative outer boxes can yield 'unresolved'.

For A/B comparison controls, randomize acquisition order and blind the
analysis labels. Require the nuisance regime to be matched or model its
differences explicitly. Test the predicted positive fourth-factorial-moment
gap after that calibration. For the first three moments, define practical
equivalence margins from metrology/model uncertainty beforehand. A
nonsignificant difference does not prove exact equality.

Prespecified secondary controls may include:
  * repeated acquisition of the same geometry;
  * no residual motion and a pure common-phase perturbation;
  * a second calibrated finite phase width;
  * a gain change that leaves the latent configuration and gate model fixed;
  * the four-count sign change near its certified gain, only after the
    actual background and gain uncertainty have been incorporated.

These controls must not be selected post hoc for a favorable outcome.
Changing exposure time can change the latent integrated-intensity law;
the gain-monotonicity theorem alone does not justify that intervention.

9. Interpretation and report
----------------------------
Publish or hand off the predetermined analysis, ordered raw observations,
calibration records, exact model predictions and numerical error bounds,
all exclusions, and results for every planned condition.

Keep separate conclusions:

* Mathematical result: the theorem follows under its stated assumptions.
* Model adequacy: measured controls are compatible or incompatible with
  specified tolerances; finite data cannot prove an exact distributional
  assumption or arbitrary independence.
* Identification: the calibrated confidence procedure retained one, both,
  or neither structure at the declared error budget.
* Physical scope: the tested realization, parameter range, and uncertainty.
* Priority: the literature/referee comparison, independently of whether an
  experiment agreed with the prediction.

A successful physical result would be agreement within the planned
uncertainties, including the discriminating distribution/moment prediction
and the required controls. It would strengthen evidence for the specified
model in that realization. It would not constitute a 100% universal proof,
establish historical priority, or settle the unresolved global optimum and
global blind-gain integer count.
