FINITE-SAMPLE JOINT CALIBRATION AND STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION
20 October 2026

Status and scope
----------------
This file proves population identifiability from only four photon-count
factorial moments and supplies a computable finite-sample confidence procedure.
The first three moments calibrate the Gaussian phase variance, gain, and
background. The fourth distinguishes the two supplied 63-site geometries.
All statements assume the specified model. No measurements were supplied to
this calculation; the finite example is an analytic, worst-case guarantee.

The model is independent snapshots of

  I_G = |sum_(j=1)^63 z_Gj exp(i theta_j)|^2,
  theta_j independent N(0,s), 0<s<infinity,
  N conditional on I_G ~ Poisson(b+eta I_G), eta>0, b>=0.

The site phasors are the exact A/B construction already documented in the
foundation. Intensities are unnormalized: 0<=I<=M=3969. The Gaussian phase
family, the geometry candidates, and a valid upper bound on the conditional
Poisson rate are model inputs. The method does not identify an arbitrary
unknown phase law or arbitrary site-specific covariance from four moments.

1. Exact population inversion
-----------------------------
Put x=exp(-s), so 0<x<1. The foundation's exact moment polynomials give

  E I = 63(1-x),
  Var(I) = (1-x)^2 V(x),
  kappa_3(I) = (1-x)^3 C(x),

where both structures share

  V(x)=3906+126x+(103987/400)x^2,

  C(x)=476532+46305x+(945333/10)x^2
       +(124102867/40000)x^3+(311961/200)x^4
       +(17164599/160000)x^5+(5721533/160000)x^6.

Let f_r=E[(N)_r], with (N)_r=N(N-1)...(N-r+1). Conditional Poisson
factorial moments are E[(N)_r|I]=(b+eta I)^r. Consequently the first
three factorial cumulants are

  c1=f1=b+eta E I,
  c2=f2-f1^2=eta^2 Var(I)>0,
  c3=f3-3 f2 f1+2 f1^3=eta^3 kappa_3(I)>0.

The calibration-invariant ratio is

  Q=c3^2/c2^3=C(x)^2/V(x)^3.

Its derivative equals C(x)H(x)/V(x)^4, where
H=2C'V-3CV'. The exact coefficients of H, in ascending order, are

  181605564,
  36392842647/50,
  364718852253/10000,
  15247747953/20000,
  41383879047/8000,
  206539212561/80000,
  2433716998413/16000000,
  1784895156213/32000000.

All are positive; C and V have positive coefficients. Therefore Q is
strictly increasing on [0,1]. It has a unique inverse on its image, and

  x=Q^{-1}(c3^2/c2^3),
  eta=sqrt(c2/[(1-x)^2 V(x)]),
  b=f1-63 eta(1-x),
  s=-log x.                                                  (C1)

These equations establish global population identifiability of (s,eta,b)
throughout the stated interior parameter domain. They are not merely a
local Jacobian test. Boundary cases s=0, s=infinity, or eta=0 are excluded
and can destroy this conclusion.

At those identified calibration parameters the fourth factorial moments
differ by

  f4_A-f4_B=eta^4 D4(x),
  D4(x)=(455015761/20000)x^4(1-x)^5(x^3+3x^2+6x+6)>0.           (C2)

Thus the first four factorial moments jointly identify the geometry and
all three calibration parameters within this Gaussian-phase model.

This proof depends on the previously verified intensity moment polynomials
in science_completion/homogeneous_control/homogeneous_control_results.json.
The new program checks the polynomial identities, positive coefficients,
and gap factorization exactly using rational arithmetic. It records the
source hash; it does not pretend to be a new derivation of all original
geometry moments independent of that input.

2. Finite confidence intervals for the moments
----------------------------------------------
Assume a known deterministic rate bound 0<=Lambda=b+eta I<=B almost surely.
The Gaussian phase density has full support on the phase torus, and all
phasors can be aligned arbitrarily closely with positive probability.
Hence ess sup I=M. The rate bound implies b+eta M<=B, in particular
eta<=B/M. This last implication uses full support, not merely I<=M.

Fix an integer sample size m, a cutoff K, and a positive integer q with
K>=q+3. Define Z_r=(N)_r 1{N<=K}, for r=1,2,3,4. Then
0<=Z_r<=(K)_r. Write z_r=E Z_r and hat z_r for the sample average.

For N>=K+1,

  (N)_r <= (N)_(r+q)/(K+1-r)_q.

Taking expectations and using E[(N)_(r+q)]=E Lambda^(r+q)<=B^(r+q)
gives the fully explicit truncation bound

  0<=f_r-z_r<=T_r=B^(r+q)/(K+1-r)_q.                           (C3)

Let epsilon=sqrt(51/(20m)), or any rational upper enclosure thereof, and
e_r=(K)_r epsilon. Hoeffding's bounded-sum inequality and a union bound
over the four moments imply

  Pr(all r: |hat z_r-z_r|<=e_r)
    >=1-8 exp(-2m epsilon^2)
    >=1-8 exp(-51/10)>19/20.                                 (C4)

The last strict inequality is certified without floating-point evaluation:
the positive Taylor sum of exp(51/10) through degree 19 exceeds 160.
No independence between the four statistics within a snapshot is needed.
Independence between snapshots is needed for this Hoeffding argument.

On that one simultaneous event, every true factorial moment belongs to

  J_r=[max(0,hat z_r-e_r), min(B^r,hat z_r+e_r+T_r)].           (C5)

The intervals may be wide. This does not compromise coverage. A conclusion
of 'unresolved' is an intended possible output of the procedure.

3. Computable joint confidence set and decision
----------------------------------------------
From J1,J2,J3, use outward rational interval arithmetic to enclose c2,c3.
If the lower bound on c2 is nonpositive, return unresolved unless another
bound already proves that the assumed model is inconsistent. With a
strictly positive c2 lower bound, intersect the c3 interval with the
positive half-line and enclose Q=c3^2/c2^3. Intersect its range with
[Q(0),Q(1)] and invert by monotone rational bisection. The implementation
uses 160 bisections; their enclosing endpoints are retained exactly.

For the gain interval, Var(I) is strictly decreasing on [0,1]. Indeed

  Var'(x)=(1-x)[-2V(x)+(1-x)V'(x)],

and an upper bound on the bracket over [0,1] is -7544.065<0. The
coefficient of x in that bracket is positive; the proof uses the constant
plus all positive coefficients, rather than falsely claiming every
coefficient is negative. Endpoints therefore enclose Var(I), and rational
square-root bounds enclose eta in (C1). Intersect with [0,B/M]. Enclose b
using (C1), intersecting with [0,B].

The resulting Cartesian box is an outer confidence set, not an assertion
that all combinations in the box are simultaneously feasible. In
particular the box safely overestimates uncertainty caused by dependencies
between estimated moments and parameters.

For each geometry G, enclose

  f4_G(x,eta,b)=sum_(k=0)^4 binom(4,k)b^(4-k)eta^k E_G[I^k]

over the calibration box by rational polynomial interval evaluation.
Reject G only if that prediction interval is disjoint from J4. Retain all
surviving structures. Report 'identified' if exactly one survives;
'unresolved' if both survive; and 'model_inconsistent' if none survive.

On the event (C4), the true parameter vector remains in every outer set
and its geometry survives. Therefore the chance of falsely excluding the
true parameters or true structure is less than 5%. Conditional confidence
given that a structure is selected is not asserted; the guarantee is the
unconditional simultaneous coverage/error statement just proved.

This is a fixed-sample procedure. Repeated inspection at data-dependent
stopping times is not covered without a separate sequential correction.

4. A certified finite example with three unknown calibration parameters
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
For the existence calculation only, take true parameters

  x=1/4, s=2 log 2, eta=1/512, b=1/100,
  B=8, K=64, q=32, m=10^36.

These true parameter values are NOT supplied to the inference procedure.
The procedure takes a count histogram, the known B, and its preset K,q.
The exact inequality b+eta M<B is checked.

For either true structure, consider population-centered envelope intervals

  E_r=[max(0,f_r-2e_r-T_r), min(B^r,f_r+2e_r+T_r)].            (C6)

On event (C4), every actual data-derived interval J_r is contained in E_r:
write f_r=z_r+delta_r with 0<=delta_r<=T_r and use
hat z_r in [z_r-e_r,z_r+e_r] in both endpoints of (C5).

All inversion and prediction steps above are inclusion-preserving. The
program applies them to the larger intervals E_r and verifies, exactly,
that the calibration box contains the true parameters and the fourth
moment interval rejects the wrong geometry. This works for both A and B.
It follows for every possible dataset on event (C4), not just a favorable
sample or a Monte Carlo realization, that the procedure identifies the
true geometry and covers the true calibration. Thus m=10^36 is an explicit
sufficient finite sample size for at least 95% joint success at these
parameters.

The envelope calibration widths are less than

  width(x)   <1.108249e-8,
  width(eta) <2.816290e-11,
  width(b)   <2.694363e-9.

These are worst-case confidence-set calculations with exact rational
endpoints. They are not measured precision from a physical experiment.
The sample size is deliberately conservative and extraordinarily large;
this closes a constructive finite-existence question, not optimality or
practical feasibility. It must not be compared as an optimized rate to the
known-calibration likelihood-ratio result at b=0.

5. Consistency and limits of the method
--------------------------------------
Fixed K and q leave a positive deterministic tail enclosure. For a
consistency statement one must let the cutoff and arithmetic precision
increase. Consider the theoretical variant with square-root precision
and inverse-bisection depth both at least ceil(log2(m)) plus a fixed
positive constant. For example,
take q=1 and K_m=max(4,floor(m^(1/16))). The largest range error is
O(K_m^4/sqrt(m))=O(m^(-1/4)), and each tail bound is
O(1/K_m). Both tend to zero. Exact model moments are then separated by
(C2), and the continuous inverse (C1) yields identification on the
concentration event for all sufficiently large m at every fixed interior
parameter point. Growing precision removes the otherwise fixed rounding
floor. The delivered finite-example implementation uses 220 square-root
bits and 160 inversion bisections, which suffice for its certified m=10^36
but do not implement this unbounded-m consistency variant.
This is pointwise, not a uniform guarantee over widths
approaching either endpoint or gains approaching zero.

No claim is made of a minimal m, shortest confidence sets, performance
under arbitrary temporal dependence, identification of an unknown phase
family, or correctness when the detector is not conditionally Poisson.
For centered equicorrelated Gaussian site motion, the procedure identifies only
s_eff=s(1-rho); s and rho separately are not identifiable from this law.
