1. POPULATION IDENTIFICATION

Let x=exp(-s) be in (0,1), with independent Gaussian phase motions of common
variance s, the specified unit-weight 63-site candidate geometry, positive gain
eta, and constant background b>=0. Let M_r be the exact intensity moments from
the source. Its powers of t=exp(-s/2) are all even; the replay explicitly checks
this before converting them to powers of x.

The replay independently forms M_2-M_1^2 and M_3-3M_2M_1+2M_1^3 by dense
polynomial convolution. It verifies the identities

  Var(I) = (1-x)^2 V(x),
  kappa_3(I) = (1-x)^3 C(x),

where, in increasing powers of x,

  V = [3906, 126, 103987/400],

  C = [476532, 46305, 945333/10, 124102867/40000,
       311961/200, 17164599/160000, 5721533/160000].

The independently recomputed coefficients of H=2C'V-3CV' are

  [181605564, 36392842647/50, 364718852253/10000,
   15247747953/20000, 41383879047/8000, 206539212561/80000,
   2433716998413/16000000, 1784895156213/32000000].

All coefficients of V, C, and H are strictly positive. Thus

  Q(x)=C(x)^2/V(x)^3,
  Q'(x)=C(x)H(x)/V(x)^4 > 0

throughout [0,1]. The endpoint values are exactly

  Q(0)=7442/1953,
  Q(1)=24774567701488718400/5059985217815055403.

For N|I ~ Poisson(b+eta I), its factorial moments equal the ordinary moments
of b+eta I. The second and third factorial cumulants are eta^2 Var(I) and
eta^3 kappa_3(I). Therefore their squared standardized ratio identifies x
globally in the open parameter domain. Then eta is the positive square root
of k_2/Var(I)(x), and b=f_1-eta M_1(x). This proves identification of all three
nuisance parameters from the first three factorial moments. Positivity of the
third cumulant also ensures that squaring its ratio loses no sign information.

The replay verifies that the fourth intensity moment difference is exactly

  D_4(x)=(455015761/20000) x^4 (1-x)^5
         * (x^3+3x^2+6x+6),

which is strictly positive on (0,1). Equality of the first three factorial
moments forces equality of x, eta, and b. At that common tuple, the difference
of fourth factorial moments is eta^4 D_4(x)>0. Hence the first four factorial
moments globally identify the nuisance tuple and A/B structure under this
Gaussian-phase model.

This is not an endpoint theorem: s=0, x=1, eta=0, or the limit x=0 removes
some identification arguments. It also is not a theorem for an unspecified
phase distribution or arbitrary geometry.

2. SUPPORT BOUND AND MONOTONICITY USED BY THE INTERVAL INVERSION

The replay verifies

  d Var(I)/dx = (1-x)D(x),
  D(x)=-7686+(28387/200)x-(103987/100)x^2.

For 0<=x<=1, D(x)<=-7686+28387/200=-1508813/200<0.
Evaluating the variance at the reversed endpoints of an x interval therefore
gives a valid enclosing variance interval.

The program also uses eta<=B/3969 from the known rate bound B. This deserves
an explicit model argument: an upper bound I<=3969 by itself would not imply
that ceiling on eta. Under the stated Gaussian phase model with finite s>0,
every site phase has positive density on the entire circle. Independence gives
positive probability to every neighborhood of a configuration in which all
63 phasors align. Consequently the essential supremum of I is exactly 3969.
An almost-sure rate bound b+eta I<=B thus implies b+3969 eta<=B, and b>=0 gives
eta<=B/3969. The ceiling is valid here because of this support property.

All inspected interval operations enclose their inputs: four-corner products,
positive powers, interval subtraction, Horner polynomial evaluation, dyadic
square-root bounds, and monotone binary inversion of Q. Clipping intensity
moments to [0,3969^r] and nuisance coordinates to their known nonnegative
ranges preserves the true values. Inverting at a ratio endpoint or clipping
to x=0 or x=1 is conservative when the admissible domain is open.

3. FINITE-SAMPLE COVERAGE AND THE CUTOFF TAIL

For independent identically distributed observations, define

  Z_r=(N)_r 1_{N<=K},  r=1,2,3,4,  K=64.

Then 0<=Z_r<=(K)_r. The truncation is applied to the entire factorial variable;
counts above K contribute zero. For any integer q with K>=r+q-1, when N>K,

  (N)_{r+q}=(N)_r (N-r)_q
             >=(N)_r (K+1-r)_q.

Taking expectations and using the mixed-Poisson identity gives the exact
one-sided tail bound

  0<=f_r-E[Z_r]<=T_r=B^(r+q)/(K+1-r)_q.

There is no missing probability factor, division by sample size, or dependence
on an unobserved cutoff event. Large counts remain part of the statistical
experiment through this bounded statistic and its bias bound. For B=8, q=32,
the independently recomputed T_1,...,T_4 are approximately

  1.31439052718566868753094750093e-24,
  2.10302484349706990004951600149e-23,
  3.41911135845975235362889053145e-22,
  5.65293077932012389133309901200e-21.

The program uses an outward rational upper bound epsilon on sqrt(51/(20m)).
Hoeffding's bounded-variable inequality gives, for each order r,

  P(|mean(Z_r)-E[Z_r]|>(K)_r epsilon)
      <=2 exp(-2m epsilon^2)<=2 exp(-51/10).

Independence is needed across observations. Independence between the four
statistics on the same observation is not needed: the union bound applies
regardless of their dependence. It gives a simultaneous failure bound
8 exp(-51/10)<1/20.

For completeness, the bounded-variable inequality can be justified without
relying on a numerical table. For Y in [0,1], the second derivative of the
log moment-generating function under exponential tilting is a variance, at
most 1/4. Subtract its mean and integrate twice to get
log E exp(u(Y-EY))<=u^2/8. Independence, Markov's inequality, optimization in
u, and the same argument for -Y give the two-sided bound above. This is the
classical inequality of Hoeffding (1963), cited below.

The data intervals

  C_r=[max(0, mean(Z_r)-e_r),

therefore contain all four true factorial moments jointly with probability

4. WHY THE POPULATION-CENTERED EXAMPLE CERTIFIES ACTUAL DATA PROCEDURES

The certificate is not a simulated dataset and does not claim that measured
moments equal their expectations. On the simultaneous concentration event,
write E[Z_r]=f_r-delta_r with 0<=delta_r<=T_r. Then

  mean(Z_r) lies in [f_r-delta_r-e_r, f_r-delta_r+e_r],

which implies

  C_r is a subset of
  [max(0,f_r-2e_r-T_r), min(B^r,f_r+2e_r+T_r)].

This is the larger population-centered envelope used in finite_example().
The interval operations in infer() are inclusion-preserving. This includes
the ratio construction with a positive k_2 lower bound, monotone inversion
of Q, reversing endpoints for the decreasing variance, dyadic square roots,
and interval polynomial prediction of the fourth factorial moment. At the
certified finite point the envelope has positive k_2 and k_3 lower bounds,
so every narrower good-event data interval follows the same valid branch.

The true parameter tuple survives on the good event. Its true fourth moment
belongs to both the true-label prediction and the measured-moment interval.
If the larger envelope excludes the opposite label, every contained data
interval also excludes it. Thus the envelope calculation proves correct
identification and simultaneous nuisance coverage for all good-event datasets.
No assumption that four empirical moments are independent is used.

6. LIMITATIONS AND SMALL IMPLEMENTATION NOTE

The population theorem is global on the stated open nuisance domain. The
finite identification sample count is a sufficient bound for the specified
interior example, not a uniform 10^36 bound for all x, eta, and b, and not an
optimal sample count. Near degeneracy or at very weak gain, the procedure may
remain unresolved. Confidence coverage remains valid when an unresolved
result is returned; unresolved output is not a structural decision.

The result assumes the Gaussian phase family, exact candidate geometry,
independent observations, the specified within-shot motion model, and a valid
known rate support bound. It does not validate these assumptions against an
experiment, allow arbitrary dependent shots, establish historical priority,
or replace external mathematical review.

The follow-up review found one qualification needed in the proof's asymptotic
paragraph, and it has been corrected in the proof snapshot listed above. If
K grows without bound, fixed 220-bit square-root rounding would eventually
give a nonvanishing epsilon floor, and fixed 160-step inversion would leave
a resolution floor. The asymptotic argument is now explicitly a theoretical
variant with precision and inversion depth at least ceil(log2(m))+constant.
For q=1 and K_m=max(4,floor(m^(1/16))), the ideal range error is
O(K_m^4/sqrt(m))=O(m^(-1/4)); the extra arithmetic error is at most
O(K_m^4/m)=O(m^(-3/4)); and the tail bound is O(1/K_m). All vanish, giving
the stated pointwise good-event identification at each fixed interior tuple.
The delivered 220/160-bit implementation is explicitly limited to its finite
certificate and does not purport to implement that unbounded-m variant.
