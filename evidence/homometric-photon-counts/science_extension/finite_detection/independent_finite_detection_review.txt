INDEPENDENT COMPUTATIONAL METHOD

The new replay script imports neither certify_finite_detection.py nor the
moment engine. Its companion JSON retains the input hashes and independent
enclosures. It can be run as

  python independent_certificate_replay.py

The replay uses one Taylor polynomial and its signed remainder to reconstruct
count-probability bounds. For Hellinger terms it bounds sqrt(p) and sqrt(q)
separately on a finer rational grid and squares their difference interval.
It therefore avoids the producer's rationalized gap/denominator formula.
Every independent Hellinger component enclosure lies inside the corresponding
saved enclosure. The entire infinite count tail is then included.

The replay verifies all 50 probability intervals for k=0,...,24 under A and B,
all 25 Hellinger components, the full-count TV interval, the Bernoulli
complement, the Hellinger ratio, all four necessary/sufficient sample integers,
the explicit Bernstein threshold rule, the blind-gain signs and derivative,
and the small-noise six-moment formulas. It also checks the logarithm
constants using a distinct degree-50 rational exponential enclosure.

1. COUNT PROBABILITIES AND THE INFINITE TAIL

For x>=0, the Lagrange remainder for exp(-x) has the required alternating
sign for every degree. The odd partial sums are lower bounds and the even
partial sums are upper bounds even when the individual terms are not yet
decreasing. Multiplying by nonnegative I^k and taking expectations preserves
the inequalities. Scaling by eta^k/k! is legitimate because eta>0. Clipping
with zero, the raw-moment bound, and probability one only strengthens valid
bounds. The decimal-grid rounding is outward for negative as well as positive
numbers, since the lower endpoint uses floor and the upper endpoint ceiling.

For counts above K=24, (N)_25 is at least 25! on N>=25. Its expectation equals
eta^25 E[I^25] under a Poisson mixture. Thus the tail upper bound used in the
certificate controls every omitted count, not just one next term. The
Hellinger tail is at most the sum of the two omitted probability masses;
the TV tail is at most half that sum. These factors are correct.

2. HELLINGER DENOMINATORS AND RATIONAL SQUARE ROOTS

The producer's identity

  (sqrt(p)-sqrt(q))^2=(p-q)^2/(p+q+2sqrt(pq))

is valid except at p=q=0, where its explicitly coded zero case is valid.
The denominator increases in both probabilities. For a lower bound, the
smallest absolute gap is squared and divided by a denominator upper bound.
For an upper bound, the largest absolute gap is squared and divided by a
denominator lower bound, with p+q supplying a valid bound if that denominator
vanishes. The directions are correct, including overlapping intervals.

If j=floor(sqrt(x)*2^200), j/2^200 and (j+1)/2^200 enclose sqrt(x).
Computing j by integer square root of floor(x*2^400) gives the same integer.
The code verifies both squared inequalities exactly. No floating-point square
root affects the result. The independent direct-square-root replay confirms
the producer's denominator calculation without reusing it.

At t=1/2 and eta=1/512, the independent full-count h^2 enclosure is approximately

 [2.4026364459022103063e-17, 2.4026364470053042484e-17],

which is contained in the saved outward interval. The independent four-count
Bernoulli h^2 enclosure is approximately

 [2.2274424323159241440e-19, 2.2274424323159241507e-19].

The ratio of the two squared Hellinger distances is between the quoted
107.8652543852 and 107.8652544348. This numerical ratio is specific to the
stated parameter point and observable; it is not a universal information or
sample-efficiency ratio.

3. SAMPLE INEQUALITY DIRECTIONS

With h^2 in [L,U], rho=1-h^2/2, and m independent snapshots, the exact product
affinity is rho^m. Equal-prior optimal success is (1+TV)/2. The two bounds

  1-rho^m <= TV <= sqrt(1-rho^(2m))

give the directions used in the proof. Success at least 19/20 requires
rho^(2m)<=19/100. Since h^2<=U, this yields the stated necessary lower bound
after applying -log(1-U/2)<= (U/2)/(1-U/2). Replacing log(100/19) by a smaller
rational number weakens the necessary bound safely.

For sufficiency, h^2>=L gives rho^m<=exp(-m L/2). Making this at most 1/10
ensures optimal success at least 19/20. Replacing log(10) by a larger rational
number strengthens this requirement safely. The rational exponential checks
and the integer ceilings are correct.

The following exact integers were independently recovered:

  Full counts: necessary at least 69,121,152,393,630,388;
               sufficient 191,671,944,702,842,206.
  Four-count indicator only:
               necessary at least 7,455,770,689,765,032,714;
               sufficient 20,674,743,073,884,456,509.

The first number in each row excludes every smaller m from achieving the
specified optimal equal-prior success. The second number guarantees the
existence of an optimal likelihood-ratio rule with the specified success.
The exact transition is not located by these inequalities. The necessary
four-count-only number cannot be imposed on an observer keeping full counts.

The explicit empirical-frequency rule is also valid. Its threshold is midway
between the upper A probability and lower B probability. Both hypotheses
are therefore at least d/2 away. Centered Bernoulli variables have magnitude
at most one and variance at most the probability upper bound u. Applying the
same bound to either sign of the centered variable gives each conditional
error at most exp[-m d^2/(8u+4d/3)]. The integer
26,937,971,154,603,499,910 makes the exponent at least three; exp(3)>20 proves
the claimed strict error bound. This rule supplies a concrete test without
computing the full-count likelihood ratio.

4. THE FOUR-COUNT BLIND GAIN

The reconstructed bounds are strictly positive for F at 1/640 and strictly
negative at 1/512. At the final exact rational endpoints they remain strictly
positive on the left and strictly negative on the right. Their difference is
1/184467440737095516160, below 10^-20. Bounded intensity makes F analytic and
therefore continuous, so the sign change proves an exact zero, independently
of the displayed approximate decimal.

For a<=eta<=b, R_G,5(b)<=R_G,5(eta)<=R_G,5(a). Hence

 -upper(R_A,5(a))+lower(R_B,5(b)) <= F'(eta)
 <= -lower(R_A,5(b))+upper(R_B,5(a)).

This confirms the correct derivative directions without assuming F' itself
is monotone. Independent exact arithmetic gives the same strictly negative
uniform range, approximately

 [-41829.3019434014557484, -41829.3019433515423953].

There is exactly one simple zero inside the exact rational interval stated
in the proof. Its outward decimal display is near 0.00165929160405052585.
The derivative enclosure was proved on the exact rational interval, not the
slightly wider decimal display. No global uniqueness statement follows.

At this zero the two probabilities of N=4 coincide exactly, so their
Bernoulli indicators and every finite product of those indicators have the
same law. Equal-prior classification from only that indicator cannot exceed
one half. Meanwhile E_A[(N)_4]-E_B[(N)_4]=eta^4 D4>0 proves that the full count
laws remain distinct. The blindness is caused by discarding the other count
values; it does not contradict Poisson-mixture injectivity.

5. SIX-MOMENT FINITE-s BOUND

Using 1-x below exp(-x) for A and 1-x+x^2/2 above it for B gives exactly

  p_A(4)-p_B(4) >= eta^4 [D4-eta D5-eta^2 mu_B,6/2]/24.

The negative sixth-moment term belongs to B, as written. The reverse upper
comparison uses A's sixth moment. The probability upper bound U uses the
larger fourth moment and exp(-eta I)<=1. Whenever L>0,
(sqrt(p_A)-sqrt(p_B))^2 >= L^2/(4U), because the denominator is at most 4U.
The complement contribution is nonnegative. These steps are finite-parameter
inequalities, not asymptotic substitutions.

Substituting the accepted small-s moment expansions gives

  L^2/(4U) = eta^4 c5^2 s^6/(96 m4)+O(s^7).

The coefficient and s power are correct. The hypotheses ensuring eventual
L>0 and the common m4 are already in the prior moment proof. At t=999/1000,
the independently replayed six-moment expressions are positive and yield the
saved sufficient integer 38,905,896,029,459,193,642,493,650,866.

LIMITATIONS THAT MUST TRAVEL WITH THE RESULT

All finite sample claims assume known competing models, zero background,
the specified gain multiplying unnormalized intensity, and independent
snapshots conditional on the structure. Population identifiability of unknown
nuisance parameters does not remove these assumptions from a finite-sample
bound. Estimating nuisance parameters would require another analysis.

The proof certifies mathematical separation and explicit sample guarantees.
The enormous sample integers at the two illustrative points do not justify
an acquisition-time estimate or a claim of practical feasibility. Conversely,
they do not prove that every other operating point is equally difficult.
No global detector optimum or global blind-gain count was established.
