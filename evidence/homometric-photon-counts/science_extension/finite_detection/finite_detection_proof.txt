Scope
-----
These results extend the saved homogeneous Gaussian-motion theorem for the
specified 63-site pair. They are mathematical results for calibrated models,
not experimental observations or publication-priority claims. The notation is

  F_G = sum_j z_Gj exp(i sqrt(s) Z_j),   I_G = |F_G|^2,
  G in {A,B},  Z_j iid N(0,1),  n=63,  0 <= I_G <= n^2=3969,
  t=exp(-s/2),  N given I ~ Poisson(eta I),  eta>0.

Write mu_G,k=E[I_G^k] and D_k=mu_A,k-mu_B,k. The established exact result gives
D_0=D_1=D_2=D_3=0 and

  D_4 = (455015761/20000) t^8 (1-t^2)^5

Every intensity moment is finite. Its calculation below is exact, using the

1. EXACT MOMENTS AND RIGOROUS COUNT-PROBABILITY INTERVALS
------------------------------------------------------
For a single Gaussian phase factor W=exp(i sqrt(s) Z),

  E[W^a conjugate(W)^b] = t^((a-b)^2).

For fixed rational t, these are rational numbers. Define single-site mixed
cumulants kappa_ab by the usual moment-cumulant recurrence. Independence gives
the field cumulants K_G,ab=kappa_ab M_G,a-b, where
M_G,k=sum_j z_Gj^k. The recurrence obtained by selecting the block containing a
distinguished positive slot is, for a>0,

  E[F^a conjugate(F)^b]
    = sum_{i=1..a,j=0..b} binom(a-1,i-1) binom(b,j)
             K_ij E[F^(a-i) conjugate(F)^(b-j)].

For a=0, select a distinguished negative slot instead. This recurrence is a
partition identity; it includes every partition exactly once and requires no
limiting or Gaussian-field approximation. The adjacent unchanged engine uses
exact arithmetic in Q(sqrt(3),sqrt(74),sqrt(191),i), rebuilding all unit phases
from the point-set definition. All diagonal moments here reduce to rationals.

The supplied fixed_t_moments.json contains mu_G,k for k=0,...,32 at t=1/2.
The script can rebuild it from the geometric phases. A separate direct
site-convolution calculation agrees through order five. Every retained moment
is also checked for positivity, the support bound

  mu_G,k+1 <= 3969 mu_G,k,

and moment log-convexity

  mu_G,k^2 <= mu_G,k-1 mu_G,k+1.

Those latter checks are consistency checks; the partition identity is the
justification for the higher-order calculation.

Put R_G,k(eta)=E[I_G^k exp(-eta I_G)]. Taylor's theorem gives, for every x>=0,

  sum_{j=0..2r+1} (-x)^j/j! <= exp(-x)
       <= sum_{j=0..2r} (-x)^j/j!.

The sign follows from the Lagrange remainder and holds even when successive
Taylor terms are not decreasing. Multiply by I^k and take expectations. Every
odd partial sum is a lower bound for R_G,k, and every even partial sum is an
upper bound. The calculation takes the largest available lower bound and the
smallest available upper bound, also imposing 0<=R_G,k<=mu_G,k.

The count probabilities then satisfy

  p_G(k) = eta^k R_G,k(eta)/k!.

Thus rational eta and rational t give rational enclosures without quadrature.
The output uses outward rational rounding to a grid of spacing 10^-90; this
rounding only widens the inequalities. Descriptive decimal strings are not
used as premises of any assertion.

For a cutoff K, the remaining mass has the exact upper bound

  P_G(N>=K+1) <= eta^(K+1) mu_G,K+1/(K+1)!.

Indeed 1{N>=K+1} <= (N)_(K+1)/(K+1)!, and Poisson factorial moments conditional
on I equal (eta I)^(K+1). This controls the entire infinite count tail.

2. FINITE TV AND HELLINGER CERTIFICATION
--------------------------------------
Use the convention

  h^2(P,Q)=sum_k (sqrt(p(k))-sqrt(q(k)))^2,
  rho(P,Q)=sum_k sqrt(p(k)q(k))=1-h^2/2,
  TV(P,Q)=(1/2)sum_k |p(k)-q(k)|.

For intervals a=[a_lo,a_hi], b=[b_lo,b_hi] enclosing one pair of probabilities,
the signed gap lies in [a_lo-b_hi,a_hi-b_lo]. Its absolute value lies between
max(0,a_lo-b_hi,b_lo-a_hi) and max(|a_lo-b_hi|,|a_hi-b_lo|).

To enclose each Hellinger summand, use

  (sqrt(p)-sqrt(q))^2=(p-q)^2/(p+q+2sqrt(pq)).

The denominator is increasing in both p and q. Rational square-root bounds
are obtained by integer square root on a grid of spacing 2^-200. Squaring each
endpoint verifies the bounds exactly. If the denominator lower bound is zero,
the valid upper bound p+q is used instead.

Sum the enclosures for k=0,...,K. The omitted Hellinger contribution is at most
P_A(N>K)+P_B(N>K), and the omitted TV contribution is at most half that sum.

At t=1/2, s=2 log 2, eta=1/512, and K=24, the sum of the two tail bounds is
less than 1.030e-26. The exact output supplies the following outward summaries:

  2.40263644590220e-17 < h^2(full counts) < 2.40263644700612e-17,
  3.65527413391791e-10 < TV(full counts)  < 3.65527413391793e-10.

These are enclosures of the full count-distribution distances at a specified
FINITE positive phase variance. They are not just leading small-noise terms
or selected-moment lower bounds.

For Y=1{N=4}, the Bernoulli squared Hellinger distance has two terms, for Y=1
and Y=0. Applying the same interval calculation gives

  2.22744243231592e-19 < h^2(four-count indicator) < 2.22744243231593e-19.

The full-count/four-count h^2 ratio is enclosed by

  107.8652543852 < ratio < 107.8652544348.

This is a ratio of the specified squared Hellinger separations. It is not a
claim about mutual information or a universal efficiency ratio.

3. NECESSARY AND SUFFICIENT FINITE SAMPLE BOUNDS
----------------------------------------------
With equal priors and m independent observations, optimal success equals
(1+TV(P^m,Q^m))/2. Product affinity is rho^m. The elementary bounds

  1-rho^m <= TV(P^m,Q^m) <= sqrt(1-rho^(2m))

follow respectively from min(p,q)<=sqrt(pq) and Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
|p-q|=|sqrt(p)-sqrt(q)|(sqrt(p)+sqrt(q)).

Suppose L<=h^2<=U with 0<L<=U<2. To attain success at least 19/20, the right
bound requires rho^(2m)<=19/100. Since rho>=1-U/2, and
-log(1-x)<=x/(1-x) for 0<=x<1, a necessary condition is

  m >= [log(100/19) (1-U/2)]/U.

Since rho<=1-L/2<=exp(-L/2), a sufficient condition for the optimal rule is

  m >= 2 log(10)/L.

The implementation uses rational constants

  830365/500000 < log(100/19),
  log(10) < 230259/100000.

Both are certified by rational Taylor bounds for the exponential, so no
floating-point logarithm controls an integer threshold. Taking ceilings gives:

  Data retained        Necessary m at least       Sufficient m
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Full photon counts   69,121,152,393,630,388       191,671,944,702,842,206
  Only 1{N=4}          7,455,770,689,765,032,714   20,674,743,073,884,456,509

The necessary bound in the second row concerns FOUR-COUNT-ONLY observations;
it must not be applied to an observer retaining the full counts. The first
row concerns the full distributions. These intervals do not determine the
exact optimal sample count. They also do not predict an acquisition time.

The large counts are a consequence of this illustrative mathematical operating
point. They do not establish that all operating points are similarly difficult,
and no practical experiment is recommended on their basis.

An explicit simple rule, with no exact likelihood ratio required
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
At this operating point p_A(4)<p_B(4). Put

  theta=(upper_bound(p_A(4))+lower_bound(p_B(4)))/2,

whose value is about 0.0000462384885924556627812842839232. Given m snapshots,
choose B when the observed fraction of N=4 exceeds theta and A otherwise.
Let d be the certified positive lower bound for p_B(4)-p_A(4), and let u bound
both probabilities from above. Each hypothesis is separated from theta by at
least d/2. For a centered Bernoulli variable X, |X|<=1 and E[X^2]<=u. Since
k!>=2*3^(k-2) for k>=2,

  log E exp(lambda X) <= u lambda^2/[2(1-lambda/3)],  0<=lambda<3.

This follows by expanding the exponential, bounding higher absolute moments
by E[X^2], and applying log(1+y)<=y. The Chernoff optimization then yields

  P(error | either configuration)
    <= exp[-m d^2/(8u+4d/3)].

The exact integer 26,937,971,154,603,499,910 makes the exponent at least 3.
A rational Taylor lower bound proves exp(3)>20. Thus EACH conditional error
is below 1/20. This concrete test has a somewhat larger sufficient count than
the optimal likelihood-ratio guarantee, as expected.

4. A CERTIFIED FOUR-COUNT BLIND GAIN
----------------------------------
Fix t=1/2. Define

  F(eta)=R_A,4(eta)-R_B,4(eta),
  p_A(4)-p_B(4)=eta^4 F(eta)/24.

Because intensities are bounded, these functions are real analytic in eta.
At eta=0, F(0)=D_4>0. The exact interval computation gives a positive count-four
gap at eta=1/640 and a negative gap at eta=1/512. At the latter point,

  -6.418373913380575e-12 < p_A(4)-p_B(4) < -6.418373913380574e-12.

Continuity therefore gives a positive blind gain between those endpoints.
Rational bisection and the moment bounds isolate a zero in the exact interval

  [61217055127150107/36893488147419103232,
   38260659454468817/23058430092136939520],

which is contained in the outward decimal interval

  [0.0016592916040505258470, 0.0016592916040505258525].

This zero is also SIMPLE and UNIQUE WITHIN THE EXACT RATIONAL INTERVAL above;
the decimal interval is only an outward display. Differentiate:

  F'(eta)=-R_A,5(eta)+R_B,5(eta).

Each R_G,5 decreases in eta because its derivative is -R_G,6<=0. For an interval
[a,b], evaluating R_G,5 at a and b therefore gives a uniform enclosure of F'
throughout the interval, without assuming F' itself is monotone. The saved
certificate establishes the strict uniform bounds

  -41829.301943402 < F'(eta) < -41829.301943351 < 0.

The strict endpoint signs plus F'<0 establish exactly one simple zero there.
No claim is made about the number of zeros elsewhere on the positive gain axis.

At this gain, Y=1{N=4} has EXACTLY identical Bernoulli distributions for A and B.
Any number of independent Y observations is therefore uninformative: equal-
prior success cannot exceed 1/2. The full count distributions remain different,
because their fourth factorial moments differ by eta^4 D_4>0. Thus there is no
conflict with full-detector identifiability.

This result precisely limits the earlier four-count conclusion. That conclusion
was an s->0 theorem with fixed gain. The blind point occurs at fixed finite s
while gain varies; uniform finite-parameter optimality would be false.

5. A FINITE-s SIX-MOMENT GUARANTEE WITH THE SHARP SMALL-s POWER
-------------------------------------------------------------
The scalar inequality

  1-x <= exp(-x) <= 1-x+x^2/2,  x>=0,

gives the following exact lower bound for the four-count probability difference:

  p_A(4)-p_B(4) >= L(s),

  L(s) = eta^4/24 [D_4(s)-eta D_5(s)-eta^2 mu_B,6(s)/2].

For the difference, the A remainder is nonnegative and the B remainder is at
most eta^2 mu_B,6/2, which is why only the B sixth moment appears in this lower
bound. A corresponding upper bound uses +eta^2 mu_A,6/2. Also,

  p_A(4),p_B(4) <= U(s) = eta^4 max(mu_A,4,mu_B,4)/24.

Whenever L(s)>0, the four-count Hellinger contribution satisfies

  h^2 >= (sqrt(p_A(4))-sqrt(p_B(4)))^2
       >= L(s)^2/[4 U(s)].

The same lower bound applies to full counts by retaining this one nonnegative
summand. Therefore an entirely nonasymptotic sufficient sample number is

  m >= ceil[8 U(s) log(10)/L(s)^2]

for 95% equal-prior success of the optimal four-count test. The implementation
replaces log(10) by its proved rational upper bound.

As s->0 with fixed eta>0, the established moment facts give

  D_4=c5 s^5+O(s^6),  c5=455015761/1250,
  D_5=O(s^6),  mu_B,6=O(s^6),
  mu_G,4=m4 s^4+O(s^5),  m4=75408578015121/160000.

Thus L(s)>0 eventually and

  L(s)^2/[4 U(s)]
    = eta^4 c5^2/(96 m4) s^6 + O(s^7).

The finite-s bound therefore recovers the same leading Hellinger coefficient
as the previously proved exact small-s asymptotic, and its sample guarantee
has the sharp s^-6 order. It does not merely use a variance-insensitive
Hoeffding bound, which would lose this power for rare four-count events.

For a concrete finite point, choose t=999/1000, s=-2 log(999/1000), eta=1/512.
Exact moments through order six prove the outward summaries

  L(s) > 1.46925051894049e-21,
  U(s) < 4.55932474093031e-15,
  h^2(four-count) > 1.18367149197977e-28.

The integer 38,905,896,029,459,193,642,493,650,866 is sufficient under this
conservative six-moment bound for 95% optimal four-count success. This is a
proof of a finite parameter guarantee, not a practical sample recommendation.

This supplement proves the count transformation, finite enclosures, sample
inequalities, and blind-gain statements. It does not establish physical
