WHAT THE EXTENSION HAS CLOSED

1. The common-null cancellation is broader than Gaussian motion. For any
   common iid unit-circle phase law, the first three intensity moments of
   the specified homometric pair agree exactly. The fourth gap is

     Delta4 = 6 Re[kappa40 conjugate(kappa20)^2 (T_A-T_B)],
     T_G = M_G,4 conjugate(M_G,2)^2.

   The complex conjugation matters for asymmetric phase laws. Exact
   asymmetric and two-point examples check the formula. A biased two-point
   law can reverse the fourth-gap sign; its exceptional fourth-gap zero
   does not by itself prove equality of the full intensity laws.

2. Wrapped-Cauchy phases give a different selection order. The first six
   intensity moments agree, and the first difference is exactly moment seven:

     Delta7 = -(22295772289/1000) r^8 (1-r^2)^3 < 0,  0<r<1.

   The all-parameter statement has an analytic charge-budget proof and exact
   polynomial checks. Uniform phases erase the complete single-frequency
   distinction for equal site counts. The seventh-moment theorem excludes
   that endpoint and the deterministic endpoint.

3. Within the two specified centered phase families, the first three
   population intensity moments identify Gaussian versus wrapped Cauchy
   and its width parameter. The sole interior second-moment collision is
   resolved by a strictly nonzero third-moment difference. After identifying
   the family, moment four or seven identifies which structure generated
   the law. A common phase rotation remains unobservable in intensity.

4. A quantified neighborhood of the Gaussian law preserves the result.
   At Gaussian phase variance s=log(2), any common iid phase law whose first
   four circular moments differ by at most 1/100000 retains exact equality
   through intensity order three and a fourth gap at least

     29505367039/81920000 = 360.17293748779296875 > 0.

   A sufficient stronger condition is single-site phase-law total variation
   at most 1/200000. These are conservative tolerances with fixed geometry
   and frequency, not maximal tolerances or instrument error budgets.

5. Any COMMON independent additive error preserves distinctness of bounded
   intensity laws, even if that error has no moments. Within Gaussian
   readout Y=b+eta I+sigma Z, the complete law identifies offset, positive
   gain, Gaussian noise scale, phase family/width, and structure. Within
   Poisson readout N|I~Poisson(b+eta I), it identifies nonnegative background,
   positive gain, phase family/width, and structure. These statements use
   exact population laws and the full intensity support [0,3969].

   An unrestricted unknown additive-error family is different: two distinct
   independent zero-mean errors with equal variance can make the competing
   outputs identical. No finite sample size works uniformly over all allowed
   Gaussian noise scales or arbitrarily small positive Poisson gains.

6. A finite zero-background Poisson point now has rigorous FULL count-law
   bounds: Gaussian phase variance s=2 log(2), t=1/2, gain eta=1/512.
   The full-count squared Hellinger distance is about 2.402636446e-17.
   At equal priors, 95% optimal success requires at least
   69,121,152,393,630,388 independent snapshots, while
   191,671,944,702,842,206 are sufficient. The exact optimal sample count
   lies between these bounds and is not determined. Keeping only whether
   N=4 loses a factor of about 107.865 in squared Hellinger separation at
   this point; that is not an exact sample-count ratio.

7. The four-count statistic has a certified blind gain near
   0.00165929160405052585 at the same Gaussian phase variance. A simple zero
   is unique INSIDE the exact rational interval recorded in the certificate.
   At that gain the Bernoulli four-count laws are exactly identical, so any
   number of those binary observations has equal-prior success at most one
   half. The full count laws remain distinct by their fourth factorial
   moments. Global uniqueness of the blind gain is not claimed.

8. A six-intensity-moment inequality now gives a genuinely finite-s
   four-count sample guarantee whenever its certified lower gap is positive.
   It recovers the earlier sharp s^-6 sample order at fixed positive gain
   as s tends to zero. Its finite demonstration is conservative and requires
   an enormous number of samples; it is not an experiment recommendation.

MODEL RESTRICTIONS

The construction has two fixed known 63-site structures, unit scattering
weights, and common observation frequency q0=pi/3. Intensity means
I=|sum_j z_j W_j|^2 without division by n or n^2, so 0<=I<=3969. The phase
variables are independent copies of one law, used identically in both
structures. Gaussian s is phase variance and t=exp(-s/2); wrapped-Cauchy r
is a concentration parameter, not a displacement variance.

Joint detector identification applies to finite positive Gaussian s or
0<r<1 in the wrapped-Cauchy family, known n and geometry candidates,
positive gain, and the specified independent Gaussian or conditional
Poisson readout. Gaussian sigma may be zero and Poisson background may be
zero. It does not identify an arbitrary unknown phase law or a common
phase angle. The finite sample numbers use Gaussian phases, zero Poisson
background, known competing calibrated models, and independent snapshots
conditional on the unknown structure. Unknown-parameter population
identifiability does not supply finite-sample calibration guarantees.
