AN EXPLICIT NEIGHBORHOOD OF PHASE-LAW ROBUSTNESS
20 October 2026

This result uses the arbitrary-iid-phase selection identity proved in
phase_laws. It concerns a COMMON phase law used at every site of both exact
structures. It does not permit unrelated site-specific perturbations.

Write mu_k=E[W^k], |W|=1, and let nu_k be the corresponding moments of a
second common phase law. Suppose

  max_{1<=k<=4} |mu_k-nu_k| <= delta.

Let k2(mu)=mu2-mu1^2 and

  k4(mu)=mu4-4mu3*mu1-3mu2^2+12mu2*mu1^2-6mu1^4.

For either phase law, |k2|<=1: the modulus of E[(W-EW)^2] is bounded by
E|W-EW|^2=1-|EW|^2. Moreover |k4|<=7, because

  k4=E[(W-EW)^4]-3 k2^2,
  E|W-EW|^4 <=4 E|W-EW|^2 <=4.

Products of d factors, each of modulus at most one, change by at most
d*delta when the individual moments change by delta; telescope the product
one factor at a time. Hence

  |k2(mu)-k2(nu)| <=3 delta,
  |k4(mu)-k4(nu)| <=(1+8+6+36+24)delta=75 delta.

For alpha=k4*conjugate(k2)^2 this gives

  |alpha(mu)-alpha(nu)| <=75 delta +7*2*3 delta=117 delta.

The fixed structural quantity is

  T = M_A4 conjugate(M_A2)^2 - M_B4 conjugate(M_B2)^2
    = -455015761/120000
      + i sqrt(3) * 28907930179/4800000.

The general phase-law theorem states D4(mu)=6 Re[alpha(mu) T]. Thus

  |D4(mu)-D4(nu)| <=702 |T| delta.                 (R1)

This bound is valid for asymmetric as well as symmetric phase laws.

For an explicit finite margin, take the reference phase law to be Gaussian
with phase variance s=log 2. Then x=exp(-s)=1/2 and

  D4(reference)=35946245119/81920000.

Exact rational arithmetic proves |T|<11200. Consequently any common phase
law satisfying delta<=1/100000 preserves the strict distinction with

  D4(perturbed) >= 35946245119/81920000 - 702*11200/100000
                = 29505367039/81920000
                = 360.17293748779296875 >0.         (R2)

The rational constant in (R2) is independently checked by the accompanying
script; the fraction is authoritative if a displayed decimal is rounded.

The exact first-three-moment equalities also survive, because the general
selection theorem holds for every common iid phase law with these fixed
structural power sums. This is therefore a quantitative robustness
statement for both the cancellation pattern and the nonzero fourth gap.

As a sufficient condition in a familiar probability metric, if the total
variation distance between the reference and perturbed SINGLE-SITE phase
laws is at most 1/200000, then |mu_k-nu_k|<=2 TV<=1/100000 for every k.
The moment inequality follows from integration against a signed measure
whose total mass variation is 2 TV and from |W^k|=1. The Fourier-moment
condition is more general than this sufficient TV condition.

Limitations. The constants are conservative guarantees, not a maximal
tolerance or a calibrated experimental error budget. The theorem keeps
the geometry, common frequency, iid property, and equality of the phase
law across both structures fixed. It addresses misspecification of that
common phase law, not arbitrary structural or detector errors.
