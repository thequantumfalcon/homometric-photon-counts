Analytic checks

1. For |W|=1 and m=E W, |kappa20|=|E(W-m)^2|<=E|W-m|^2=1-|m|^2<=1.
2. Since |W-m|<=2, E|W-m|^4<=4E|W-m|^2<=4. Hence
   |kappa40|<=E|W-m|^4+3|kappa20|^2<=7.
3. Telescoping products of unit-disk Fourier moments gives the stated
   coefficient bounds: kappa20 changes by at most 3*delta; kappa40 changes
   by at most (1+8+6+36+24)*delta=75*delta.
4. For alpha=kappa40*conj(kappa20)^2, changing kappa40 first and then the
   squared second cumulant bounds the change by 75*delta+7*2*3*delta
   =117*delta. The complex conjugation is correctly retained.
5. The general phase-law selection theorem then gives
   |Delta4(mu)-Delta4(nu)|<=6*117*|T|*delta=702*|T|*delta.
6. For a signed difference of probability measures, its total mass variation
   is 2*TV. Since |W^k|=1, Fourier errors are at most 2*TV, including for
   complex Fourier moments. The claimed sufficient single-site TV tolerance
   is therefore valid.
7. The first-three-moment equalities survive exactly because the structural
   null and magnitude conditions are fixed and the new law remains common
   and iid across all sites. This would not cover unrelated site-wise errors.

Scope

The tolerance is conservative and concerns a common iid phase law at fixed
geometry/frequency. It is not a maximal tolerance, a detector calibration
budget, or a claim about independent geometric perturbations. The proof
correctly states those limitations.
