5. JOINT UNKNOWN DETECTOR IDENTIFIABILITY, INCLUDING BOTH PHASE FAMILIES

The compact-signal/common-error convolution theorem needs no error
moments. Local nonvanishing of the error characteristic function follows
from continuity at zero; compact support makes the signal difference
entire, and the complex identity theorem completes the argument.

Both interior Gaussian and wrapped-Cauchy phase laws have full support
on the circle. Translating by clean phases preserves the torus support.
The continuous intensity map has compact connected image [0,n^2], and
every neighborhood of an image point has positive probability. Thus the
same exact support lemma applies to BOTH phase families.

For Gaussian readout, the quadratic MGF growth identifies sigma^2
because the compact affine signal contributes only linear log-MGF growth.
This includes sigma=0. Gaussian deconvolution identifies the affine signal
law. Its support endpoints identify b and positive eta, because n is known.
For Poisson readout, equality of count laws identifies the bounded mixing
law by its entire Laplace transform; its support endpoints likewise
identify background b and positive eta, including b=0.

The important sequence is therefore:
  equal complete output laws -> equal detector nuisance parameters and
  equal intensity laws -> equal means -> equal x -> equal first three
  moments -> same phase family -> same width -> same geometry.

The cross-family third-moment certificate rules out the sole candidate
second-moment collision; the strictly nonzero fourth/seventh gap then
rules out an A/B alias within the identified family. Thus the new report's
strict uniqueness across family, width, structure, and the specified
Gaussian or Poisson detector parameters is justified. A common phase
rotation is expressly not included among identifiable parameters.

The nonuniform-performance examples and unknown-unrestricted-error
counterexample are also sound. The Gaussian TV bound has the correct
1/sqrt(2*pi) factor; the Poisson coupling bound is valid. Letting noise
grow or gain decrease shows absence of a single finite sample size that
works uniformly over all allowed nuisance parameters. The swapped
independent-error construction creates identical output laws with
zero-mean equal-variance errors, but uses different error laws under the
two competing explanations. The report clearly distinguishes that
obstruction from injectivity under one common error law.
