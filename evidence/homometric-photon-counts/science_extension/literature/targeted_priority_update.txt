The precise homogeneous-noise homometry theorem still has unresolved

1. WEBSTER ET AL. 2003: PREVIOUS ABSTRACT-ONLY GAP CLOSED

Mark A. Webster, Kevin J. Webb, Andrew M. Weiner, Junying Xu, Hui Cao,
"Temporal response of a random medium from speckle intensity frequency
correlations," JOSA A 20(11), 2057-2070 (2003).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAA.20.002057
Author-hosted full text: https://www.eng.yale.edu/caolab/papers/josaa03.pdf
Read: sections 2A-2C, section 3, and relevant Appendix A assumptions.

The model explicitly passes to large N, uses independent uniformly
distributed phases, and obtains jointly circular complex Gaussian fields.
Section 3 relates a THIRD intensity correlation at MULTIPLE frequencies to
twice the real bispectrum of a temporal response. Equation (33) gives the
usual product of two Fourier amplitudes and a conjugate at their sum.
This confirms strong precedent for accessing phase invariants through
intensity fluctuations. Its inspected derivation uses different assumptions
and observable from the project's finite-N, single-frequency fourth raw
intensity moment. It cannot be cited as experimental verification of the
project's constructed geometries or Gaussian displacement model.

2. SHIMIZU 2025: FINITE PHASOR FRAMEWORK CONFIRMED

Kenji Shimizu, Ocean Science 21, 2233-2253 (2025).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.5194/os-21-2233-2025
Full text:
https://os.copernicus.org/articles/21/2233/2025/os-21-2233-2025.html
Read in this pass: introduction's finite/nonidentical model scope; Appendix A,
especially equations A3 and A14; explanatory discussion of wrapped-normal
versus von Mises phases.

Appendix A handles a finite number of independent waves with individual
deterministic phase offsets and phase spreads. Equation A14 supplies a
Fourier-Bessel characteristic function with wrapped-normal Fourier
coefficients exp(-k^2 sigma_j^2/2); equation A3 combines component laws.
Setting all amplitudes to one, all variances equal, and phase offsets to the
structural phases specializes this framework to our random-phasor model.
At the common null the total centering shift cancels. That specialization is
our inference from the displayed formulas. It rules out a claim that finite
wrapped-normal phasor calculation is a new general method of this project.
No homometric moment-selection theorem was identified in the passages read.

3. ABDI, HASHEMI AND NADER-ESFAHANI 2000: ADDITIONAL FINITE-SUM PRECEDENT

Ali Abdi, Homayoun Hashemi, Said Nader-Esfahani, "On the PDF of the Sum of
Random Vectors," IEEE Transactions on Communications 48(1), 7-12 (2000).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/26.818866
Author-hosted version: https://web.njit.edu/~abdi/srv.pdf
Read: introduction's assumptions/classification; derivation scope;
conclusion and bibliography relevant to Barakat.

The paper organizes prior finite-random-vector methods and develops exact
envelope representations and moment-based Laguerre calculations. Its main
development explicitly focuses on deterministic N and uniform phase laws.
Consequently its general phrase "random vectors" does not by itself
establish the project's nonuniform-phase selection rule. The inspected
paper provides another reason to treat moment recurrences, exact finite
phasor sums, and alternatives to Monte Carlo as established techniques.

5. POISSON MIXTURE INJECTIVITY IS ESTABLISHED

Theofanis Sapatinas, "Identifiability of mixtures of power-series
distributions and related characterizations," Annals of the Institute of
Statistical Mathematics 47(3), 447-459 (1995).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00773394
Official full text: https://www.ism.ac.jp/editsec/aism/pdf/047_3_0447.pdf
Read: section 2, Theorems 2.1 and 2.2, Note 1, and applicable remarks.

The paper proves identifiability criteria for arbitrary power-series
mixtures and explicitly identifies Poisson mixtures as satisfying them.
The argument also subsumes finite and countable mixing laws. This is an
accessible primary proof of the principle used by the project's Poisson
detector extension. Recoverability of the mixing-rate distribution from
the complete count law is not a new theorem claimed by this project.

For historical orientation, Henry Teicher, "Identifiability of Mixtures,"
Ann. Math. Statist. 32(1), 244-248 (1961),
https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177705155, establishes identifiability for
additively closed families in its publisher abstract. Its direct full-text
routes were blocked by an unavailable page during this pass. We do not
attribute an unread detailed proof to it. Sapatinas supplies the inspected
primary mathematical support needed here.

6. UNKNOWN GAUSSIAN READOUT: ESTABLISHED DECONVOLUTION SETTING

Catherine Matias, "Semiparametric deconvolution with unknown noise variance,"
ESAIM: Probability and Statistics 6, 271-292 (2002).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1051/ps:2002015
Author full text: https://cmatias.perso.math.cnrs.fr/Docs/psns5.pdf
Read: pages 272-275, model definition, no-Gaussian-component restriction,
and compact-support discussion; not a complete audit of every minimax proof.

The paper studies independent centered Gaussian measurement error with
unknown variance. Its signal class excludes a Gaussian convolution
component, which makes that variance identifiable. Compact support is a
particularly transparent way to exclude such a component. Therefore the
project's compact-support argument identifying Gaussian variance is an
elementary specialization of established deconvolution reasoning. Its
application to the complete structural/nuisance parameter family still
requires the project's separate support and intensity calculations.

Alexander Meister, "Deconvolving compactly supported densities,"
Mathematical Methods of Statistics 16, 63-76 (2007),
https://doi.org/10.3103/S106653070701005X, further confirms the established
role of compact support in relaxing noise Fourier-transform assumptions.
Only its publisher abstract was available in this pass. This is background,
not the source of an uninspected claim of arbitrary-noise injectivity.

7. UNKNOWN ARBITRARY NOISE: DO NOT CONFUSE TWO EXPERIMENTS

Jeremie Capitao-Miniconi, Elisabeth Gassiat and Luc Lehericy,
"Deconvolution of repeated measurements corrupted by unknown noise,"
arXiv:2409.02014v1 (3 September 2024).
Full author preprint: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02014
Read: introduction, section 2 assumptions and Theorem 1; analytic-function
observation immediately preceding the theorem. The full estimation proof
was not audited here.

Their experiment makes two noisy measurements of the SAME latent signal,
with independent error coordinates. Under the stated analytic-tail
condition on the signal, they establish joint identifiability up to
translation without nonvanishing restrictions on error characteristic
functions. Compact support satisfies the signal-tail condition. This
demonstrates that analytic deconvolution and unknown-noise identifiability
are active established subjects. It does not license an unknown arbitrary
noise claim for one readout of each independently changing intensity.

8. WHAT THIS UPDATE CHANGES AND DOES NOT CHANGE

The old statement that Webster 2003 could only be inspected at abstract
level is superseded. The finite-phasor and bispectral method precedents
are now more sharply separated from the project's exact theorem. The
detector extension can cite accessible primary mathematical sources for
Poisson mixtures and unknown Gaussian variance. No new historical priority
claim follows from any of these improvements.

The project's own proofs should stand independently: cumulant charge
classification for moment selection; explicit geometry and invariant;
support and analytic-transform arguments for nuisance identifiability;
and probability inequalities for finite-sample bounds. Existing general
tools should be cited as such. The candidate contribution is the precise
combination and its explicit examples, pending comparison against the
remaining closely related full texts and external mathematical review.

This is a targeted follow-up, not an exhaustive systematic review, a patent
search, experimental validation, or proof of universal priority. No external
messages were sent.

9. AINSLEIGH 2017: DIRECT PRECEDENT FOR THE MOMENT-COMPUTATION FRAMEWORK

Phillip Ainsleigh, "A Method for Computing Moments of Quadratic Forms
Involving Wrapped Random Variables," SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and
Applications 38(2), 554-573 (2017).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082019
Publisher: https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/16M1082019
Read depth: publisher abstract and bibliography; the publisher-supplied
first-page preview hosted at
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317704412_A_Method_for_Computing_Moments_of_Quadratic_Forms_Involving_Wrapped_Random_Variables
The page explicitly labels this as a preview supplied by SIAM. It is only
page 554, not the complete article. No ResearchGate user commentary is used.

The abstract describes analytical higher moments of quadratic forms in
independent wrapped variables using structured matrices, characteristic
functions, and symmetries that reduce the computation. It includes wrapped
normal examples. This is a directly relevant general computational
predecessor. The full publisher PDF endpoint returned a retrieval error;
targeted title, DOI, author, arXiv, and institutional-domain searches did not
locate an accessible author or institutional full text in this pass.

The following specialization is our own algebraic comparison, not a claim
about an unread equation in Ainsleigh. Write the project phase vector as
W=(exp(i theta_1),...,exp(i theta_n))^T and the structural coefficients as
z=(z_1,...,z_n)^T. Let a=conjugate(z), with * denoting conjugate transpose.
Then

  I=|z^T W|^2=|a^* W|^2=W^* (a a^*) W.

Thus our intensity is exactly a Hermitian positive-semidefinite rank-one
quadratic form in independent wrapped variables. Its p-th raw moment is a
higher moment of precisely this type of quadratic form. This makes it
particularly inappropriate to claim that computing finite wrapped-phase
intensity moments analytically is itself a new general method.

The algebraic specialization by itself does not establish whether the
paper contains the common-null, two-structure cancellation theorem. That
requires full-text comparison. Neither the abstract nor the first-page
preview establishes or excludes the exact equal-first-three/different-fourth
homometry result, the diagonal-bispectrum coefficient, or the arbitrary-iid
phase-law selection formula. This is an additional unresolved full-text
priority comparison, alongside the Barakat papers. Do not claim the exact
theorem is absent from the unread sections.
