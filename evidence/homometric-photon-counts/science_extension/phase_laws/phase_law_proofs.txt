PHASE-LAW EXTENSION: WHEN THE FIRST DISTINGUISHING MOMENT CHANGES
20 October 2026

Status: exact ideal-model theorems and reproducible internal verification.
This document makes no publication-priority or experimental-realization claim.
The geometry is the same 63-site homometric pair used in the completed record.

MAIN FINDINGS

1. The first three intensity moments agree at the common clean null for ANY
   common iid unit-circle phase law, without Gaussianity or reflection symmetry.
   The exact fourth-moment difference is a real part of a complex cumulant
   coefficient times a structural Fourier invariant.

2. The first differing moment depends on the noise law. Symmetric two-point
   phases distinguish this pair at intensity moment four. Poisson-kernel
   (wrapped-Cauchy) phases match exactly through moment six and first differ
   at moment seven, at every nondegenerate finite parameter 0<r<1.

3. For wrapped-Cauchy phases, the exact seventh-moment difference is

       -(22295772289/1000) r^8 (1-r^2)^3 < 0,  0<r<1.

   The sign is opposite to the fourth-moment difference under Gaussian phases.
   This does not compare the signs of the SAME statistic: the moments are
   different, so no ordering between the full intensity laws is implied.

4. Uniform independent phases remove all geometric information from the full
   intensity distribution at one frequency, for any point configurations with
   the same number of unit-weight sites. No universal claim that arbitrary
   independent noise distinguishes homometry is therefore possible.

1. NOTATION AND ASSUMPTIONS

Let z_(G,j) be unit complex clean phases, G=A or B, j=1,...,n. Put

  M_(G,k) = sum_j z_(G,j)^k,  M_(G,0)=n,
  T_G = M_(G,4) conjugate(M_(G,2))^2.

Let W_1,...,W_n be independent copies of the same unit-circle random variable W.
Use exactly the same law of W at every site of either configuration. Define

  F_G = sum_j z_(G,j) W_j,   I_G = |F_G|^2.

All moments exist and 0<=I_G<=n^2. Assume the common clean null

  M_(A,1)=M_(B,1)=0.

Clean homometry at every frequency implies |M_(A,k)|=|M_(B,k)| for every integer
k. The individual theorems below need only the explicitly stated finite subset
of these magnitude equalities. They do not require Gaussian phase errors.

Define joint cumulants kappa_(a,b) of a copies of W and b copies of conjugate(W)
through the formal moment-cumulant relation, equivalently the coefficients of

  log E exp(u W+v conjugate(W)).

The logarithm exists near u=v=0 because W is bounded. Deterministic scaling
and independence give the cumulants of the field sum:

  K_(G,a,b) = kappa_(a,b) M_(G,a-b).

Complex conjugation gives kappa_(b,a)=conjugate(kappa_(a,b)). A mixed cumulant
has charge a-b. In a partition contributing to E[F_G^p conjugate(F_G)^p],
all charges sum to zero. A charge +1 or -1 block vanishes at the clean null.

2. GENERAL IID PHASE-LAW SELECTION THEOREM

THEOREM 1. If |M_(A,k)|=|M_(B,k)| for k=2,3,4, then

  E I_A^p = E I_B^p, p=0,1,2,3,

and, without any symmetry assumption on W,

  E I_A^4 - E I_B^4
    = 6 Re[ kappa_(4,0) conjugate(kappa_(2,0))^2 (T_A-T_B) ].       (1)

In terms of the circular Fourier moments a_k=E W^k,

  kappa_(2,0) = a_2-a_1^2,
  kappa_(4,0) = a_4-4a_3a_1-3a_2^2+12a_2a_1^2-6a_1^4.           (2)

Under the stated structural assumptions, the right side of (1) being nonzero
is a necessary and sufficient condition for separation by the fourth moment.
If it is zero, (1) does not decide equality of the full intensity laws.

PROOF. At p<=3, the nonzero charge multiset can only be empty, (+2,-2), or
(+3,-3), apart from zero-charge blocks. Their structural factors depend only
on n, |M_2|^2, and |M_3|^2. The cumulant coefficients depend on the common law
of W and are identical in the two models, so every contribution agrees.

At p=4 the complete list of nonzero charge multisets is

  empty; (+2,-2); (+3,-3); (+4,-4);
  (+2,+2,-2,-2); (+4,-2,-2); (-4,+2,+2).

The first five depend only on common magnitudes. For (+4,-2,-2), all four
positive slots are in one block and the four negative slots are paired into
two unordered blocks. There are three such pairings. The resulting term is

  3 kappa_(4,0) kappa_(0,2)^2 T_G.

The opposite orientation contributes its complex conjugate. Subtracting A and
B proves (1). Expanding ordinary second and fourth cumulants proves (2).

If the law of W is invariant under complex conjugation, every a_k and every
kappa_(a,b) is real. Then (1) reduces to

  Delta_4 = 6 kappa_(4,0) kappa_(2,0)^2 D,
  D = Re(T_A-T_B).                                               (3)

A common rotation of the phase law does not change intensity: replacing W
by exp(i alpha)W multiplies the entire field by exp(i alpha). Formula (1)
reflects this, because kappa_(4,0) and conjugate(kappa_(2,0))^2 acquire
opposite phase factors.

3. THE CONCRETE STRUCTURAL INVARIANT

For the existing 63-site pair at q0=pi/3, exact reconstruction gives

  D = -455015761/120000,

and the FULL complex difference is

  T_A-T_B = -455015761/120000
            + i sqrt(3) (28907930179/4800000).                    (4)

Thus a general asymmetric phase law can couple to the imaginary structural
invariant as well as the real one. Dropping the complex conjugates or replacing
the right side of (1) by a product of real parts would in general be wrong.

The reproducibility script rebuilds every clean phase in the exact field
Q(sqrt(3),sqrt(74),sqrt(191),i). It checks the common null and the needed
power-sum magnitudes, then computes (4). For an asymmetric three-atom law
supported on 1, i, and -1 with probabilities 1/2, 1/3, and 1/6, the coefficient
kappa_(4,0) conjugate(kappa_(2,0))^2 is genuinely nonreal. Both the cumulant
formula and a separate site-by-site convolution reproduce (1) exactly,
including the resulting sqrt(3) contribution.

4. SYMMETRIC TWO-POINT PHASES: THE FOURTH MOMENT STILL DISTINGUISHES

Let epsilon be equally likely +1 or -1 and put W=exp(i theta epsilon), for any
real theta. Since W=cos(theta)+i sin(theta) epsilon and M_1=0,

  F_G = i sin(theta) sum_j z_(G,j) epsilon_j.

The ordinary Rademacher cumulants are kappa_2(epsilon)=1 and
kappa_4(epsilon)=-2. Therefore

  kappa_(2,0)(W)=-sin(theta)^2,
  kappa_(4,0)(W)=-2 sin(theta)^4.

Equation (3) yields the exact identity

  Delta_4 = -12 sin(theta)^8 D
          = (455015761/10000) sin(theta)^8.                      (5)

The first three moments agree, and the fourth differs whenever sin(theta)!=0.
When sin(theta)=0, W is deterministic and both intensities are zero at the null.
In the small-angle parameter s=theta^2, (5) has leading order s^4. Gaussian
phase variance s instead gave a fifth-power gap. Equal variance of site noise
alone therefore does not determine the information-loss power.

4.1 A biased two-point law can reverse the fourth-moment sign

Let W take the real values +1 and -1 with probabilities (1+m)/2 and
(1-m)/2, respectively, for -1<=m<=1. Its pure cumulants are

  kappa_(2,0)=1-m^2,
  kappa_(4,0)=-2(1-m^2)(1-3m^2).

Substituting in (3) gives

  Delta_4=-12(1-m^2)^3(1-3m^2)D.                               (5b)

The first three moments still agree. For this pair with D<0, the fourth gap
is positive when m^2<1/3, negative when 1/3<m^2<1, and zero when m^2=1/3
or m^2=1. The special value m^2=1/3 cancels this fourth-moment test; it does
not prove equality of the full laws. No higher-moment conclusion is made
at that exceptional value. The reproducibility script checks the reversed
sign exactly at m=3/4, using probabilities 7/8 and 1/8.

5. POISSON-KERNEL / WRAPPED-CAUCHY PHASES

For 0<=r<1, give Theta in [-pi,pi) the density

  f_r(theta)=(1-r^2)/(2 pi (1-2r cos(theta)+r^2)),
  W=exp(i Theta).

The absolutely convergent geometric series gives

  (1-r^2)/(1-2r cos(theta)+r^2)
      = sum_(k in Z) r^|k| exp(i k theta).

Integrating term by term therefore proves

  E W^k = r^|k|,  k in Z.                                       (6)

The density is conventionally called the Poisson kernel on the circle or the
wrapped-Cauchy phase density. All conclusions below are derived directly from
(6). No property of an unwrapped Cauchy displacement variance is assumed.
The parameter r is not the Gaussian phase variance s from the earlier report.

5.1 Pure and minimally mixed cumulants

For every a>=0, E W^a=r^a, so E exp(uW)=exp(ru). Its logarithm is linear.
Consequently all pure cumulants of order at least two vanish:

  kappa_(a,0)=kappa_(0,a)=0, a>=2.                              (7)

For r>0, differentiation of the bivariate cumulant generating function once
with respect to v at v=0 gives

  [d/dv log E exp(uW+v conjugate(W))]_(v=0)
     = E[conjugate(W) exp(uW)] / E exp(uW)
     = 1/r + (r-1/r) exp(-ru).

Taking a derivatives in u at zero proves

  kappa_(a,1)=(-1)^(a+1) r^(a-1)(1-r^2), a>=1.                 (8)

The polynomial identity extends to r=0. In particular,

  kappa_(3,1)=kappa_(1,3)=r^2(1-r^2),
  kappa_(5,1)=kappa_(1,5)=r^4(1-r^2).                          (9)

5.2 A charge-budget proof of equality through order six

THEOREM 2. Suppose the common clean null holds and |M_(A,k)|=|M_(B,k)| for
k=2,3,4,5. Under the common iid phase law (6), the first six intensity moments
agree. The seventh-moment difference is exactly

  Delta_7 = E I_A^7-E I_B^7 = 5880 r^8(1-r^2)^3 D.             (10)

PROOF. A surviving charged block must have |charge|>=2, because charge-one
blocks vanish at the common null. By (7), it must also contain both signs of
slots. If its charge is q!=0, it therefore uses at least |q|+2 slots.

Suppose a partition has k charged blocks and total positive charge L, which
also equals the absolute total negative charge. These blocks use at least
2L+2k slots. Thus a necessary budget is

  p >= L+k.                                                    (11)

For a single matched pair (+q,-q), the structural factor is |M_q|^2 and
therefore agrees across A and B. The budget permits only q<=p-2.

A non-pairing pattern with three charged blocks has L>=4, with the minimal
pattern (+4,-2,-2) or its conjugate. Equation (11) requires p>=7. A pattern
with four charged blocks requires L>=4 and hence p>=8. Five or more blocks
require still more slots. Therefore at p<=6 every surviving structural factor
is a common magnitude or a product of charge-zero factors. The moments agree.

At p=7, the only potentially different patterns are (+4,-2,-2) and its
conjugate. Their minimal slot budget is exactly saturated, so there are no
additional zero-charge blocks, and the blocks have mixed sizes

  (5,1), (1,3), (1,3),

or the conjugate arrangement. For one orientation the number of labeled-slot
partitions is

  (7! 7!) / [5! (3!)^2 2!] = 2940.

An independent direct count is: choose the five positive and one negative
slots in the first block, in 21*7 ways. Two positive and six negative slots
remain. Fix the smaller label of the two positive slots and choose its three
negative partners in C(6,3)=20 ways; the other mixed block is then determined.
This gives 21*7*20=2940 without imposing an ordering on the two blocks.

The two orientations together contribute

  5880 kappa_(5,1) kappa_(1,3)^2 Re(T_G).

Equation (9), followed by subtraction, proves (10). Other allowed matched
pairs use q<=5, exactly the magnitudes assumed in the theorem.

5.3 Exact sign and the first distinguishing moment for this pair

Substituting (4) into (10) gives

  Delta_7=-(22295772289/1000) r^8(1-r^2)^3.                     (12)

For every 0<r<1 the right side is strictly negative. Since moments through
order six agree, the first differing raw intensity moment is exactly seven.
This is the fourteenth field moment, not a seventh spatial correlation.

At r=1/2 the exact gap is

  Delta_7=-601985851803/16384000.

The exact polynomial calculation reproduces the identity for every r, and a
separate direct-site convolution checks both seventh moments at r=1/2. The
direct method uses no cumulants or structural power sums.

Since the lower moments agree, the seventh central moments and seventh
cumulants also differ by exactly Delta_7.

The absolute raw moment gap has a unique interior maximum. Setting x=r^2
reduces its r-dependent factor to x^4(1-x)^3, whose derivative is

  x^3(1-x)^2(4-7x).

It is positive for 0<x<4/7 and negative for 4/7<x<1. Therefore the unique
maximum occurs at r^2=4/7. This maximizes this raw moment difference, not the
full total variation distance or the performance of any physical detector.

6. ENDPOINTS AND A NECESSARY LIMIT ON THE GENERAL CLAIM

At r=0, the phase is uniform on the circle. For any fixed unit z_j, the product
z_j W_j is again uniform, and independence is preserved. Hence the field-sum
law, and therefore the entire intensity law, depends only on n. It is exactly
the same for every geometry with n unit-weight sites, even without homometry.

At r=1, understood as the point-mass limit W=1, F_G=M_(G,1)=0. Both intensities
are zero at the clean null. A deterministic common phase rotation has the same
effect on intensity.

Thus the strict separation in (12) holds precisely in the nondegenerate
interior 0<r<1 for this pair. It does not extend to the endpoints. More broadly,
the Gaussian result cannot be strengthened to the statement that every common
iid phase law separates the pair, or that every such law first separates it
at moment four. Uniform phases are a full-law counterexample, and the
wrapped-Cauchy family is an exact higher-moment counterexample.

6.1 An explicit bounded seventh-degree distinguishing statistic

Let y=I/n^2, so 0<=y<=1, and define

  g_7(y) = (1+T_7(2y-1))/2
         = 4096y^7-14336y^6+19712y^5-13440y^4
           +4704y^3-784y^2+49y.

Here T_7(cos theta)=cos(7 theta), so 0<=g_7<=1 on [0,1]. Equality of the
first six moments gives exactly

  |E g_7(I_A/n^2)-E g_7(I_B/n^2)| = 4096 |Delta_7| / n^14.

By the bounded-test characterization of total variation, this is a lower
bound on the full intensity-law distance. At r=1/2 for n=63 it equals

  455015761/46910259941745934556172000
      approximately 9.699706665e-18.

This conservative bound certifies a positive distinction; it is not an
estimate of the actual distance or an experimental feasibility claim.
Its maximum over r occurs at the same r^2=4/7 as the raw seventh-moment gap.

6.2 Identifying which of the two phase families generated the data

For this concrete geometry, suppose the phase family is known to be either
centered wrapped Gaussian with t=exp(-s/2) in (0,1), or centered wrapped
Cauchy with r in (0,1). The first three POPULATION intensity moments identify
the family and its width parameter, even though the geometry A/B is unknown.
A common mean-angle rotation cannot be identified from intensity and is
irrelevant here.

PROOF. In both geometries the mean is n(1-t^2) for Gaussian phases or
n(1-r^2) for Cauchy phases. Equal means force t=r. Set x=t^2=r^2 and
R=|M_2|^2=129187/400, n=63. Exact expansion of the second moments gives

  E I_Gaussian^2 - E I_Cauchy^2
     = x^2(1-x) [(R-3n)-(R-n)x].                              (13)

The only interior zero is

  x0=(R-3n)/(R-n)=53587/103987.

The exact third-moment difference polynomial is

  H(x) = (29268063/400)x^2
         -(11491316033/40000)x^3
         +(14125382499/40000)x^4
         -(22229052597/160000)x^5
         +(51630667/40000)x^6
         -(107619801/80000)x^7
         -(5721533/160000)x^9.

At the sole interior second-moment collision,

  H(x0) = -25087690453913212850809554872899594124966635200
          /1421711387011936544792839799933222941958356627
        approximately -17.646120501743283 < 0.                 (14)

Therefore the two families cannot match all first three moments at any
interior width. Within each family these first three moments agree across
A and B, so the family selection is unaffected by which of the two
geometries generated the data. The mean then identifies s=-log(x) or
r=sqrt(x). Once the family is known, the fourth Gaussian moment or seventh
Cauchy moment identifies A/B by the preceding strict-gap theorems.

The accompanying verify_cross_family.py reconstructs both families' first
three moment polynomials from the geometry and cumulant recursions. It
imports no previously calculated moment outputs and checks (13)-(14) using
exact fractions. This closes population-moment identifiability within this
specific two-family model; it supplies neither a finite-sample guarantee
nor identification among every possible phase law. The uniform and
deterministic endpoints are excluded, as required by their shared laws.

7. REPRODUCIBILITY AND VERIFICATION SCOPE

Run, with assertions enabled:

  python phase_law_extension.py

For the cross-family identifiability certificate, also run

  python verify_cross_family.py

The script uses only Python's standard library and the accompanying
exact_phase_algebra.py. It recreates the geometry from its exact definition;
no rounded coordinates or sampled parameter grid are proof premises.

The output phase_law_results.json contains the exact intensity-moment
polynomials through order seven, the zero differences through order six, the
factored seventh difference, exact direct-site moments at r=1/2, asymmetric
and symmetric finite-law checks, and source hashes. Hashes identify the
checked bytes; they are not mathematical proofs of correctness.

The cross_family_results.json output separately records the first-three
moment polynomials for both families, the unique second-moment collision,
and the exact nonzero third-moment difference there.

The algebra dependency retains the previously reviewed exact geometry and
number-field arithmetic. The new cumulant recursion and the separate direct
site convolution independently compute the moments, but share those geometry
and arithmetic primitives. This is internal computational cross-checking,
not external peer review or a formal proof-assistant certification.
