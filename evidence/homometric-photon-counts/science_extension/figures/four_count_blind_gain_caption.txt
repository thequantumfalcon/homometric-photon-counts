Figure: A blind gain for the four-count statistic. Both configurations use the same iid Gaussian phase variance s=2 log(2), and the detector has zero background. The unnormalized intensity is I=|sum of 63 unit-weight phasors|^2, with 0<=I<=3969, and N conditional on I is Poisson(eta I). Panel A plots the A-minus-B probability of exactly four counts at 51 rational gains in [1/700,1/500]. Every point has a rigorous rational Taylor enclosure based on exact moments through order 32; the enclosures are too narrow to resolve at this plotting scale. The joining line is only a visual guide and supplies no additional root or global uniqueness proof. The gold vertical mark locates the separately certified exact rational root interval near eta=0.00165929160405052585; its width is much smaller than a pixel. Opposite endpoint signs and a uniform negative derivative prove a unique simple root within that exact interval. At the root, the four-count indicator has identical laws under A and B, while the complete count distributions remain different. No global uniqueness is claimed. The red point marks eta=1/512, where the gap is negative. Panel B shows certified squared Hellinger distances at eta=1/512: about 2.402636446e-17 for complete counts and 2.227442432e-19 after retaining only the four-count indicator. Their ratio lies in [107.8652543852,107.8652544348]. This is a ratio of squared Hellinger separations, not mutual information or an exact optimal sample-count ratio.
