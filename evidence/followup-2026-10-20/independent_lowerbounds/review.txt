Independent review of the fixed-bin necessary sample bound
=========================================================

The lowerbounds/proof.txt argument and its producer were reviewed without
importing the producer. No mathematical gap was found within its declared
fixed-model, fixed-gain, iid, 13-bin observation scope. The result does not
give a necessary bound for unpooled full photon counts or adaptive gains.

The affinity deficit identity uses normalization correctly. The rational
denominator lower bound 8*min(p_lower,q_lower) has the correct direction.
The chosen maximum of endpoint cross-differences bounds every difference
within the two intervals. Tensorization uses independence. Cauchy-Schwarz
gives TV<=sqrt(1-affinity^2) with the standard TV convention 1/2 L1.
Both errors strictly below 1/20 imply TV>9/10, so the strict inequalities
and the producer's integer floor plus one are correct. A randomized rule
is a function f in [0,1], which includes arbitrary tie handling; the
expectation-difference inequality still holds. The KL alternative also
handles randomized tests by data processing. Its two directions are valid.
Upward rounding of the one-gate loss weakens, rather than strengthens,
the necessary bound. The saved sufficient test has strictly smaller than
1/20 errors, so the upper endpoint matches the stated strict target.

An independent exact arithmetic route now checks the integer exclusion
without using the producer's logarithm series or floor calculation.
Let delta be its rational upper bound on 1-affinity; recomputing all four
corners of each pair of probability intervals verifies this upper bound.
Set m0=28091647285173 and z=2m0*delta/(1-delta). For 0<delta<1,

  log(1-delta)>=-delta/(1-delta),
  (1-delta)^(2m0)>=exp(-z).

Taylor's theorem for exp(-z), z>0, has a strictly positive remainder after
odd degree31: the next derivative of exp(-z) is positive. Thus the exact
rational polynomial sum_(k=0)^31 (-z)^k/k! is a strict lower bound.
The independent script verifies this polynomial exceeds 19/100. Hence
affinity(P^m,Q^m)^2>19/100 for every integer m<=m0. The TV bound is then
strictly below 9/10, making both errors <1/20 impossible. This directly
establishes the necessary sample endpoint 28091647285174.
