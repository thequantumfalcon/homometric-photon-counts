An open neighborhood with exactly four simple four-count blind gains
==================================================================

Theorem and scope
-----------------
Keep the two verified 63-site geometries fixed. Let their site phase errors
be independent centered wrapped Gaussians with variance s>0 and put
t=exp(-s/2). Let the detected count conditional on intensity I be Poisson
with mean b+eta I, where eta>0 is gain and b>=0 is common background.
There exist epsilon_t>0 and epsilon_b>0 such that, whenever

  |t-1/2|<epsilon_t and 0<=b<epsilon_b,

the difference Pr_A(N=4)-Pr_B(N=4) has EXACTLY FOUR positive zeros in eta,
all simple. They can be ordered eta_1(t,b)<...<eta_4(t,b), and each is a
real-analytic function of (t,b) on a neighborhood of (1/2,0), restricted
to physical nonnegative b. Equivalently there is an open variance
neighborhood of s=2 log 2 with this property for sufficiently small b.

This is an existence theorem for a genuine open neighborhood, not a
numerically specified radius for the full four-root conclusion. Explicit
endpoint bounds below prevent roots escaping eta=0 or eta=infinity.
The remaining compact-interval continuation radius is not computed.
No assertion of exactly four roots at every variance or every background
is made. The geometry, independence, and common-background model matter.

The reference t=1/2,b=0 result has exactly four simple positive roots:

1. Parameter dependence
-----------------------
For 0<t<1 the phase density is
w_t(theta)=(1/(2pi)) sum_{n in Z} t^(n^2) exp(i n theta).
Its series, and all derivatives needed here, converge uniformly on each
compact t interval inside (0,1). The same series is holomorphic for
complex |t|<1, uniformly on compact disks and real theta. Integration
over the compact phase cube therefore makes expectations of
exp(-eta I)(b+eta I)^4 real analytic jointly in (t,b,eta).
Bounded I justifies all eta and b derivatives. This analyticity also
holds for real negative b as a mathematical extension of the expression;
the physical conclusions below use b>=0 only.

2. Uniform Fourier decay on t in [2/5,3/5]
----------------------------------------
Put q=3/5 and x=q^2. Let W=sup_theta w_t(theta) and
V=integral_0^(2pi)|w_t'(theta)| dtheta. Since n^2>=2n for n>=2,

sum_{n>=1} t^(n^2) <= q+x^2/(1-x),
sum_{n>=1} n t^(n^2) <= q+x^2(2-x)/(1-x)^2.

Using pi>3 gives W<=521/1200. Integrating the Fourier derivative
termwise and using 4/pi<2 gives V<=7161/3200.

For any single-site characteristic in any direction, integrate
w_t(theta-shift) exp(i r cos theta). For r>=4 remove neighborhoods of
0 and pi of total length 4d, where d=2/sqrt(r)<=1. Their contribution
is at most 4dW. Integration by parts on the two remaining intervals
gives at most (8W+V)/(r sin d): boundary terms contribute 4W, the
density derivative V, and the derivative of 1/sin contributes 4W.
Since sin d>=5d/6, the total bound is

  (64W/5+3V/5)/sqrt(r) <= (331201/48000)/sqrt(r) <7/sqrt(r).

For smaller r use modulus<=1. Independence gives, uniformly in t and
direction, |Phi_G,t(r,angle)|<=B_63(r), where
B_m(r)=min(1,7^m r^(-m/2)).

Also a single-site t derivative satisfies

|partial_t phi| <=2 sum_{n>=1} n^2 t^(n^2-1)
 <=2[1+(x(1+x)/(1-x)^3-x)/q] =35971/5120 <8.

The derivative of the 63-site product is consequently bounded by
63*8*B_62(r). The integrable envelopes r B_63, r^3 B_63, and r B_62
justify planar Fourier inversion, its required derivatives, and t
differentiation of the angularly averaged density-at-zero integral.

3. An explicit neighborhood with a positive density gap
-----------------------------------------------------
Write psi_G,t for the angular average of the planar characteristic and
Delta psi_t=psi_A,t-psi_B,t. Fourier normalization gives

h_A,t(u)-h_B,t(u)=1/2 integral_0^infinity
                  r Delta psi_t(r) J0(r sqrt(u)) dr.

The difference of two characteristic derivatives contributes a factor
2, which cancels this 1/2. Therefore

|delta'(t)| <=63*8 integral_0^infinity r B_62(r) dr
 =63*8*7^4(1/2+1/29)=18756612/29 <1000000.

It follows from the reference lower bound that

  delta(t)>2/100000000 whenever |t-1/2|<=1/10^14.             (A)

This explicit parameter band is only an endpoint-control result; it is
not a claimed certified radius for the full four-root theorem.

Since |J0(v)-1|<=v^2/4, the same inversion formula gives for every u>=0

|h_A,t(u)-h_B,t(u)-delta(t)| <= L u,
L=1/4 integral_0^infinity r^3 B_63(r) dr
 =63*7^8/880=363182463/880.                                 (B)

The density difference is zero beyond M, and the bound still holds
there: it was obtained from the inversion formula for all u>=0, not by
extending a local Taylor approximation past its domain.

4. No root can arrive from infinite gain, even with background
------------------------------------------------------------
Remove positive factors by defining

Z(t,b,eta)=24 exp(b)[Pr_A(N=4)-Pr_B(N=4)]
         =Delta E[exp(-eta I)(b+eta I)^4].

Using u=I and then v=eta u in its density integral, (B) implies

|Z-delta(t) A(b)/eta| <= L B(b)/eta^2,
A(b)=b^4+4b^3+12b^2+24b+24,
B(b)=b^4+8b^3+36b^2+96b+120.

Here A=int_0^infinity exp(-v)(b+v)^4 dv and
B=int_0^infinity v exp(-v)(b+v)^4 dv. For b>=0, A>0 and B<=5A
coefficientwise. In the explicit t band (A),

  eta>=R=200000000000000 implies Z(t,b,eta)>0 for EVERY b>=0,

because R>5L/(2/100000000). The sign is therefore uniform on an entire
parameter neighborhood and an unbounded gain interval. Roots cannot
silently appear beyond a finite numerical search range.

5. No root can arrive from zero gain
----------------------------------
Let q_b(z)=exp(-z)(b+z)^4. For 0<=b<=1/8 and 0<=z<=1, its fifth
derivative has modulus at most
(9/8)^4+20(9/8)^3+120(9/8)^2+240(9/8)+120 <600.
The first four moment differences D_0,...,D_3 vanish. Taylor's theorem
therefore gives, for eta<=1/M,

|Z(t,b,eta)/eta^4-D_4(t)L_4(b)| <=10 eta M^5,
L_4(b)=1-4b+3b^2-(2/3)b^3+b^4/24.

The factor 10 includes both intensity laws: each Taylor remainder is
at most 600(eta M)^5/5!=5(eta M)^5. For b<=1/8,
L_4(b)>=1-4/8-(2/3)(1/8)^3=383/768>1/3.
On the full t interval [2/5,3/5], the exact D_4 polynomial gives

D_4(t)>=d_*=(455015761/20000)(2/5)^8(16/25)^5*6
           =22901693117104128/2384185791015625 >0.

Set a=min(1/M,d_*/(60 M^5))>0. Then Z/eta^4>=d_*/6>0 for
0<eta<=a, uniformly for t in [2/5,3/5] and b in [0,1/8]. Thus the
fourfold algebraic zero of the unnormalized count difference at eta=0
cannot split into additional positive-gain roots in the physical
parameter neighborhood under consideration.

6. The remaining compact interval and the four analytic branches
---------------------------------------------------------------
Only eta in [a,R] remains. Let the four reference roots be r_1<...<r_4.
They lie strictly inside this interval by the endpoint positivity just
proved. At b=0, Z=eta^4 F_t(eta); hence
partial_eta Z(1/2,0,r_j)=r_j^4 F'_(1/2)(r_j) !=0.
Choose four disjoint closed intervals U_j around these roots, ordered
from left to right and lying inside (a,R). They can be chosen so that
partial_eta Z has a strict constant sign on each reference U_j and its
two endpoint values have opposite signs. Continuity on these finite
compact intervals preserves both properties in a parameter neighborhood.
Thus exactly one simple root persists in each U_j; the analytic implicit
function theorem gives the real-analytic root branch eta_j(t,b).

The compact set K=[a,R] minus the interiors of the U_j contains no
reference root. Therefore min_K |Z(1/2,0,eta)| is a strictly positive
number. Joint continuity, uniformly on K, preserves this nonzero lower
bound in another parameter neighborhood. There are no other roots in K.

Intersect these finitely many open parameter neighborhoods with
|t-1/2|<10^(-14) and |b|<1/8, then choose a smaller open rectangle about
(1/2,0). Restricting that rectangle to b>=0 gives epsilon_t,epsilon_b
in the theorem. Sections 4 and 5 control both ends of the gain axis;
the compact argument controls everything between. Root ordering follows
from the disjoint ordered intervals, and no hidden roots can escape or
arrive at either endpoint.

7. What this does and does not settle
------------------------------------
The reference four-root count is not an isolated single-parameter fact:
it is stable under sufficiently small phase-variance changes and small
nonnegative background. This is a new local theorem, conditional on the
sealed exact reference certificates and the specified model.

The full count laws remain distinct at every positive gain throughout
this neighborhood, including the four blind gains: their fourth falling
factorial moment difference is eta^4 D_4(t)>0, since all lower intensity
moment differences vanish. Blindness here refers only to the four-count
indicator, not the full distribution.

The proof does not give an explicit usable radius epsilon_t or epsilon_b
for exact-four behavior, nor an exact global count at all phase widths or
backgrounds. The explicit 10^(-14) t band controls only the density gap
and infinite-gain tail; it must still be intersected with the unquantified
compact-interval stability neighborhood. No experiment or external human
review is claimed. No network or new dependency is needed for this proof.
