NECESSARY SAMPLE BOUND FOR THE FIXED 13-BIN OBSERVATION

Result

At the retained reference model s=2 log(2), eta=1/20, b=0, any test of
m independent observations of the 13 bins (0,...,11,>=12), including a
randomized test, needs at least 28,091,647,285,174 observations to have
both misclassification probabilities strictly below 1/20. The retained
explicit score test suffices at 102,399,876,723,169 observations. Thus the
optimal sample count for THIS fixed binned experiment lies in that integer
interval; the sufficient endpoint is less than 3.646 times the necessary
endpoint. This is a new lower bound, not an optimality proof.

The result is conditional on the archived certified probability intervals.
It does not apply as a necessary bound to access to individual counts above
11, other gains or phase widths, changing measurements, or dependent gates.
The larger observation space could contain information discarded by pooling.
These are statements within a mathematical model, not experimental results.

Exact input and computation

certify_binned_lower_bound.py reads only the selected row of the sealed
continuation-2026-10-20/multibin/certificate.json. Its entire file hash is
pinned. All arithmetic uses Python Fraction and integer arithmetic; there
are no numerical square roots, logarithms, optimizers, Monte Carlo samples,
or imports of the original arithmetic implementation. Every lower endpoint
is positive, every interval is ordered, and each family's box intersects
the probability simplex. The validity of the inherited enclosures is a
dependency; these checks alone would not establish their model validity.

Let actual probabilities be p_i and q_i, and their saved interval endpoints
be l_i^P,u_i^P,l_i^Q,u_i^Q. Set
 D_i=max(|l_i^P-u_i^Q|,|u_i^P-l_i^Q|),
 e=sum_i D_i^2/[8 min(l_i^P,l_i^Q)].
The script rounds e UP to a rational delta on a denominator 10^30 grid,
checking 0<e<=delta<1 exactly. This is conservative and introduces no
floating-point decision. The certificate records delta and all final bounds.

Affinity argument

Define a=sum_i sqrt(p_i q_i). Probability normalization gives
 1-a = (1/2) sum_i (sqrt(p_i)-sqrt(q_i))^2
     = sum_i (p_i-q_i)^2/[2(sqrt(p_i)+sqrt(q_i))^2]
     <= sum_i D_i^2/[8 min(l_i^P,l_i^Q)] <= delta.
The lower bounds on every probability make all denominators positive.

For any two finite probability vectors r,s, Cauchy-Schwarz gives
 TV(r,s)=(1/2)sum |sqrt(r)-sqrt(s)|(sqrt(r)+sqrt(s))
         <= sqrt(1-affinity(r,s)^2).
Independence gives affinity(P^m,Q^m)=a^m by expanding the finite product.
Consequently TV(P^m,Q^m)<=sqrt(1-(1-delta)^(2m)).

If a possibly randomized rule decides A with probabilities f(z) in [0,1],
its errors are alpha=1-E_P f and beta=E_Q f. Splitting the signed mass
function into its positive and negative parts gives
 E_P f-E_Q f <= TV(P^m,Q^m), hence alpha+beta>=1-TV.
Both errors <1/20 therefore require TV>9/10. Combining the two inequalities
forces (1-delta)^(2m)<19/100, and thus
 m > log(100/19)/[-2 log(1-delta)].

No transcendental estimate is trusted numerically. For 0<x<1, integration
of the positive geometric series gives
 log((1+x)/(1-x))=2 sum_(k>=0) x^(2k+1)/(2k+1).
The first 100 terms at x=81/119 give an exact rational L<log(100/19).
Also -log(1-delta)=integral_0^delta 1/(1-t)dt <=delta/(1-delta).
It follows that m > L(1-delta)/(2delta). Taking the integer floor plus one
gives the stated necessary endpoint 28,091,647,285,174.

Secondary KL check

An independent information inequality route gives the weaker endpoint
11,206,322,081,385. With positive q_i, log u<=u-1 yields
 KL(P||Q)<=sum_i (p_i-q_i)^2/q_i<=sum_i D_i^2/l_i^Q=:U.
The same upper bound is computed with P,Q reversed. The script rounds
each U upward exactly on the denominator 10^30 grid.

For the two-valued decision, the relative entropy is at least
(9/10)log(19) when both errors are <=1/20. This follows by differentiating
the binary relative entropy in its two probabilities: it increases with
the first and decreases with the second when the first exceeds the second.
The log-sum inequality (convexity of u log u) shows that passing the full
data through a decision cannot increase relative entropy, including for
a randomized decision. Product expansion gives KL(P^m||Q^m)=m KL(P||Q).
Therefore mU>(9/10)log(19). A 100-term positive series at x=9/10 bounds
log(19) from below exactly. This yields the secondary recorded endpoints.

Reproduction and limits

Run from the workspace root, choosing a fresh output file in an existing
replay directory:
 python -B followup-2026-10-20/lowerbounds/certify_binned_lower_bound.py --output PATH
The required --output argument prevents a default evidence overwrite. An
existing output is rejected, and exclusive creation prevents a write race.
The three malformed-input controls (missing bin, zero lower bound, reversed
interval) are rejected. This is an internal AI-assisted derivation and exact
arithmetic check; neither a newly completed external review nor a Lean proof
of this added lower bound is claimed.

To extend the lower bound to the unbinned detector, one needs an upper bound
on full-law statistical distance, including the within-tail information.
The pooled >=12 mass alone supplies no such upper bound: data processing
goes the opposite way. Moment reconstruction or more outcome certificates
and a rigorous residual-tail argument would be additional work.
