Effective terminal-gain certificate, t=1/2
=========================================

Scope and conventions. S_G is the sum of the 63 independent unit phasors
specified by the preserved geometry; I_G=|S_G|^2. Each phase error has Fourier
coefficients E exp(in theta)=2^(-n^2). F4(eta)=E_A[I^4 exp(-eta I)] minus
E_B[I^4 exp(-eta I)], corresponding to zero background b=0. The producer
certify_density_zero.py and its JSON
certificate establish an explicit positive tail, not an exact global root
count by themselves. Arithmetic is rigorous Arb ball arithmetic, conditional
on the usual correctness of Python, python-flint/Arb, and the host machine.

1. A uniform single-site characteristic bound

Write w(theta)=(1+2 sum_{n>=1}2^(-n^2) cos(n theta))/(2 pi).
Let W=sup |w| and V=integral_0^{2pi}|w'|. Since
sum_{n>=1}2^(-n^2)<=1/2+sum_{n>=2}4^(-n)=7/12,
W<=13/(12 pi)<13/36<3/8. Also
sum_{n>=1}n 2^(-n^2)<=1/2+1/8+sum_{n>=3}n 8^(-n)
=5/8+11/1568, so V<=991/784<13/10.

The single-site characteristic in any direction is an integral of
w(theta-shift) exp(i r cos(theta)). For r>=4, put d=2/sqrt(r)<=1.
The neighborhoods of the stationary points 0 and pi have total length 4d
and contribute at most 4dW. Integrating by parts on [d,pi-d] and
[pi+d,2pi-d] bounds the boundary terms by 4W/(r sin d), the w' terms
by V/(r sin d), and the derivative of 1/sin(theta) by 4W/(r sin d).
Thus the whole modulus is at most
4dW+(8W+V)/(r sin d).
Since sin d>=d-d^3/6>=5d/6, its coefficient of r^(-1/2) is at most
64W/5+3V/5 < 24/5+39/50 =279/50<6.
For r<4 use the trivial characteristic bound 1. Consequently each site
satisfies |phi_j(r,angle)|<=min(1,6/sqrt(r)). Independence gives
|Phi_G(r,angle)|<=B(r)=min(1,6^63 r^(-63/2)).

2. Density normalization and the anchored Lipschitz estimate

Define psi_G(r)=(1/(2pi)) integral Phi_G(r,angle) d angle.
The planar Fourier inversion convention is
p_G(x)=(2pi)^(-2) integral_R2 Phi_G(k) exp(-i k.x) dk.
Absolute integrability follows from B, as does integrability after two
derivatives. The intensity density is
h_G(u)=(1/2) integral_0^{2pi} p_G(sqrt(u) omega_angle) d angle
      =(1/2) integral_0^infinity r psi_G(r) J0(r sqrt(u)) dr.
This explicitly accounts for both the planar Fourier normalization and
the polar change of variable u=|x|^2. It also defines h at u=0 continuously.

Put Delta psi=psi_A-psi_B and delta=h_A(0)-h_B(0). The real Bessel
integral representation and 1-cos(v)<=v^2/2 imply
|J0(v)-1|<=v^2/4. Since |Delta psi|<=2B,
|h_A(u)-h_B(u)-delta|<=L u,
L=(1/4) integral_0^infinity r^3 B(r) dr
 = (1/4)6^8(1/4+2/55)=63*6^8/880.
Only this estimate anchored at zero is needed; no unproved radial Hessian
conversion is used. The densities vanish beyond the bounded physical
support, and the estimate remains valid there by the same inversion formula.

3. Certified Fourier and radial truncations

The integer-order Jacobi-Anger expansion gives a site's Laurent polynomial
sum_{n=-K}^K i^n J_n(r) 2^(-n^2) z^n q^n, K=7.
Its omitted real-r tail is bounded, uniformly in angle and unit z, by
eps=2*2^(-(K+1)^2)/(1-2^(-(2K+3))), because |J_n(r)|<=1.
The 63-site product error is at most E=63 eps(1+eps)^62, by a telescoping
product bound. Each true single-site factor has modulus at most 1.

The 21-site base has the six phases and multiplicities encoded in the
producer: rho*(+/-sqrt(222)+i sqrt(3))/15, each five times;
(+/-sqrt(573)-i sqrt(3))/24, each four times; +1 twice and -1 once,
where rho=(1+i sqrt(3))/2. Structure A uses rotations 0,1,3 and B uses
0,2,3. The producer extracts the Laurent constant coefficient of
B(q)B(-q)[B(rho q)-B(rho^2 q)]. A constant Laurent coefficient is precisely
the angular average. The shifted ordinary polynomial has offset 21K;
the final constant coefficient is index 63K. Exact geometry correspondence
is independently checked in the adjoining geometry audit.

On [0,10], Arb integrates r Delta psi_truncated(r)/2. Every integer-order
Bessel J_n is entire, and the remaining operations are polynomial. The
callback therefore is analytic throughout every complex integration ball;
ignoring the callback's analytic flag does not assert a false branch or
domain property. The returned ball, rather than the requested tolerance,
is used as the enclosure. Algebraic phase parameters are themselves balls.
The external Fourier error is 50E.

On [10,100], the producer checks an exhaustive adjacent interval cover for
|J0(r)|+2 sum_{n=1}^7 2^(-n^2)|J_n(r)|+eps <3/5.
Thus this omitted radial contribution is bounded by
(3/5)^63 (100^2-10^2)/2.
On [100,infinity], use B to obtain
integral_100^infinity r B(r) dr =2*6^63/(59*10^59).
Adding these absolute errors to the inner integral yields the certificate
3/100000000 < delta < 1/25000000.

4. Consequence for large gain

Integrating the anchored estimate against u^4 exp(-eta u) gives
|F4(eta)-24 delta/eta^5|<=120L/eta^6.
Hence F4(eta)>0 whenever eta>5L/delta. The certificate checks exactly that
30000000000000 > 5L/(3/100000000), proving
F4(eta)>0 for every eta>=30000000000000.

5. Compactified representation for further certificates

For y>0 define G(y)=F4(1/y)/(24 y^5); set G(0)=delta.
The elementary Laplace-Bessel identity, differentiated four times, gives
integral_0^infinity u^4 exp(-eta u) J0(r sqrt(u)) du
=24 eta^(-5) exp(-v)L4(v), v=r^2/(4eta).
Fubini is justified by the integrable B bound. Thus
G(y)=(1/2) integral_0^infinity r Delta psi(r) exp(-v)L4(v) dr,
v=r^2 y/4, including y=0 by dominated convergence.
L4(v)=1-4v+3v^2-(2/3)v^3+v^4/24.
Its exponentially weighted derivative is exp(-v)P(v), where
P(v)=-5+10v-5v^2+(5/6)v^3-v^4/24.
For v>=0, exp(-v)v^j/j!<=1 gives the safe uniform bounds
|exp(-v)L4(v)|<=16 and |exp(-v)P(v)|<=31.
Since integral r^3 B(r) is finite, differentiation under the integral is
valid on every closed y>=0 interval, including the right derivative at 0.
A certified negative derivative on [0,6], together with opposite endpoint
signs, would prove exactly one simple root above gain 1/6. This last step
requires its own interval certificate and is not implied by the density
certificate alone.
