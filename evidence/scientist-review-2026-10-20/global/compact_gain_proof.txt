Exactly one remaining positive-gain root at t=1/2
================================================

Read with density_zero_proof.txt for the model, Fourier normalization,
uniform characteristic bound, and entire-integrand justification. This
certificate concerns t=1/2, zero background b=0, and independent
wrapped-Gaussian site phases.
It does not assert that the global count is four at every phase width.

1. Compactification

For y>0 put G(y)=F4(1/y)/(24 y^5), with G(0)=delta. The Fourier identity
proved in density_zero_proof.txt is
G(y)=1/2 integral_0^infinity r Delta psi(r) K(r^2 y/4) dr,
where K(v)=exp(-v)L4(v). For y>=0, |K(v)|<=16 and |K'(v)|<=31.
Consequently G is continuously differentiable from the right at zero and
throughout y>=0, with
G'(y)=1/8 integral_0^infinity r^3 Delta psi(r) K'(r^2 y/4) dr.
The dominator r^3 min(1,6^63 r^(-63/2)) is integrable. This gives a
well-defined compact interval [0,6] corresponding to all gains >=1/6,
including the positive infinite-gain limit through y=0.

2. Uniform single-site tail certificates

With K=7 Fourier terms on each side, the real-r single-site characteristic
is J0(r)+2 sum_{n=1}^7 i^n J_n(r)2^(-n^2)cos(n theta), plus error eps.
The magnitude is pi-periodic: theta->theta+pi conjugates the expression,
and the same holds for its infinite series. Thus theta in [0,pi] suffices.

The producer covers all radial/angular rectangles exhaustively, bisecting
only rectangles whose interval bound has not closed. For each real radial
interval [a,b], J_n(r) lies in J_n((a+b)/2)+[-(b-a)/2,(b-a)/2].
The bound |J_n'|<=1 follows by differentiating the integer-order integral
representation; equivalently use J_n'=(J_{n-1}-J_{n+1})/2 and |J_n|<=1.
All cosine factors are evaluated on their full Arb angular intervals.
Adding eps to the computed magnitude yields the strict certified bounds
  |single-site characteristic| <5/8 for 2<=r<=3;
  |single-site characteristic| <1/2 for 3<=r<=10;
  |single-site characteristic| <9/20 for 10<=r<=200.
The third bound uses the angular-independent Bessel absolute sum.
The certificate records all 288, 2050, and 872 leaves, respectively.
An independent verifier checks exact partition coverage and re-evaluates
these inequalities. The main producer always regenerates them; it never
loads a preexisting numerical tail cache as evidence.

If a segment [a,b] has single-site bound q, independence gives
|Delta psi|<=2q^63. Its omitted G contribution is at most
16 q^63 (b^2-a^2)/2, and its omitted G' contribution is at most
31 q^63 (b^4-a^4)/16.

For r>200 use the global characteristic bound 6^63 r^(-63/2), and
enlarge the tail to r>196=14^2 for rational convenience. Its contributions
are bounded by 32*6^63/(59*14^59) and 31*6^63/(110*14^55), respectively.

3. Inner integrals and interval parameters

Let E=63 eps(1+eps)^62 be the per-structure Fourier-product error from the
density proof. Integrating on [0,2] gives external Fourier errors at most
32E for G and 31E for G'. All errors above are added as centered Arb balls
after numerical integration. The JSON contains their exact rational sums.

For each candidate inverse-gain interval [a,b], the callback uses the whole
real Arb ball Y=[a,b] in v=r^2Y/4 and in exp(-v)P(v), with
P(v)=-5+10v-5v^2+(5/6)v^3-v^4/24.
For every fixed y in Y this is an entire function of complex r, and every
returned callback ball encloses its value. Arb's rigorous integration thus
encloses the integral for every y in Y, rather than just the midpoint.
The resulting real ball plus all external errors has strictly negative
upper endpoint on each of the 24 intervals [j/4,(j+1)/4], j=0,...,23.
The code separately checks that the sorted accepted intervals join exactly
and cover [0,6]. It records exact rational endpoints of each result ball;
the approximate display string is not used as proof of a sign.

The expensive Fourier base callback is cached only in memory. The key
contains precision and exact dyadic midpoint/radius pairs for both real
and imaginary parts of the complex radial ball. This is not a decimal
approximation key, so distinct argument balls cannot collide. The cached
value is independent of y; only the inexpensive kernel depends on y.
The analytic flag does not affect the base value because it is entire.

4. Root existence, uniqueness, and simplicity

The final strict enclosures give G(0)>0 and G(6)<0. Continuity provides
a root in (0,6); strict negativity of G' on all [0,6] gives exactly one,
and it is simple. For finite positive eta, the multiplying factor 24y^5
is nonzero and the map y=1/eta has nonzero derivative. Therefore F4 has
exactly one simple root for eta>1/6. The endpoint eta=1/6 is not a root.

For a narrower bracket the producer integrates the two rational gain
endpoints 2934841/10000000 and 1467421/5000000 using radial cutoff 3 and
absolute integration tolerance 1e-17. The [2,3] tail is then removed;
the Fourier error is 72E (16E times 3^2/2). The tails from 3 onward are
unchanged. The exact sign checks place the unique root strictly between
these gains, i.e. 0.2934841 < eta_4 < 0.2934842.

Combining this independent inverse-gain certificate with the preserved
order-96 certificate of exactly three simple roots on (0,1/6] establishes
exactly FOUR simple positive roots globally at t=1/2. No infinite domain
is left unchecked. This conclusion remains conditional on the stated
mathematical model and the standard software/hardware arithmetic trust
boundary; it is not a claim of experimental proof or a fully formal Lean
verification of Arb itself.

Reproduction:
  python certify_compact_gain.py --output <new-directory>/compact_gain.json
The optional PHOTON_FLINT_RUNTIME environment variable selects a local
python-flint package; otherwise the local pinned runtime is preferred,
with normal installed-package import as fallback. The output records the
actual runtime package/version, producer SHA256, and imported density
producer SHA256. The final calculation has no network requirement and
does not modify the preserved research archive or sealed audit folders.
