ANALYTIC AUDIT AND A SCOPED STRENGTHENING
Audit date: 2026-10-20 (America/Denver)

Conclusion
==========
The four principal analytic chains examined here are mathematically coherent:
global qualitative finiteness, at least four positive sign changes, guarded
calibration, and the specified Gaussian AR(1) transfer. The cited integration
and o-minimality theorems apply as stated. No mathematical counterexample or
missing premise was found within their expressly stated model. This is a
fresh reasoning audit with source checking, not external peer review or
machine-checked formalization, and it cannot establish infallibility.

A new consequence is proved below: on every compact interval of strictly
positive finite phase variances, a single finite upper bound exists for the
number of blind gains, simultaneously for every zero-background count
channel. The bound is non-effective. It does not settle an exact count.

1. Global finiteness: premise-by-premise audit
============================================
Input: two fixed 63-site unit-phasor geometries, iid N(0,s) site phases,
0<s<infinity, bounded intensity I in [0,3969]. The lower-moment equalities
and nonzero fourth gap are dependencies on the geometric moment calculation.

The wrapped Gaussian w_s(theta) has Fourier coefficients exp(-s n^2/2).
For complex theta on any bounded strip, the nth summand is bounded by
exp(-s n^2/2+|n|L). The series and every derivative converge uniformly on
compact strips. Thus its restriction to a real compact phase cube is
analytic on a neighborhood of that cube. This matters: arbitrary smooth
densities would not automatically satisfy the restricted-analytic premise.

The nonconstant analytic intensity map has an absolutely continuous
pushforward. One non-identically-zero partial derivative has a null zero
set; the full critical set is a subset. On the regular set, local coordinates
with intensity as first coordinate and Fubini prove absolute continuity.
A countable chart cover is sufficient, and the critical set has probability
zero. This justifies the density claim and absence of a zero atom without
assuming a globally nonsingular intensity map.

The CDF integrand is the analytic density restricted to a compact cube,
multiplied by a sublevel indicator of a restricted analytic function.
Discontinuity at the indicator boundary causes no problem: globally
subanalytic functions need not be continuous. Rescaling the cube to [-1,1]^63
fits the standard definition. All fibers are integrable, so the zero-in-case-
of-nonintegrability convention in Cluckers-Miller causes no issue.

Cluckers-Miller Definition 1.2 and Theorem 1.3 say that the parameter integral
belongs to the algebra generated by globally subanalytic functions and
logarithms of positive such functions. The CDF therefore is definable in
R_an,exp. DMM Corollary 5.13 establishes that structure's o-minimality.
Finite differentiability and its derivative are first-order definable via
the epsilon-delta limit definition. Absolute continuity makes the exceptional
set null, hence finite by o-minimality. Assign derivative zero there.

Consequently h_A-h_B, and u^4(h_A-h_B), have finitely many nonzero sign
blocks up to null sets. No closure of R_an,exp under arbitrary integration
has been assumed; that would be an invalid shortcut.

The archived Laplace zero bound is sound. For m sign changes, m+1 distinct
derivative constraints from real transform zeros produce a dimension m+1
space of real exponential polynomials. Dividing by one exponential and
differentiating reduces its dimension by one; Rolle's theorem with
multiplicity proves the zero bound inductively. A nonzero element vanishing
at the m sign cuts therefore has exactly those simple zeros. Choosing its
overall sign to agree with the density makes its integral strictly positive,
contradicting the m+1 constraints. Zero intervals between sign blocks can
contain the cut points and do not invalidate the argument.

Result retained: finitely many real transform zeros, counted with
multiplicity, hence finitely many positive four-count blind gains for each
fixed finite positive s. The theorem gives no computed upper integer,
no tail cutoff, and no exact positive-root count.

2. Orthogonality and at least four positive blind gains
======================================================
For F_4(eta)=integral u^4 exp(-eta*u) d(P_A-P_B)(u), Tonelli applied
SEPARATELY to each positive law gives, for j=0,...,3,

 integral_0^infinity eta^j F_4(eta) d eta
   = j! [E_A I^(3-j)-E_B I^(3-j)] = 0.

Absolute convergence is bounded by the sum of the two positive integrals.
At j=3 the equality uses equal mass away from zero; here neither law has
a zero atom. This premise must not be silently dropped for general laws.

If fewer than four positive sign changes existed, multiplying F_4 by the
polynomial whose roots are exactly those changes would produce a function
of one sign with positive integral. Its degree is at most three, so the
orthogonality identities instead make that integral zero. Analyticity
ensures isolated zeros and excludes pathological accumulating zeros in the
interior; F_4(0)>0 ensures nontriviality. This proves at least four distinct
sign-changing roots, without assuming the finiteness theorem. Sign changes
do not prove simplicity; odd multiplicities larger than one are allowed.

For two bounded intensity laws matching moments 0,...,r-1, differing at r,
and having equal zero atoms, the same argument gives at least min(k,r)
positive sign changes in every k-count channel k>=1. For k>=r the appropriate
function is eta^(k-r)F_k(eta). Its jth Mellin moment involves intensity
moment r-j-1. For k<r use F_k itself. Nontriviality follows because an
identically zero kth derivative of the Laplace difference would make that
difference a polynomial, while it is bounded and tends to zero at +infinity.

Result retained for these structures: at least four distinct positive blind
gains for channel 4 at every interior s. The separately certified two roots
on (0,1/32] do not establish exactly four on the whole positive axis.

3. NEW CONSEQUENCE: uniform finiteness on compact width ranges
=============================================================
Fix any 0<s_min<=s_max<infinity and let K=[s_min,s_max]. There exists a
finite integer B_K such that, for EVERY s in K and EVERY integer k>=0,
F_(k,s)(eta)=E_A[I^k exp(-eta I)]-E_B[I^k exp(-eta I)] has at most B_K
real zeros counted with multiplicity. In particular all zero-background
count channels have at most B_K positive blind gains.

Proof:
(a) The wrapped-Gaussian Fourier series is jointly analytic in (s,theta)
on a complex neighborhood of K times the real phase cube. Choose Re(s)
bounded below by a positive constant there; exp(-Re(s)n^2/2+|n|L) controls
every derivative. Thus the product density restricted to K times the cube
is a single restricted analytic function, rather than a separately chosen
function for each s.
(b) Apply Cluckers-Miller to the CDF integral with parameters (s,u).
The two CDFs are jointly definable in R_an,exp. Their partial derivatives
in u, assigned zero at points of failure, are jointly definable densities.
Each fixed-s exceptional fiber is finite by the earlier absolute continuity
argument. Therefore h(s,u)=h_A(s,u)-h_B(s,u) is a definable family.
(c) Cylindrical cell decomposition adapted to {h>0} and {h<0}, with s as
base coordinate, is finite. Every vertical fiber meets at most that finite
number of cells. Its positive and negative intervals therefore have a
uniformly bounded number of sign blocks; isolated points do not matter for
integration. This is the elementary cell-decomposition proof of uniform
finiteness, without needing F_(k,s) itself to be definable.
(d) Multiplication by u^k for u>0 preserves every nonzero sign block,
independently of k. It does not annihilate the density almost everywhere:
the laws differ and are absolutely continuous. The Laplace zero bound
from section 1 now supplies the SAME finite bound for every k and s.
This proves the claim.

This is a non-effective compact-parameter generalization of the archived
theorem. It does not produce a numerical B_K, a uniform bound as s approaches
zero or infinity, a positive-background theorem, or an exact global root
count. It is newly derived in this audit and has not received external review.

4. Calibration: unconditional correctness versus local termination
=================================================================
The archived positive-coefficient identity H=2C'V-3CV' implies
Q'=CH/V^4>0 on [0,1]. Together with c2>0 and c3>0 this makes the first
three factorial-moment inverse global on the interior physical model.
The positive fourth gap then identifies the geometry as well. The
nonnegative-background and valid rate-bound constraints are additional
physical feasibility conditions, not substitutes for the inverse proof.

The three-stage procedure has valid unconditional control despite
data-dependent gates. Fix a true parameter value. If b exceeds the fixed
background cap, reaching stage 1 implies failure of the preliminary
concentration event. If b is below the cap, the stage-1 variance bounds are
deterministically valid. If the true parameter lies outside the fixed local
box, passing the stage-1 outer-box gate implies that stage's confidence
event failed. If it lies inside the box, the stage-2 variance bounds are
deterministically valid. Union bounds on these unconditional events yield
the stated error bound. No conditional iid argument after selection is
needed and no cross-group independence is required.

The local finite identification guarantee is a different claim. At the
reference parameter, population-centered envelopes of radius 2e contain
EVERY realized confidence rectangle on the concentration event. Strict
gate and wrong-model-exclusion margins on those larger envelopes force
successful identification for every such dataset. A check at just a
single synthetic mean vector would not prove this, but the archived proof
does use the envelopes.

The profiled fourth-moment gradient formulas were checked by differentiation:
for a=u2-u1^2, d=u3-3u2u1+2u1^3 and q=d^2/a^3,
partial_a[a^2 R(Q^-1(q))]=2aR-3(d/a)^2 R'/Q',
partial_d[a^2 R(Q^-1(q))]=2(d/a)R'/Q'.
Chain rule gives the recorded three derivatives. Positivity of a,d and
strict interior q over the WHOLE rectangle justify the segment mean-value
bound. The midpoint-bisection enclosure must be retained, as it is in code.

Result retained: at least 95% unconditional simultaneous coverage/decision
control in the stated model, and at least 95% identification plus coverage
at the certified reference point for the prescribed group sizes. This is
not conditional-on-selection coverage, a uniform finite identification
budget, or optional-stopping validity.

Interface hypothesis rejected after checking the helper: integer conversion
uses a canonical-string validator, so a suspected alias such as '01' is
rejected before the dictionary is constructed. The dict comprehension alone
was insufficient evidence of a bug. No such bug is asserted here.

5. Specified temporal dependence
================================
The common phase cancels pathwise, so Gaussian contrasts have dimension 62.
For a stationary AR(1) contrast sequence with coefficient r=a^ell, conditional
Gaussian KL gives -62(m-1)log(1-r^2)/2. Pinsker in the convention
TV=sup_events difference gives TV<=sqrt(KL/2), hence the recorded factor
one half in front of sqrt(62(m-1)[-log(1-r^2)]). Passing through the same
geometry-specific Poisson kernel contracts TV. Applying the event bound
under both geometries and averaging gives a loss of delta, not 2delta,
in equal-prior success. Taking suprema over rules preserves that bound.

For any square-integrable count statistic Z, conditional independent
detector noise gives Cov(Z_t,Z_(t+k))=Cov(g(X_t),g(X_(t+k))). The multivariate
Hermite expansion has covariance sum c_alpha^2 alpha! a^(k|alpha|).
Its absolute value is bounded by |a|^k Var(g), valid also for negative a.
Summing the covariance series yields kappa=(1+a_max)/(1-a_max).
Chebyshev plus union bounds can therefore transfer fixed-group calibration
even for adjacent groups, using inflated radii; independence between groups
is unnecessary for that argument.

The photon-mean refinement also checks: the exact clean null makes every
first derivative of the phase-shifted mean intensity vanish. Gaussian
integration by parts eliminates the linear Hermite projection. The latent
variance can use kappa_2=(1+a_max^2)/(1-a_max^2), while independent Poisson
noise contributes without inflation. This refinement does not automatically
apply to higher factorial statistics or imperfect geometry.

Result retained: the transfer is valid for the specified stationary
Gaussian AR(1) model, known coefficient bound, and conditional independent
Poisson emissions. It supplies no empirical evidence that actual experiments
satisfy those assumptions. Frozen phases provide a rigorous obstruction to
claims covering arbitrary stationary dependence: indefinitely many emissions
reveal at most one intensity draw, whose two laws overlap.

7. What remains open
====================
Exact total positive-root count and locations beyond the certified interval;
effective tail exclusion; optimal gain or sample size outside already
certified fixed experiments; model validation against measurements;
arbitrary dependence or detector memory; robustness to geometry errors;
literature priority; outside peer review; and full proof-assistant
formalization. These cannot be converted into established facts merely
by using a reverse-engineering tool or replaying exact arithmetic.
