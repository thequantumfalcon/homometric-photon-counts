FINITE-EXPOSURE TRANSFER UNDER SPECIFIED GAUSSIAN MOTION
20 October 2026 (America/Denver)

Scope
-----
This is an additional conditional mathematical result, not physical evidence.
It quantifies one bridge from the archived instantaneous-count model to a
time-integrating detector. No original certificate is changed. The bound is
conservative; its existence does not establish an achievable exposure time.

Model
-----
Let z_1,...,z_n have modulus one and sum zero. Each site has an independent
stationary centered Gaussian process theta_j(u), with covariance
E theta_j(u) theta_j(v) = s exp(-|u-v|/tau), s,tau>0. Put

 F(u)=sum_j z_j exp(i theta_j(u)), I(u)=|F(u)|^2,
 J_D=(1/D) integral_0^D I(u) du, D>0.

The exposure count has conditional law Poisson(b+eta J_D), where b>=0 and
eta>0 are TOTAL per-gate background and gain. This normalization matters:
for a physical rate model these parameters ordinarily depend on D. Holding
eta fixed while reducing D requires a corresponding change in rate/flux.
No claim about the feasibility of that change is made here.

Assume emissions are conditionally independent given the relevant paths.
For the explicit iid test below, entire trajectories are independent between
observations (fresh stationary realizations). A single continuously evolving
trajectory is not iid; its dependence must be controlled separately.

Theorem
-------
For either fixed geometry, if P_D is its exposure-count law and P_0 its
archived snapshot-count law at the same s,eta,b, then

 TV(P_D,P_0) <= min(1, eta C sqrt(D/tau)),
 C=(4 sqrt(2)/3) n sqrt(s(1-exp(-s))).                    (1)

For m independent observations,

 TV(P_D^m,P_0^m) <= min(1, m eta C sqrt(D/tau)).           (2)

Consequently any fixed snapshot decision rule with conditional error at
most alpha under each geometry has exposure error at most alpha+epsilon
under each geometry whenever m eta C sqrt(D/tau)<=epsilon.

Proof
-----
The common clean null and independent sites give E|F(u)|^2=n(1-exp(-s)).
Write W_j(u)=exp(i theta_j(u)). Stationarity gives
E[W_j(u)-W_j(0)]=0. Independence across sites therefore kills all distinct-
site cross terms in the squared amplitude increment. The Gaussian
characteristic function and increment variance 2s(1-exp(-u/tau)) give

 E|F(u)-F(0)|^2=2n[1-exp(-s(1-exp(-u/tau)))].

Use ||a|^2-|b|^2|<=|a-b|(|a|+|b|), Cauchy-Schwarz, and
E(|F(u)|+|F(0)|)^2<=4n(1-exp(-s)). Thus

 E|I(u)-I(0)|
 <=2 sqrt(2) n sqrt((1-exp(-s))[1-exp(-s(1-exp(-u/tau)))])
 <=2 sqrt(2) n sqrt(s(1-exp(-s)) u/tau).

The last step uses 1-exp(-v)<=v twice. Integrating and applying the
triangle inequality to J_D-I(0) proves E|J_D-I(0)|<=C sqrt(D/tau).

For deterministic rates lambda,mu, couple the smaller-rate Poisson count
to the larger by adding an independent Poisson(|lambda-mu|) count.
They disagree with probability 1-exp(-|lambda-mu|)<=|lambda-mu|.
Condition on the shared phase path and average. The mixture coupling
proves (1), without claiming that J_D itself has a Gaussian-phase snapshot
distribution. Independent product couplings and a union bound prove (2).
Every decision event changes probability by at most total variation.

The product bound can also be replaced by 1-(1-delta)^m, with delta any
single-observation bound in [0,1]. The simpler m delta is used below.

Explicit reference-point consequence
-----------------------------------
Use the archived four-bin rational score at s=2 log(2), eta=1/28, b=0.
Its certified exponential-moment contraction is d>0. With m=ceil(6/d),
the snapshot conditional error is at most exp(-6)<1/400. This follows
from the same Markov argument as the original certificate, with a larger
sample budget. An exact Taylor partial sum proves exp(3)>20.

Since exp(-s)=1/4 and 2 log(2)<7/5,

 C^2=(32/9)63^2 s(3/4) < (32/9)63^2(21/20)=74088/5.

Choose epsilon=1/25. It suffices that

 D/tau <= (1/25)^2 / [m^2 (1/28)^2 (74088/5)].           (3)

Then each exposure-test error is strictly below
1/400+1/25=17/400<1/20. The exact m and rational threshold are generated
by verify_finite_exposure.py from the archived certified d.

What this closes and what remains
--------------------------------
Equation (3) is an explicit nonzero exposure condition under the model
above. It replaces an unquantified snapshot approximation for this one
specified family. It does not prove model adequacy, physical independence,
detector linearity, achievable flux, or an experimentally useful bound.
It does not establish that these extremely short gates are necessary.
Other within-gate motion laws, dead time, saturation, and afterpulsing need
their own bounds. This is a human-readable derivation plus rational
arithmetic verification, not a proof-assistant formalization.
