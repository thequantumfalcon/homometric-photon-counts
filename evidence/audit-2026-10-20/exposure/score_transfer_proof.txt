1. Existing one-observation coupling
===================================
At s=2 log(2), eta=1/28, b=0, the preceding finite-exposure theorem gives

 TV(P_D,G, P_0,G) <= eta C sqrt(D/tau), G=A,B,
 C^2 < Cbar^2 := 74088/5.

Both models use the same total per-gate gain and background. The exposure
process has independent stationary N(0,s) sites, covariance
s exp(-|u-v|/tau), and the clean phasor null. Its count law given the path
is Poisson(eta D^-1 integral_0^D I(u)du). The reference law is its time-zero
snapshot count law.

2. Bounded expectations contract with oscillation times TV
=========================================================
For any bounded real function f with L<=f<=U,

 |E_P f-E_Q f| <= (U-L) TV(P,Q).

If U=L the claim is immediate. Otherwise g=(f-L)/(U-L) belongs to [0,1],
and the layer-cake identity E g=integral_0^1 Pr(g>v)dv bounds the difference
by TV. This proves the result in the TV=sup_events convention without an
extra factor two.

Let w_j, j=0,1,2,3, be the archived rational score for bins N=0,1,2,N>=3.
Let Uplus_j and Uminus_j be the archived certified upper bounds on exp(w_j)
and exp(-w_j). Positivity gives

 1/Uminus_j <= exp(w_j) <= Uplus_j,
 1/Uplus_j <= exp(-w_j) <= Uminus_j.

Thus a rigorous simultaneous upper bound for both oscillations is

 R = max(max_j Uplus_j - min_j 1/Uminus_j,
         max_j Uminus_j - min_j 1/Uplus_j).

All these numbers are rational and the archived score has R>0. No count
tail is removed: every N>=3 receives the fourth score. The exact certificate
gives R approximately 3.178065721383697e-7.

3. Transfer one-count exponential moments before taking products
===============================================================
The archived contraction d>0 satisfies

 E_0,A exp(-w(N)) <= 1-d,
 E_0,B exp( w(N)) <= 1-d.

If both exposure laws have one-count TV distance at most delta from their
snapshot laws, section 2 implies

 E_D,A exp(-w(N)) <= 1-d+R delta,
 E_D,B exp( w(N)) <= 1-d+R delta.

Require delta<=d/(2R). Then both exposure exponential moments are at most
1-d/2. Independence of entire trajectories between gates AND conditional
independent emissions make the exposure counts iid. Therefore for S=sum w,
with the unchanged rule choosing A if S>=0 and B otherwise, Markov gives

 Pr_D,A(S<0) <= (1-d/2)^m <= exp(-m d/2),
 Pr_D,B(S>=0) <= (1-d/2)^m <= exp(-m d/2).

Take m=ceil(6/d), so m d/2>=3. A finite exact positive Taylor sum proves
exp(3)>20, hence both errors are strictly below 1/20. The ties are assigned
to A exactly as in the archived test; the B event includes equality.

This avoids comparing the entire m-count path laws, whose TV bound may
already be uninformative at this exposure ratio. Only the bounded scalar
exponential moments need to be close.

5. Scope retained
=================
The count budget remains extremely large and the sufficient exposure ratio
extremely small. Neither is claimed optimal or necessary. The theorem uses
fresh stationary independent realizations between observations; a single
continuous OU trajectory requires separate dependence control. Keeping total
eta=1/28 while shortening D requires changing physical rate or flux. No
achievable source brightness, reset schedule, detector linearity, or absence
of detector memory has been demonstrated. This conditional bridge neither
validates the physical model nor substitutes for measurements or external
review. The verification is exact arithmetic, not proof-assistant formalization.
