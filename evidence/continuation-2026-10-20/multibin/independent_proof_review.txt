INDEPENDENT REVIEW OF THE MULTI-BIN TEST ARGUMENT

The count partition is {0},...,{cutoff-1},{cutoff or greater}. Every
observation belongs to exactly one bin. The exact rational scores define
an implementable sum comparison, including an unambiguous tie decision.

For bin k, P(N=k)=eta^k E[I^k exp(-eta I)]/k! at zero background.
Positive double-node interpolation of exp(-eta x) has even-order positive
derivative and nonnegative remainder product, so it bounds from below.
Adding a simple zero endpoint produces an odd-order negative derivative
and a nonnegative product on x>=0, giving an upper bound. Multiplication
by x^k preserves both signs. The node-count constraints ensure every
integrated polynomial uses only the available moments through order64.
The final-bin interval follows from exact probability normalization.

For fixed rational scores w and iid observations, choosing A when the
sum is nonnegative gives
 P_A(error) <= E_A[exp(-w)]^m,
 P_B(error) <= E_B[exp(w)]^m.
A common exact bound of 1-c with c>0 and m*c>=3 therefore bounds each
conditional error by (1-c)^m < exp(-3) < 1/20. The proof is finite-sample
and separate for the two structures. No Gaussian approximation to the
score sum, empirical error estimate, or Monte Carlo result is needed.

The independent verifier uses confluent Newton divided differences to
integrate the interpolants, whereas the producer uses cardinal Hermite
bases. It encloses exponentials using a positive Taylor series and a
reciprocal, whereas the producer uses an alternating negative-exp series.
It maximizes each MGF over the full capped probability simplex, rather
than eliminating the final probability and bounding remaining terms
individually. These provide different arithmetic representations of the
probability and MGF steps. The verifier checks interval containment in
the serialized producer bounds and checks all finite-test inequalities.

The inherited independent implementation is pinned by a literal source
hash. Producer source, arithmetic dependency, nodes, moment input, and
certificate hashes are checked or recorded. Exact scripts reject Python
optimization because their internal invariant checks use assertions.

Scope: the independent_verification.json result concerns the selected
test and its conditional error bound. Selection of the least sufficient
count among a finite candidate list is not a global optimality theorem
or a necessary sample bound. The Gaussian phase law, independence across
snapshots, known calibration, zero background, and reconstructed exact
moment table remain assumptions/dependencies. Computational independence
of arithmetic representations does not constitute external human review
or physical validation.
