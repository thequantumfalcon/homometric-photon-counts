CONTINUOUS OU EXPOSURES WITHOUT INDEPENDENT RESETS
2026-10-20 (America/Denver)

New result and scope
====================
This extends the finite-exposure score test to gates on ONE continuously
evolving stationary Gaussian path at each site. Neither the paths nor the
latent intensities are reset between observations. Two exact finite sample
designs are certified: every consecutive gate, and narrow gates separated
by one correlation time. All statements remain conditional mathematical
guarantees; no physical model validation or practical acquisition claim is
made. Original and sealed-audit files are unchanged.

1. Model
========
There are 63 independent stationary centered Gaussian OU processes with
covariance E theta_j(u)theta_j(v)=s exp(-|u-v|/tau), s,tau>0. A continuous
version can be used. Phasors z_G,j have unit modulus and sum zero. Define

 I_G(u)=|sum_j z_G,j exp(i theta_j(u))|^2,
 J_i=(1/D) integral_(iH)^(iH+D) I_G(u)du,
 N_i | all phase paths ~ independent Poisson(b+eta J_i), i=0,...,m-1.

Gate width D>0 and stride H>=D are fixed in advance. Thus gates do not
overlap; consecutive gates touch only at endpoints of measure zero. The
conditional Poisson independence is an explicit detector assumption.
The marginal count law is the same for every gate by stationarity, but
the count sequence is not independent. eta,b are TOTAL per-gate parameters,
held fixed in this mathematical design, not physical rates held fixed as D
changes. The Gaussian family, tau, gain and phase width are known inputs.

2. Whole-gate covariance bound from the OU Markov semigroup
==========================================================
Let Z_i=h(N_i) be any bounded real statistic, and let g_i=E[Z_i|phase paths].
Each g_i is a bounded measurable function of the path inside gate i.
Conditional emission independence gives Cov(Z_0,Z_k)=Cov(g_0,g_k), k>=1.
Write X_t for the standardized 63-dimensional OU phase vector. Its stationary
law is standard Gaussian and its transition over time l has correlation
r=exp(-l/tau). (Projecting away common phase could reduce the dimension;
the following dimension-free bound makes that unnecessary.)

For centered square-integrable f,h under the standard Gaussian law,

 |E[f(X_t)h(X_(t+l))]| <= exp(-l/tau)||f||_2||h||_2.       (C1)

To prove this, expand f,h in normalized multivariate Hermite polynomials.
The OU transition multiplies a coefficient of total degree a by r^a;
constants are absent because functions are centered. Cauchy-Schwarz bounds
the resulting sum by r||f||_2||h||_2. The covariance identity for Hermites
follows directly from the joint Gaussian exponential generating function,
and L2 completeness extends it from polynomials to general f,h. This is the
same Gaussian semigroup principle used for snapshot dependence in the
archive, applied here to conditional expectations of entire gate paths.

Define f(X_D)=E[g_0|X_D] and h(X_(kH))=E[g_k|X_(kH)]. By the OU Markov
property, the future gate is conditionally independent of the past given its
starting state, and the intervening transition links X_D to X_(kH).
Consequently

 Cov(g_0,g_k)=Cov(f(X_D),h(X_(kH))).

This identity also holds when H=D,k=1, where the gap is zero. Conditional
expectation contracts centered L2 norm, and stationarity gives equal
Var(g_i). Applying (C1), then total variance, gives

 |Cov(Z_0,Z_k)| <= exp(-(kH-D)/tau) Var(g_0)
                <= exp(-(kH-D)/tau) Var(Z_0).             (C2)

No false independence between adjacent gates is used. Summing the covariance
expansion of the sample mean gives, with V any marginal variance upper bound,

 Var(m^-1 sum_i Z_i) <= kappa(H,D) V/m,
 kappa(H,D)=1+2 exp(D/tau)/(exp(H/tau)-1).                 (C3)

The finite-m sum includes factors 1-k/m; replacing it by the infinite
positive geometric sum gives (C3). This is a sufficient variance bound,
not an asymptotic independence assertion. Keeping the emission noise term
separate could improve it, but is unnecessary for validity.

3. One-gate exposure bounds and score moments
===========================================
Use s=2 log(2), eta=1/28, b=0, and the archived fixed rational score w for
bins N=0,1,2,N>=3. The existing finite-gate coupling, which does not require
resets for a single observation, proves

 TV(P_D,G,P_0,G) <= eta C sqrt(D/tau),
 C^2 < Cbar^2=74088/5.                                   (C4)

For completeness, E|F(u)-F(0)|^2 equals
2*63[1-exp(-s(1-exp(-u/tau)))], because centered site increments have
independent cross terms. The clean null gives E|F|^2=63(1-exp(-s)).
Cauchy-Schwarz and integration over a gate imply
E|J_D-I(0)| <= (4sqrt(2)/3)*63*sqrt(s(1-exp(-s)))*sqrt(D/tau).
The additive Poisson coupling bounds TV by eta times this expectation.
At this s, exp(-s)=1/4 and s<7/5, giving Cbar^2 as above.

Choose the following exact fixed gate width and allowance:

 q=D/tau=10^-18,
 delta=1/200000000=5*10^-9.

The verifier checks eta^2 Cbar^2 q < delta^2, so both one-gate TV distances
are strictly below delta. No product-law TV comparison is made.

For any bounded function v, changes of expectation are bounded by
osc(v)*TV, where osc(v)=max(v)-min(v). This follows by subtracting the
minimum and applying the layer-cake formula to a [0,1]-valued function.
Use v=w and v=w^2. The archived probability intervals and exact normalization
give rational enclosures [a_G,b_G] of E_0,G w and an upper bound t_G on
E_0,G w^2. Therefore

 E_D,G w belongs to M_G=[a_G-osc(w)delta,b_G+osc(w)delta],
 E_D,G w^2 <= t_G+osc(w^2)delta,
 Var_D,G(w) <= V_G := t_G+osc(w^2)delta-min_(x in M_G) x^2. (C5)

All four count bins remain in the statistic. There is no cutoff tail.
The expectation enclosures use exact probability normalization:
sum_j p_j v_j=v_3+sum_(j<3) p_j(v_j-v_3), selecting the correct interval
endpoint for each signed coefficient. This retains cancellations crucial
to the tiny mean differences. The verifier proves the exposed intervals
remain separated, M_A.lower>M_B.upper.

Define the exact fixed decision threshold and drift

 theta=(M_A.lower+M_B.upper)/2,
 d0=(M_A.lower-M_B.upper)/2>0.

Choose A iff sum_i w(N_i)>=m theta. Under A the mean exceeds theta by
at least d0; under B it is below theta by at least d0.

4. Finite conditional errors without independent gates
======================================================
Cantelli's inequality and (C3) give under each geometry

 error_G <= kappa V_G/(kappa V_G+m d0^2).                 (C6)

Thus any integer m>19 kappa V_G/d0^2 for both G has error strictly below
1/20 under each structure. The tie convention is included: under B the
upper-tail event includes equality, and Cantelli applies to that closed
event; under A the open lower-tail event is contained in its closed version.
The test and sample size are fixed before observation. No CLT, independent
products, optional stopping, or unbudgeted selection is used.

5. Two explicit acquisition designs on a continuous path
========================================================
Consecutive gates, H=D:
 kappa=1+2/(1-exp(-q)) <= 3+2/q.
Indeed exp(q)>=1+q implies exp(-q)<=1/(1+q), and inversion gives
1/(1-exp(-q))<=1+1/q. Use the exact rational upper bound 3+2/q.
The elapsed span from the first gate start to the last gate end is mD,
or mq in units tau. All gates during this span are used.

One gate per correlation time, H=tau:
 kappa=1+2exp(q)/(exp(1)-1) < 1+6/[5(1-q)].
For 0<q<1, exp(q)<=1/(1-q) by positive power series; exp(1)>8/3 by a
finite positive partial sum. The elapsed span in units tau is m-1+q.
The OU paths continue to evolve during unrecorded gaps; no reset is assumed.

For each design the verifier chooses
 m=max_G (floor(19*kappa_upper*V_G/d0^2)+1)
and directly checks both exact rational error bounds are below 1/20.
The exact counts, moment bounds, threshold, and time spans are in
continuous_exposure_verification.json. The very large counts and spans
are sufficient guarantees, not practical recommendations or lower bounds.

6. Dependencies and unresolved questions
=======================================
The original detector-gain certificate supplies exact count-probability
enclosures and rational score weights. Its SHA256 is checked against the
release manifest. This continuation does not reconstruct those moments or
reprove the entire original certificate; those are existing independently
checked dependencies. It rechecks the new moment, width, covariance-bound
constants, and final sample inequalities by exact rational arithmetic.

The new substantive conclusion is that independent phase resets are not
necessary for these finite-exposure guarantees under known stationary OU
motion and conditional independent emissions. Physical adequacy, achievable
gain at tiny gates, detector dead time, arbitrary motion, unknown tau,
nonstationary initialization, adaptive stopping, and global optimality
remain separate. This is a mathematical derivation plus arithmetic verifier,
not a proof-assistant formalization or external peer review.

The global blind-gain root-count route was also considered. The existing
non-effective density-sign argument does not supply a computable near-zero
density expansion or signed remainder uniform enough for tail exclusion.
No exact global root count or effective cutoff is claimed by this work.
