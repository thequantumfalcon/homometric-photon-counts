# Exact real-coordinate homometry and noncongruence

The symbolic coordinate records define two noncongruent sets of 63 distinct real positions. Their complete multisets of directed pair differences agree. The new result connects this geometric statement to the already proved photon-count separation under independent Gaussian site phases and conditional Poisson counts.

[PhotonHomometry.lean](PhotonHomometry.lean) defines `differences xs` as all $x-y$ for indexed entries of `xs`, preserving multiplicities and diagonal zeros. It proves a general result for lists in any commutative additive group: if two translation lists have the same difference multiset, translating a common base list by either one preserves that equality. The nine differences of `[0,31,63]` and `[0,32,63]` agree by exact kernel reduction. This is a formalization of the construction, not a novelty claim about this general method.

[PhotonCoordinateHomometry.lean](PhotonCoordinateHomometry.lean) proves that translating the symbolic integer base translates the real position by that integer. It binds the parsed coordinate lists to the 21-site base and these two translation lists. Consequently, all **3,969 directed differences** agree with multiplicities. Mapping any function over the differences preserves the permutation; absolute pair distances are one instance. These are exact real identities, independent of rounded coordinate displays.

[PhotonCoordinateOrder.lean](PhotonCoordinateOrder.lean) proves that each signed displacement from its integer base is strictly smaller than $1/2$. The kernel checks increasing integer bases and valid signs in the saved lists; a general order argument then proves that both real-coordinate lists are strictly increasing, have no duplicates, and contain exactly 63 sites.

[PhotonCoordinateNoncongruence.lean](PhotonCoordinateNoncongruence.lean) excludes every translation $x\mapsto x+c$ and every reflection $x\mapsto c-x$ between the two lists, even allowing arbitrary reordering. Strictly ordered permuted lists must agree entry by entry. Their common first coordinate fixes a possible translation; zero-based index 21 then gives 31 versus 32. Their common endpoints 0 and 93 fix a possible reflection; the second/penultimate coordinates differ by one after reflection. No numerical arcsine approximation is needed for these contradictions.

[PhotonSpatialSeparation.lean](PhotonSpatialSeparation.lean) combines these results. Inspect `GeometricPair` and `CountGap` alongside `decoded_spatial_separation`: the exact coordinate literals parse to lists of positions satisfying the geometric conclusions, the concrete fourth factorial-count moment gap, and unequal complete count laws. The detector-model assumptions remain positive Gaussian phase variance and gain, and a common nonnegative background. The coordinate phasor convention is $\exp(i\pi x/3)$.

## Validation and scope

All five new modules passed ordinary Lean kernel compilation with 26 standard-axiom audits. The generic `PhotonHomometry` module and its mathlib environment also passed an additional fresh-kernel check. The [source-bound receipt](../docs/validation/homometry_formal_replay.json), [validation record](../docs/validation/README.md) and [source manifest](manifest.json) identify the exact coverage. Previous modules and the scientific evidence are preserved byte-for-byte. The existing [external byte checks](../docs/validation/coordinate_byte_binding.json) still bind the coordinate literals to the unchanged archive.

The real-coordinate ordering, distinctness, directed-difference homometry and exclusion of translations/reflections are now formalized for this exact one-dimensional construction. This does not formalize an instrument's diffraction response, graph thresholds or correlated-motion covariance, decimal rounding, temporal/exposure models, the root/testing certificates, experimental validity or historical priority. The previously recorded optional fresh reindexing timeout remains a timeout; these new proofs do not turn it into a completed replay. No new Nanoda export is claimed.
