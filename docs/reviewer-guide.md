# A route through the evidence

The [recovered original report](report/historical/science_completion_report.pdf) provides an 18-page account of the foundational construction and derivations. Read it with the [23 October addendum](report/update-2026-10-23.md) for the later root-count, testing and concrete formal results. The [source and recovery guide](report/README.md) explains the historical scope; the current claim ledger links each result to its evidence.

1. Read the [model](model.md), [claim ledger](claims.md), and [research disclosure](research-disclosure.md).
2. Run `python tools/project.py verify` and `python tools/project.py test --output build/reviewer-check`.
3. For the reference root count, inspect the compact inverse-gain proof and tail cover linked in the ledger. Check how the bounded-gain and large-gain arguments join at $1/6$.
4. For the new local theorem, check Fourier normalization, the uniform small/large-gain bounds, and the compactness/implicit-function argument. The rational checker alone does not prove those analytic implications.
5. For the necessary count, check the affinity bound, product law, total-variation testing inequality, strict error criterion, and integer rounding. The independently coded Taylor and Arb checks address different numerical routes.
6. Inspect each Lean theorem's hypotheses. The [mixture construction](../formal/SCOPE.md) derives the archived factorial-tower premises. The [geometry extension](../formal/GEOMETRY.md) proves finite phase algebra and parameter substitution. The [Gaussian extension](../formal/GAUSSIAN.md) connects an explicit product probability law to the real intensity moments and constructs the Gaussian/Poisson marginal law. Follow the [exact certificates](../formal/CERTIFICATES.md) to `PhotonConcreteSeparation.count_laws_distinct`: the moment identities are proved there. Continue to the [joint snapshot proof](../formal/JOINT_MODEL.md), which constructs one probability space and proves the conditional Poisson interpretation. Follow the [symbolic-coordinate proof](../formal/COORDINATES.md) through ordered phase equality, Gaussian reindexing and the final coordinate count-law theorem. Check the external byte binding separately, and distinguish this mathematical joint law from an experimentally validated detector.

The general moment formula sums over pairs of site assignments. Its proof applies to any finite geometry, but direct evaluation has $n^{2k}$ terms at order $k$. The later certificate modules check every required recurrence identity directly in Lean, then bind the vectors to the original formal geometry. They do not verify the archived Python evaluator as a general-purpose program. Compare source coverage with the executed replay coverage in the [validation record](validation/README.md).

Criticism should identify a specific implication, assumption, certificate entry, or reproduction failure. A successful file hash proves identity, and a successful formal check proves its exact statement; neither validates a physical detector.

For the later prototype, start with the [optical-study guide](optical-studies.md), then compare the [ideal feasibility certificate](../studies/optical-feasibility/README.md) with the [optical/detector supplements](../studies/optical-validation/README.md). Review the failed wider calibration budget, finite selection of the tighter settings, 48 separate simulation error comparisons, and external-data wavelength/processing limitations. Run `python tools/optical_validation.py audit`. The finite Arb transfer checks bound numerical evaluation of a declared optical model; they do not turn the selected simulations into a continuous-tolerance guarantee or an experiment.
