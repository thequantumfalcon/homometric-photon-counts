## The model carried forward

The original experiment uses the two fixed, noncongruent 63-site homometric
sets at spatial angular frequency $q=\pi/3$. For the corresponding unit phasors
$z_{G,j}$, independent standard Gaussian variables $Z_j$, phase variance
$s>0$, gain $\eta>0$, and a common background $b\geq0$, its snapshot law is

$$
t=e^{-s/2},\qquad
I_G=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{63}z_{G,j}e^{i\sqrt{s}Z_j}\right|^2,
\qquad N\mid I_G\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(b+\eta I_G).
$$

Here $G$ is either structure $A$ or $B$, and $0\leq I_G\leq3969$. Independent
site phases within one snapshot and independent snapshots across an experiment
are separate assumptions. The testing result below requires both. The
[model guide](../model.md) separates this law from the report's earlier motion
and exposure controls.

## Exactly four positive blind gains

A **blind gain** is a positive $\eta$ at which the event $N=4$ has the same
probability under $A$ and $B$. This concerns one count event; the complete
count distributions still differ. Define

$$\Delta p_4(\eta)=\Pr_A(N=4)-\Pr_B(N=4).$$

At **$t=1/2$ and $b=0$**, $\Delta p_4$ has exactly **four simple positive roots**.
Their approximate locations are $0.00165929160405$, $0.010900414$,
$0.04465495517295$, and $0.293484$. These decimal locations are illustrative;
the [certified root summary](../../evidence/scientist-review-2026-10-20/global/certified_roots.json)
gives the authoritative strict rational brackets. In particular,
$0.2934841<\eta_4<0.2934842$.

The proof covers all positive gains, however large. An order-96 moment
certificate establishes exactly three simple roots on $(0,1/6]$. The
[inverse-gain argument](../../evidence/scientist-review-2026-10-20/global/compact_gain_proof.txt)
then maps the remaining half-line to a compact interval. Fourier tail bounds,
endpoint signs and a strictly signed derivative establish exactly one
remaining root. The [global certificate](../../evidence/scientist-review-2026-10-20/global/compact_gain_certificate.json)
and [separate implementation](../../evidence/scientist-review-2026-10-20/review/independent_compact_gain.py)
support that argument; an illustrative numerical scan alone would not do so.

The four-root conclusion is specific to the stated parameter point. A later
[perturbation argument](../root-stability.md) proves exactly four simple roots
throughout $|t-1/2|\leq10^{-23}$ and $0\leq b\leq10^{-20}$. Those conservative
radii do not establish useful physical tolerances. Both results combine
analytic arguments and arithmetic certificates; neither is a Lean root-count
theorem. The report's earlier mean-intensity crossings concern different
observables under different motion assumptions.

## Necessary and sufficient sample counts for one fixed experiment

Fix **$s=2\log2$, $\eta=1/20$, $b=0$**, with these parameters known. Observe
independent, identically distributed snapshots only through the 13 bins
$\{0\},\ldots,\{11\},\{12,13,\ldots\}$. Let each error mean the probability of
selecting the wrong structure conditional on that structure being true.

| Requirement: each error strictly below $1/20$ | Certified number of snapshots |
| --- | ---: |
| Necessary for any test, including randomized tests | At least 28,091,647,285,174 |
| Sufficient for the retained fixed-score test | 102,399,876,723,169 |

Thus the unknown optimal count for this experiment lies between those
integers, with endpoint ratio below 3.646. The lower endpoint is a conservative
necessary bound, not a claim that it is attainable.

The [lower-bound proof](../../evidence/followup-2026-10-20/lowerbounds/proof.txt)
uses the certified bin-probability intervals to bound the single-snapshot
Hellinger affinity. Affinity multiplies under iid sampling, giving an upper
bound on total variation and hence a lower bound on the sum of the two errors.
Exact rational bounds on the resulting logarithmic expression produce the
stated integer. A [separate exact check](../../evidence/followup-2026-10-20/independent_lowerbounds/review.txt)
supports the exclusion.

The sufficient result uses fixed rational bin scores and a specified tie
decision. Exponential-moment bounds control each error separately, without
a normal approximation or simulated error estimate. The
[certificate](../../evidence/continuation-2026-10-20/multibin/certificate.json)
and [argument review](../../evidence/continuation-2026-10-20/multibin/independent_proof_review.txt)
give the probability enclosures and finite-sample inequalities.

These are bounds for a fixed binned observation with known calibration and iid
snapshots. They do not lower-bound tests using the individual counts above 11,
other gains, other phase variances, other frequencies, or adaptive measurements.
Pooling the tail discards information. Unknown or drifting calibration and
temporal dependence need additional analysis. Tens of trillions of independent
snapshots do not establish a practical acquisition plan. The bounds are
analytic/computational results, not Lean theorems or measurements.

## The completed concrete formal proof

The maintained Lean development now proves the moment identities for the
explicit phase lists, rather than assuming them. With
$D_k(t)=\mathbb E[I_A^k]-\mathbb E[I_B^k]$, it establishes
$D_1=D_2=D_3=0$ and

$$
D_4(t)=\frac{455015761}{20000}t^8(1-t^2)^5(t^6+3t^4+6t^2+6)>0
\qquad(0<t<1).
$$

For the constructed Gaussian/Poisson law, the difference in fourth
falling-factorial count moments is $\eta^4D_4(t)$. Common background cancels
because the lower intensity moments match. Consequently, the two marginal
count laws differ for every positive variance and gain and every common
nonnegative background, including at the blind gains above.

The [concrete certificate account](../../formal/CERTIFICATES.md) explains how
kernel-checked polynomial identities evaluate the proved Gaussian moment
recurrence. `PhotonConcreteSeparation.count_laws_distinct` discharges the
concrete moment premises. The [coordinate bridge](../../formal/COORDINATES.md)
derives phasors from exact symbolic positions and transports the count law
under reordering. The [spatial theorem](../../formal/HOMOMETRY.md) proves that
each position list has 63 distinct sites, that all 3,969 directed differences
agree with multiplicity, and that no translation or reflection identifies
the lists. Its combined theorem connects that geometry to count separation.
The [joint snapshot construction](../../formal/JOINT_MODEL.md) supplies one
probability space with the stated conditional Poisson law and count marginal.

A [22 October source rebuild](../validation/formal_replay_20261022.json)
compiled all 61 modules with Lean 4.35.0 and passed 722 theorem-axiom audits.
This was ordinary kernel compilation. Earlier fresh-kernel and selected

The completed claim is the concrete geometry-to-Gaussian/Poisson count-law

External bindings between saved files and checked coordinate literals also
