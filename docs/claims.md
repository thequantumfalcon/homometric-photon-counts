# Claim and evidence ledger

Status is attached to a statement and its assumptions, not to an entire repository.

The main result is a distinction between two specified **population** count laws at common fixed parameters. Matching their first three intensity moments does not imply equal count probabilities for $N=0,1,2,3$, and a blind gain concerns only the event $N=4$. Higher count **factorial** cumulants inherit intensity cumulants under the conditional Poisson model; ordinary count cumulants do not obey the same background-cancellation rule. The [model guide](model.md#moments-cumulants-and-count-probabilities) states these identities and the [separate bounded-count model](model.md#relation-to-bounded-count-identification). These distinctions do not add an unknown-calibration reconstruction theorem or a finite-record confidence guarantee to the claims below.

The [optical transfer/detector supplement](../studies/optical-validation/README.md) adds finite-setting robustness and afterpulse simulations, external processed detector tables, and a rigorous arithmetic check. Its 48 afterpulse comparisons each had 0/200 simulation errors (simultaneous 95% upper bound 0.03375192026711007). Its Arb certificate covers selected transfers and finite phase/rate witnesses. These results do not extend the earlier continuous-box risk guarantee to real optical errors or afterpulsing; measured calibration and physical validation remain open. The failed broader optical budget is retained.

The separate [optical feasibility extension](../studies/optical-feasibility/README.md) adds conditional computational results without changing the original claims below. At a changed spatial frequency, unbinned counts expose a second-order signal. One fixed score and threshold have each error below 4.53% with 5,000 iid snapshots uniformly over the specified ±2% gain/phase-variance and background box; 11,000 snapshots cover the wider ±4% box below 4.90%. Ideal 256-level phase quantization and initially-live dead time up to 22 ns per 5 ms gate are included. Unknown parameters must remain fixed inside the box. Afterpulsing, real optical transfer and temporal dependence are excluded. The [derivation](../studies/optical-feasibility/simulation-prototype/calibration-study/METHOD.txt) and [certificate](../studies/optical-feasibility/simulation-prototype/calibration-study/run-checked/calibration.json) are not Lean proofs, physical validation or novelty evidence. The original reference experiment still fails the specified 288,000-snapshot budget.

The reference global root proof combines the bounded-gain certificate through $1/6$ with an inverse-gain cover of the entire remaining half-line. The fourth positive root is strictly between 0.2934841 and 0.2934842. A numerical scan or illustration alone would not establish this result.

The new necessary/sufficient interval is [28,091,647,285,174, 102,399,876,723,169], for error strictly below 1/20 under each structure. The lower endpoint is conservative; it is not asserted to be the exact smallest integer compatible with every intermediate inequality.

Internal checks share some inputs and libraries. The two Fourier implementations use Arb; fresh Lean checks use the same Lean kernel. A separate [Nanoda check](validation/nanoda_concrete.json) accepted the exported final count-law theorem and its dependency closure, adding a different kernel implementation. No external human peer review, experimental validation, complete proof-assistant formalization, or historical novelty is claimed.
